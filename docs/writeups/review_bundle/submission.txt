      Steered affect moves a conversational partner’s activations:
     the estimator, three pre-registered ablations, and the controls
                                 register
                                                Manan Wadhwa∗


                                                     Abstract
          When one language-model agent is steered into an emotion and talks to another, a linear
      probe’s projection of the second agent’s reply activations moves with the dose, for five of six
      emotions on Qwen3.6-27B and on Llama-3-8B-Instruct-abliterated. We ask what carries it, and
      find that the standard linear tools mislead in ways their usual checks do not catch. The logistic-
      regression direction that probe studies use to read such states reproduces across disjoint halves
      at only 0.57 (27B, n = 2000 per half) and 0.29 (8B), while difference-of-means reaches 0.97 and
      0.94 on the same splits, and a fixed per-sample penalty does not close the gap. In the n < d
      regime probe studies work in, every logistic fit is a perfect separator: its direction is the penalty’s
      tie-break, drifting from the mean difference as n grows (cosine 0.97 to 0.63); projected onto 100
      principal components, where n ≫ d′ , the fits stop separating and the direction reproduces at
      0.90; the two estimators’ held-out projections still correlate at 0.90–0.96. With the reproducible
      direction, three pre-registered ablations test the linear account of the channel. Removing the
      emotion direction at every readable layer leaves a median 91% of the response against baseline
      (83% with the untestable emotion included) and 91% against a random-direction control (85%);
      a rank-5 affect subspace fails its own instrument check; a rank-5 subspace fitted by the same
      estimator on permuted labels blocks three emotions at BH-FDR q < 0.05 where the affect
      subspace blocks two (at half to two-thirds of its size on those two, and more than it on afraid),
      while a random 5-frame of half its footprint, a random 23-frame matched to its footprint and
      a permuted-label rank-1 direction are inert on every testable emotion: the control that bites is
      the one built from the pool’s activations, not the one that removes the most (whether its labels
      matter is untested). Four further label permutations of that control block angry in all four
      and sad in three (unadjusted intervals), but all three emotions together in only one: the three-
      emotion pattern is one permutation’s, the bite on angry and sad is not. Re-estimating every
      direction on ten re-splits of the probe pool, sad’s block against the permuted control holds in all
      ten draws (median 35%, range 24–56%); afraid’s is smaller (18%, 7–23%) and its manipulation
      check passes in only four, so the affect-specific claim rests on sad, the one emotion that also
      replicates across models. The receiver’s “other-speaker” projection also rises with dose, so the
      probe does not separate the receiver’s state from its model of the sender’s.

negotiation [16]. If one agent is in an emotional state, does anything of that state reach another’s
internal representation? A transfer that changes the receiver’s internal representation need not show
in transcripts, which we do not examine here, and is visible in activations, which makes it a question
for representation-level tools: steering vectors to induce a state in the sender [14, 15, 18, 22, 24, 25],
probes to read it in the receiver, and ablations to test what carries it.
     Those tools rest on one object, a direction in activation space, which has to be trusted twice:
once to read and once to intervene. Our claim is that both uses fail in ways the usual checks miss.
Read with a logistic-regression coeﬀicient row, the direction does not reproduce across independent
fits. The reason is separability, which no sample size within reach (n ≤ 2000 against d = 5120)
and no penalty schedule removes: the fitted row is a tie-break among separating hyperplanes, not
an estimate [17, 21, 23]. Intervene with the reproducible direction, and the standard control, a
norm-matched random vector, stays inert, while a rank-5 subspace fitted by the same estimator on
destroyed labels blocks the response at the same false-discovery threshold as the affect subspace
does.
     The evidence is an estimator study across two models and three depths, and three pre-registered
experiments on the channel, each critiqued blind before its wording entered this paper. The response
itself survives resampling: five of six emotions respond on each model. The channel is not what the
linear picture suggests. Removing the emotion direction everywhere it can be read leaves most of
the response; only sad loses at least 24% against the permuted-label control (23% against baseline)
in every one of ten direction draws, and afraid 18% at the median with its manipulation check
passing in four draws of ten. A rank-5 affect subspace cannot serve as an instrument because
it shifts the readout used to check it. A rank-1 permuted-label direction is inert while a rank-5
permuted-label frame is not.
     The practical consequence is a change to the controls register of any ablation study of a concept
direction: beside the random direction, run the same estimator on permuted labels at the rank of
the intervention, report every arm’s footprint, and check the manipulation with an instrument that
does not share the ablation’s target. The scientific consequence is narrower than we hoped: the
receiver’s response exists, the rank-1 direction carries part of it for sad on both models and across
direction draws, less reliably for afraid, and the rest is open.

Contributions.

   • Dose-responsive movement of the receiver’s present-emotion projection for five of six emotions
     on two models, with common-random-number sampling and scenario-blocked inference; the
     other-speaker projection moves comparably, which bounds the construct claim (Section 4).
   • Disjoint-half reproducibility of probe directions as the missing quantity: logistic 0.57 / 0.29
     against difference-of-means 0.97 / 0.94, unchanged by a fixed per-sample penalty, with the
     mechanism (perfect separation at every n; the direction is the penalty’s tie-break and drifts
     from the mean difference) measured directly, and its downstream cost bounded by held-out
     accuracy and projection correlation (Section 3).
   • Three pre-registered ablations with their verdicts as registered: rank-1 insuﬀiciency (H1),
     rank-5 instrument failure, and survival of the rank-1 block against a permuted-label rank-1
     control (H3), with two of the designs rerun on the second model, where only sad’s result
     carries over (Section 5).
   • Every arm against baseline in one table with one significance standard, including the two

Relation to prior work. That logistic probe directions can be unstable is not our discovery,
nor is rank-1 insuﬀiciency under ablation [2, 9, 20]; the permuted-label control is the intervention
analogue of the control task [13]. Marks and Tegmark [17] identified the deficiency and proposed
mass-mean probing, noting that logistic regression converges toward the maximum-margin sepa-
rator on separable data, the implicit bias analysed by Soudry et al. [23]; Gao et al. [10] name
directional stability as a requirement for probe-then-steer pipelines and propose ridge-tuned logis-
tic regression, reporting mean pairwise robustness of 0.87–0.98 under 20% training-data ablations.
Our contribution is the quantity: reproducibility across disjoint halves, which share no examples
where two 80% subsamples share most, plateaus at 0.55–0.61 on the 27B and falls to 0.28–0.38
on the 8B while difference-of-means rises to 0.94–0.98 on the same splits. Braun et al. [4] cor-
roborate for mean-difference steering vectors, which vary widely from 5–30 training samples and
agree above 0.99 from 200–500, the estimator we also find reproducible. Herbster et al. [12] re-
port mean-difference and logistic directions near-identical for compassion (0.98–0.99, on the same
Qwen3.6-27B) but 0.80 for one honesty case; at their training-set sizes our cosine is also above 0.9
(Figure 2), and at n = 2000 we find 0.43 and 0.33–0.37, so the results are consistent with drift in
n. Raju [19] measure stability across feature dimensions, a different axis.


2    Setup
Two agents, A and B, converse in 29 fixed scenarios. A’s message is generated under a steering
vector added at layer 42 of Qwen3.6-27B (revision 6a9e13bd) at doses 0, 0.33, 0.67 and 1.0 of a fixed
scale (the activation RMS at the focus layer), with three replicate seeds; B’s reply is then sampled
with common random numbers across experimental arms, so that sampling noise shared between
two arms cancels inside each paired contrast. Two readouts: B’s present-emotion projection, the
projection of B’s reply activations on a difference-of-means probe for B’s own emotion, and B’s
other-speaker projection on the matching probe for the interlocutor’s emotion; both probes are
fitted on one half (808 items) of a 1,615-item probe pool while the steering and ablation directions
are fitted, per layer, on the disjoint other half (807 items). A generation-side forced-choice readout
of A’s span is the manipulation check. Every dose-response slope is an ordinary least-squares
slope over the four doses on per-scenario means; every confidence interval is a scenario-blocked
percentile bootstrap [8] (29 clusters, 5,000 draws), every between-arm contrast is scenario-blocked
and dose-paired, and false-discovery rates are Benjamini–Hochberg [3] within one family of five
testable emotions unless stated.

Two pools. The estimator study (Section 3) uses a separate, larger generation pool built for
it with the same generator as the probe pool (six present emotions × six interlocutor emotions,
160 dialogues per cell on the 27B (220 on the 8B) instead of 60, degenerate and refusing outputs
dropped): 4,253 items on the 27B (kept from 5,760 generated) and 3,898 on the 8B, with no
items in common with the 1,615-item probe pool, so that disjoint halves of up to 2,000 items exist
on the 27B; on the 8B two halves of 1,949 would fit but the next point of the pre-declared grid
(2,000) does not, so its curves stop at 1,200. The second model is Llama-3-8B-Instruct-abliterated
(revision dd67dd05); its runs are out-of-pre-registration replications with a weaker, less specific

3    The estimator: reproducibility and its mechanism
Disjoint-half reproducibility. We fit the same estimator on two disjoint halves of n items each,
ten independent splits, and report the mean per-class cosine between the two fitted directions
(Figure 1). At the 27B focus layer with n = 2000 per half, difference-of-means reaches 0.974 and
logistic regression (C = 0.5) 0.566 (a2.focus_dom_split_half, a2.focus_logreg_split_half); at layer 54,
0.979 against 0.554 (a2f54.*); on the 8B focus layer at n = 1200, 0.941 against 0.286 (a2_8b.*).
Tuning C per fit by cross-validation reaches 0.61 on the 27B (a2f.focus_tuned_c_does_not_close_gap)
and 0.38 at the 8B focus layer (0.37–0.41 across three depths; a2f8.lam_declines, note).

Holding the per-sample penalty fixed does not help. A natural objection is that a fixed
C changes the effective penalty as n grows. With Cn = 0.5 Nref /n, so that the per-sample penalty
is constant, the logistic curve shows no net rise from n = 150 to 2000 at two anchors on the
27B focus layer (0.581 at both ends for the 600 anchor, with a significant dip of −0.021 at 600;
a2f.focus_logreg_fixed_lambda_flat, a2f2.lam_anchor2000_no_rise), falls once and then stays flat at
layer 54 (a2f54.lam_declines_anchor600), rises at layer 16, and on the 8B peaks at 150 and falls
to 0.276 at 1200 (a2f8.lam_declines). The picture is depth-specific, with no convergence over the
reachable range (n ≤ 2000 against d = 5120 on the 27B; n ≤ 1200 against d = 4096 on the 8B): a
proportional-regime statement, not an asymptotic one.

Why. Every multinomial logistic fit on every half at every n used, under the fixed schedule and
C = 0.5, 50 and 500 alike (the two weak arms being one tolerance-limited solution), separates
its training half perfectly: accuracy exactly 1.000 in every cell, arm and seed; worst single-fit log-
loss 0.0056; at most 81 iterations (a2m.separable_every_n, a2m.8b_same_regime, a2m.v2_ten_seeds). The
likelihood is saturated from n = 75: the direction is the penalty’s tie-break among separating
hyperplanes. Weakening the penalty a hundredfold or a thousandfold (both fits tolerance-limited,
Appendix B) leaves the direction within cosine 0.96 of the C = 0.5 fit on the same half and no
more reproducible (0.52 against 0.58 at n = 2000; a2m.weak_penalty_same_direction). The separator

claimed: that the fit sits at the max-margin limit (the weak arms are tolerance-limited), or a
support-vector mechanism for the drift.

Is this just n < d, and does it cost anything? Separation is guaranteed for generic data
whenever n < d [5, 6], so the mechanism is regime-conditional. Three checks bound what that
means. On held-out items at the 27B focus layer the logistic probe decodes better than difference-
of-means (six-way accuracy 0.82 to 0.94 against 0.79 to 0.88 at n = 75 to 2000), and the two
estimators’ projections of the same held-out items correlate at 0.96 to 0.90 over the same range
(a2l.heldout_and_projcorr): the coeﬀicient row is a fine classifier and a poor direction, the dissoci-
ation Marks and Tegmark [17] described, and the instability is largely geometric. Projecting the
activations onto their top 100 principal components and refitting shows the regime dependence
directly: with n/d′ up to 20 the fits stop separating their halves from n = 600 (training accuracy
0.999, 0.989, 0.974 at n = 600, 1200, 2000), the logistic direction becomes reproducible (0.90 at
n = 2000 in standardised coordinates against 0.94 for difference-of-means, from 0.39 and 0.33 at
n = 75), and its cosine to the difference-of-means direction stays at 0.91–0.94 across n instead of
falling from 0.90 to 0.62 as it does at full width (these are standardised-coordinate cosines; the
0.97→0.63 above is the raw-space convention of Figure 2); held-out accuracy in 100 dimensions is
0.92 against 0.94 at full width (a2l.lowd_recovers). The 8B behaves the same way: in 100 dimensions
training accuracy falls to 0.90 at n = 1200 and the logistic direction reproduces at 0.81 against 0.85
for difference-of-means (a2l.8b_lowd). The n < d regime is therefore necessary for the instability,
though not the whole of it (the 8B, with smaller d, reproduces worse), and the recommendation
holds because probe pools in this literature are small relative to model width.

labels gives a rank-1 direction with cosine 0.004 to the emotion direction (Appendix B) and 0.85×
its footprint on average (0.84 afraid, 0.77 sad); removed at all 51 layers it changes no emotion’s dose-

direction blocks afraid by 25% [17%, 33%] and sad by 29% [7%, 56%] (BH-FDR q 0.001 and 0.028;
b1e.verdict_H3); the corresponding blocks against baseline are 24% and 28%. Across ten re-splits of
the probe pool that re-estimate every direction and the readout probe (Table 4; the paper’s draw is
above afraid’s range and inside sad’s), afraid’s block is 18% at the median (7–23%) and significant in
nine draws, but its manipulation check (the A-span readout’s response to A’s own steering) passes
in only four (verdict H3 in four draws, instrument_failed in six). Sad’s block is 35% (24–56%),
significant in all ten, with the check passing in all ten. The permuted direction stays within 7%
(afraid) and 13% (sad) of baseline throughout (b1e10.sad_stable, b1e10.afraid_gate_fragile). The top
principal direction of A’s span, label-free, with 5.8× the footprint, blocks angry by 61% (q = 0.001)

footprint alone (b1d23.inert), and rank and metric remain confounded. The affect subspace and
the permuted-label frame leave B’s replies within 1.2 words and 0.13 perplexity units of baseline;
the top principal direction lengthens replies by two words and lowers perplexity by 3.7 (Table 6).
The arms that block are all estimated from the pool’s activations; the inert ones are not.

6   What the controls teach
Table 2 puts every arm on one scale (the CI-implied range is the contrast’s interval divided by
the none-arm slope). The random direction, the random 5-frame and the permuted-label rank-1
direction are inert; the permuted-label rank-5 frame blocks three emotions at the false-discovery
threshold, at half to two-thirds of the affect subspace’s size on the two it also blocks; the top
principal direction blocks angry, one of those three, and neither of the rank-1 direction’s pair. Over
five label permutations the permuted control blocks angry in five, sad in four, afraid in two and
all three together in two (Table 7): the three-emotion pattern is one permutation’s, the bite on
angry and sad recurs. A random frame scaled to the permuted frame’s footprint (rank 23) is
inert on the five testable emotions (b1d23.inert; it moves calm, the untestable one), so matching a
control’s footprint is not enough: the arms that bite are the ones built from the pool’s activations.
Whether label-fitting adds anything beyond covariance shaping is untested (a random frame inside
the pool’s top principal subspace would tell). The rule is procedural: beside the random direction,
run the same estimator on permuted labels at the intervention’s rank, report every arm’s footprint
and dose-0 readout shift, and check the manipulation with an instrument that does not share the
ablation’s target.


7   Limitations and what is not claimed
The present-emotion projection does not separate B’s own state from B’s representation of A’s; the
other-speaker projection moves comparably. One probe pool underlies all 27B ablations; direction
sampling variability is measured by ten re-splits for B1e’s two emotions only (Table 4), not for B1c
or B1d, and afraid’s manipulation check is split-sensitive. All intervals are percentile bootstraps
over 29 scenario clusters; blocked fractions divide by the none-arm slope’s point estimate, so their
ranges understate uncertainty where that slope is imprecise (happy, calm); the random frames
and the rank-1 permuted direction are one realisation each; the rank-5 permuted frame has five,
which agree on angry and largely on sad but not on afraid (Table 7); multiplicity is controlled
within experiments, not across the three experiments and two models. The estimator mechanism
is regime-conditional (n < d). Not tested: the token channel (three rewrite designs failed their own
manipulation check; b1c.text_arms_uninterpretable), the behavioural reach, layers below 13, and an
ablation rerun with the logistic direction.


8   Conclusion
The two failures we found are the same failure seen twice. A logistic direction on separable ac-
tivations is decided by the penalty’s tie-break, so two independent fits agree only as far as their
tie-breaks do; and a control direction is informative only if it is decided the way the intervention
direction was. A random vector is not decided by the data at all, so it is inert and misses what a
control built from the same activations catches (angry in all five label permutations, sad in four).
Such a study should report disjoint-half reproducibility, estimate with an average rather than an
extremum, and run its ablations against a control fitted by the same estimator on permuted labels.
For affect: the rank-1 direction carries 24–56% 9of sad’s response across the ten 27B draws, and
only sad’s block carries over to the 8B; afraid’s is less reliable; whose state the probe reads remains
open.
