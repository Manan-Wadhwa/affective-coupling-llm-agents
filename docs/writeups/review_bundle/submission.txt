              Affective transfer between LLM agents:
the estimator, two pre-registered ablations, and a control that bites
                                                 Manan Wadhwa∗



                                                      Abstract
           When one language-model agent is steered into an emotion and talks to another, the second
       agent’s internal representation of its own state moves with the dose: for five of six emotions
       on Qwen3.6-27B and the same five on Llama-3-8B. We ask what carries it, and find that the
       standard linear tools give wrong answers for reasons their usual checks do not catch. The logistic-
       regression direction most work uses to read and write such states reproduces across independent
       fits at only 0.57 (27B) and 0.29 (8B) while difference-of-means reaches 0.97 and 0.94 on the
       same splits, and holding the per-sample penalty fixed does not close the gap. The reason is
       that every logistic fit on these activations is a perfect separator: its direction is the penalty’s
       tie-break among separating hyperplanes, and it drifts away from the mean-difference direction
       as the sample grows (cosine 0.97 to 0.63). With the reproducible direction, three pre-registered
       ablations test the linear account of the channel. Removing the emotion direction from the
       sender’s span at every readable layer leaves 91% of the transfer; a rank-5 affect subspace fails
       its own instrument check; and a fitted, label-free control blocks transfer where the field’s norm-
       matched random direction is inert, while a fitted label-free rank-1 direction leaves the emotion
       direction’s block of afraid (25%) and sad (29%) intact. Transfer is real, the linear rank-1 channel
       carries at most a third of it for two emotions, and ablation claims about concept directions need
       controls fitted by the same estimator, not random vectors.


1    Introduction
Systems of several language-model agents built from one model family are now common, and the
agents talk to each other. If one of them is in an emotional state, does anything of that state
reach the others? A transfer that changes the receiver’s internal representation would be invisible
in transcripts and visible only in activations, which makes it a question for representation-level
tools: steering vectors to induce a state in the sender [4, 7–9], probes to read it in the receiver, and
ablations to test what carries it.
    Those tools rest on one object, a direction in activation space. This paper’s single idea is that
the direction has to be trusted twice, once to read and once to intervene, and that both uses fail
in ways the usual checks miss. Read with a logistic-regression coeﬀicient row, the state looks like
a moving target: the direction does not reproduce across independent fits, and we show the reason
is not the sample size or the penalty but separability, so the fitted row is a tie-break rather than
an estimate. Intervene with the reproducible direction and the standard control, a norm-matched
random vector, stays inert while a control fitted by the same estimator with the labels destroyed
can block transfer as strongly as the emotion direction does.

    The evidence is three pre-registered experiments on the channel, each critiqued blind before its
wording entered this paper, on top of an estimator study across two models and three depths. The
transfer itself is robust: five of six emotions on each model, in three runs that reproduce each other
bit for bit on the 27B. The channel is not what the linear picture suggests. Removing the emotion
direction everywhere it can be read leaves a median 91% of the transfer, and only afraid and sad

shifts the readout used to check it; and a fitted label-free direction of rank 1 is inert while a fitted
label-free frame of rank 5 is not.
    The practical consequence is a change to the controls register of any ablation study of a concept
direction: beside the random direction, run the same estimator on permuted labels, match rank and
footprint if the intervention is more than rank 1, and check the manipulation with an instrument
that does not share the ablation’s target. The scientific consequence for affect transfer is narrower
than we hoped: the transfer exists, the rank-1 direction carries part of it for two emotions, and
what carries the rest is open.

Relation to prior work. That logistic probe directions can be unstable is not our discovery.
Marks and Tegmark [5] identified the deficiency and proposed mass-mean probing; Gao et al.
[2] name directional stability as a requirement for probe-then-steer pipelines and propose ridge-
tuned logistic regression, reporting robustness of 0.88–0.99 under 20% training-data ablations. Our
contribution is the quantity: reproducibility across disjoint halves, on the same estimator family,
is 0.41–0.57 on the 27B (stabdiag.logreg_27b_600, a2.focus_logreg_split_half) and 0.29 on the 8B
(a2_8b.*), and it does not rise with the sample size while difference-of-means rises to 0.94–0.98 on
the same splits. Two overlapping 80% subsamples share most of their examples; two disjoint halves
share none, and the gap between the two numbers is the finding. Braun et al. [1] corroborate from
the other side, noting that steering vectors from few samples vary widely, without fitting twice.
Herbster et al. [3] report mean-difference and logistic directions as near-identical (0.98–0.99) for
compassion; in a quantity closer to theirs than our split-half battery, though not identical to it, we
find 0.43 and 0.33–0.37. Raju [6] measure stability of a representation across feature dimensions, a
different axis.

2                                              Setup
Two agents, A and B, converse in 29 fixed scenarios. A’s message is generated under a steering
vector added at layer 42 of Qwen3.6-27B (revision 6a9e13bd) at doses 0, 0.33, 0.67 and 1.0 of a fixed
scale, with three replicate seeds; B’s reply is then sampled with common random numbers across
experimental arms, so that sampling noise shared between two arms cancels inside each paired
contrast. Two readouts: B’s present-emotion projection, the projection of B’s reply activations on
a probe fitted on one half of a 1,615-item probe pool while steering and ablation directions come
from the disjoint other half; and a generation-side forced-choice readout of A’s span, used as the
manipulation check. Every dose-response slope is an ordinary least-squares slope over the four
doses on per-scenario means; every confidence interval is a scenario-blocked bootstrap (29 clusters,
5,000 draws), and every between-arm contrast is scenario-blocked and dose-paired. The second
model is Llama-3-8B-Instruct-abliterated (revision dd67dd05), used for the estimator results and
for replications of two ablation designs. All code runs from one vendored core; every number below
was produced by a committed script from a committed result file.


3                                              The estimator: reproducibility and its mechanism
Disjoint-half reproducibility. We fit the same estimator on two disjoint halves of n items each,
ten independent splits, and report the mean per-class cosine between the two fitted directions
(Figure 1). At the 27B focus layer with n = 2000 per half, difference-of-means reaches 0.974 and
logistic regression (C = 0.5) 0.566 (a2.focus_dom_split_half, a2.focus_logreg_split_half); at layer 54,
0.979 against 0.554 (a2f54.*); on the 8B focus layer at n = 1200, 0.941 against 0.286 (a2_8b.*).
Tuning C per fit by cross-validation reaches 0.61 on the 27B (a2f.focus_tuned_c_does_not_close_gap)
and 0.37–0.41 on the 8B.

changes the effective penalty as n grows. With Cn = 0.5 Nref /n, so that the per-sample penalty is
constant, the logistic curve shows no net rise from n = 150 to 2000 at two anchors on the 27B focus
layer (0.581 at both ends for the 600 anchor, with a significant dip at 600 and a partial recovery;

Why. Every multinomial logistic fit on every half at every n used, under the fixed schedule and
C = 0.5, 50 and 500 alike (the two weak arms being one tolerance-limited solution), separates
its training half perfectly: accuracy exactly 1.000 in every cell, arm and seed; worst single-fit log-
loss 0.0055; at most 80 iterations (a2m.separable_every_n, a2m.8b_same_regime, a2m.v2_ten_seeds). The
likelihood is saturated from n = 75: the direction is the penalty’s tie-break among separating hyper-
planes. Weakening the penalty a hundredfold leaves the direction unchanged on the same half (co-
sine ≥ 0.96) and no more reproducible (0.52 against 0.58 at n = 2000; a2m.weak_penalty_same_direction).
The separator starts near the mean-difference direction and departs from it as n grows, cosine
0.97→0.63 on the 27B (a2m.logistic_departs_from_dom) and 0.91→0.43 on the 8B (a2m.8b_same_regime),
while difference-of-means becomes reproducible (Figure 2). The wording adopted from blind review:
on separable activations the multinomial direction is the penalty’s tie-break among separating hy-
perplanes, not a likelihood estimate; its split-half reproducibility plateaus at 0.55–0.60 on the 27B
and falls to 0.28 on the 8B across a 27-fold increase in n, while difference-of-means, an average
rather than an extremum, rises to 0.94–0.98 on the same splits. Not claimed: that the fit sits at
the max-margin limit (the weak arms are tolerance-limited), or a support-vector mechanism for the
drift.

Against the near-identity claim. In a quantity closer to that of Herbster et al. [3] than our
split-half battery, though still not identical to it (mean-difference against logistic, raw space, ten sub-
samples), the cosine is 0.43 on the 27B and 0.33–0.37 on the 8B, not 0.98 (a2f.caa_raw_vs_logreg_raw,
a2f8.cross_estimator).

4                                             Transfer
B’s present-emotion projection rises with A’s dose for desperate, afraid, happy, sad and angry, with
none-arm slopes from +48 to +212 and CIs excluding zero; calm’s (+44 [−3, +99]) never does
(b1c.verdict_H1; Figure 3). The same slopes appear, bit for bit, in the three runs that share the pool
and directions (b1c.partial_block_replicates, b1d.replicates_b1c_arms), and the manipulation check
on A passes for all six emotions in each. Happy’s is the weakest of the five (+48 [+8, +88]). On the
8B the same five emotions transfer (b1c8.transfer_replicates), with a steering manipulation half as
strong and less specific, so only the pattern, not the size, is compared across models.

B1e: a fitted, label-free rank-1 control (verdict H3). The same estimator on permuted
labels gives a rank-1 direction with cosine 0.004 to the emotion direction (measured before launch,
not stamped in the file) and 0.85× its footprint; removed at all 51 layers it changes no emotion’s dose-
response and removes no readable affect (b1e.permdir_inert). Against it the emotion direction blocks
afraid by 25% [17%, 33%] and sad by 29% [7%, 56%] (BH-FDR q 0.001 and 0.028; b1e.verdict_H3).
The top principal direction of A’s span, with 5.8× the footprint, blocks angry by 61% (and calm,
knife-edge on an untestable emotion) while leaving afraid and sad untouched even though it removes

they differ in rank and in footprint (6.5 against 0.5 per position), and which matters is untested. The
control’s footprint is below the emotion direction’s, so the footprint caveat stands; the secondary
readout on B’s reply agrees for sad and not for afraid (Appendix A).

6    What the controls teach
Norm-matched random and orthogonal directions, the field’s standard controls, were inert in every
run; so was a fitted, label-free rank-1 direction. What blocked without emotion information was a
fitted rank-5 frame with ten times the emotion direction’s footprint. Any ablation claim about a
concept direction therefore needs, beside the random control, a control fitted by the same estimator
on permuted labels, and if the intervention is more than rank 1, a control matched in rank and
footprint; and the manipulation check must not share its target with the ablation, or the check fails
in the way B1d’s did.


7    Limitations and what is not claimed
One probe pool and one direction estimate underlie all ablation runs on the 27B; the A-span readout
is forced-choice and shares a target with the subspace ablation; calm is untestable at this dose grid;
B1d’s per-emotion contrasts do not clear BH-FDR; the median CIs are scenario-only bootstraps
and do not include emotion-to-emotion spread. Provenance limits and the defects recorded under
review are listed in Appendix A. Not tested: the token channel (three rewrite designs failed their
own manipulation check: the neutral rewrite kept 67–98% of the readable affect and the affect-
preserving rewrite changed the transfer itself; b1c.text_arms_uninterpretable), the behavioural reach,
layers below 13, and n > d.


8    Conclusion
The idea to take away is not that affect transfers between agents, though it does, nor that logistic
probes are unstable, which was known. It is that the two failures we found are the same failure
seen twice. A logistic direction on separable activations is decided by the penalty’s tie-break, so


                                                  7
two independent fits agree only as far as their tie-breaks do; and a control direction is informative
only if it is decided the same way the intervention direction was. Random vectors are decided
by nothing, which is why they are inert and why they miss what a fitted control catches. Any
study that reads a concept with a probe and intervenes with its direction should therefore report
disjoint-half reproducibility of the direction, use an estimator that is an average rather than an
extremum, and run its ablations against a control fitted by the same estimator on permuted labels.
For affect transfer specifically, the linear rank-1 direction carries a fifth to a third of the effect
for two emotions and none of it for the others, and the next experiment is one that separates the
receiver re-reading the sender’s tokens from the receiver reading the sender’s residual stream.

within scenario and dose; Benjamini–Hochberg over the testable emotions where a per-emotion rate
is reported; every interval in the paper is such a bootstrap and every count of runs is stated. The
probe pools and scenario lists are committed with the results.


A Details and defects recorded under review
Each item below is in the registry note of the claim it qualifies.

   • B1c. The contrast between the 30-layer and 51-layer windows was declared in the driver,
     not in the pre-registration; its null is unpowered for desperate and calm. Calm’s contrast is
     the largest and is dropped by the testability rule. Afraid’s top-dose readout is non-monotone.
     The pre-registration stamp is empty because the driver resolved its path relative to the wrong
     directory; the document was on the machine one second before the run started. The pool has
     1,615 items against the registered 2,160; the false-discovery rate ran over five emotions rather
     than six.
   • B1d. The permuted-label subspace removed only 6.5% of afraid’s readable affect and still
     blocked its transfer by 49.5, so overlap with the readable-affect direction does not explain its
     blocking; recomputing the instrument share with each arm’s own floor keeps three failures
     but changes which emotions fail. The median’s confidence interval resamples scenarios only.
     No per-emotion contrast clears the registered false-discovery threshold (q = 0.051).
   • B1e. The control removes less norm than the emotion direction (0.84× afraid, 0.77× sad) and
     less than B1c’s inert random direction (1.22), so it is a fitted label-free null, not a footprint-
     matched one; the 0.85×–5.8× footprint band, where B1d’s frame blocked, is unsampled. The
     rule tests the point fraction and sad’s range starts at 0.07. The secondary readout on B’s reply

    margin identification and the support-vector mechanism are not claimed. Cross-estimator
    cosines use subsamples that overlap by about half the pool, so their intervals overstate preci-
    sion. The layer-43 and layer-54 files come from three driver builds; the shared cells agree bit
    for bit.
