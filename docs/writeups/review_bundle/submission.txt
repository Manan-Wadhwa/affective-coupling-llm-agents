       Steered affect moves a conversational partner’s activations:
    the estimator, three pre-registered ablations, and which controls
                                   bite
                                                 Manan Wadhwa∗



                                                      Abstract
          When one language-model agent is steered into an emotion and talks to another, a linear
      probe’s projection of the second agent’s reply activations moves with the dose, for five of six
      emotions on Qwen3.6-27B and on Llama-3-8B. We ask what carries it, and find that the standard
      linear tools mislead in ways their usual checks do not catch. The logistic-regression direction
      most work uses to read and write such states reproduces across disjoint halves at only 0.57
      (27B, n = 2000 per half) and 0.29 (8B), while difference-of-means reaches 0.97 and 0.94 on
      the same splits, and a fixed per-sample penalty does not close the gap. In the n < d regime
      probe studies work in, every logistic fit is a perfect separator: its direction is the penalty’s
      tie-break, drifting from the mean difference as n grows (cosine 0.97 to 0.63); projected onto 100
      principal components, where n ≫ d′ , the fits stop separating and the direction reproduces at
      0.90. The cost is largely geometric: the two estimators’ held-out projections correlate at 0.90–
      0.96. With the reproducible direction, three pre-registered ablations test the linear account of
      the channel. Removing the emotion direction at every readable layer leaves a median 91% of
      the transfer against a random-direction control (85% against baseline); a rank-5 affect subspace
      fails its own instrument check; a rank-5 subspace fitted by the same estimator on permuted
      labels blocks three emotions as strongly as the affect subspace does, while a random 5-frame
      of half its footprint and a permuted-label rank-1 direction are inert. The emotion direction’s
      blocks of afraid (25%) and sad (29%) survive the rank-1 permuted control; across models only
      sad’s replicates. Whether rank or footprint makes the rank-5 control bite is untested, and the
      receiver’s “other-speaker” projection rises with dose as much as its own, so the probe does not
      separate the receiver’s state from its model of the sender’s.


1    Introduction
Systems of several language-model agents built from one model family are now common, and the
agents talk to each other [3, 8]. If one of them is in an emotional state, does anything of that state
reach the others? A transfer that changes the receiver’s internal representation would be invisible in
transcripts and visible only in activations, which makes it a question for representation-level tools:
steering vectors to induce a state in the sender [7, 10, 13, 14], probes to read it in the receiver, and
ablations to test what carries it.
    Those tools rest on one object, a direction in activation space. This paper’s single idea is that
the direction has to be trusted twice, once to read and once to intervene, and that both uses fail

sample size or the penalty but separability, so the fitted row is a tie-break rather than an estimate

vector, stays inert, while a rank-5 subspace fitted by the same estimator with the labels destroyed
blocks transfer as strongly as the affect subspace itself.
     The evidence is an estimator study across two models and three depths and three pre-registered
experiments on the channel, each critiqued blind before its wording entered this paper. The response
itself is robust to sampling: five of six emotions on each model. The channel is not what the linear
picture suggests: removing the emotion direction everywhere it can be read leaves most of the
response, only afraid and sad lose a fifth to a third, a rank-5 affect subspace cannot serve as an
instrument because it shifts the readout used to check it, and a rank-1 permuted-label direction is
inert while a rank-5 permuted-label frame is not.
     The practical consequence is a change to the controls register of any ablation study of a concept
direction: beside the random direction, run the same estimator on permuted labels at the rank of
the intervention, report the footprint of every arm, and check the manipulation with an instrument
that does not share the ablation’s target. The scientific consequence for affect transfer is narrower
than we hoped: the transfer exists, the rank-1 direction carries part of it for two emotions, only
sad’s part replicates across models, and what carries the rest is open.

Relation to prior work. That logistic probe directions can be unstable is not our discovery.
Marks and Tegmark [9] identified the deficiency and proposed mass-mean probing, noting that logis-
tic regression converges toward the maximum-margin separator on separable data, the implicit bias
analysed by Soudry et al. [12]; Gao et al. [5] name directional stability as a requirement for probe-
then-steer pipelines and propose ridge-tuned logistic regression, reporting mean pairwise robustness

of 0.87–0.98 under 20% training-data ablations. Our contribution is the quantity: reproducibility
across disjoint halves, which share no examples where two 80% subsamples share most, plateaus at
0.55–0.61 on the 27B and falls to 0.28–0.38 on the 8B while difference-of-means rises to 0.94–0.98
on the same splits. Braun et al. [2] corroborate for mean-difference steering vectors: refit on re-
sampled draws of 5–30 activations they vary widely, and agree at cosine above 0.99 from 200–500
draws, which is the estimator we also find reproducible. Herbster et al. [6] report mean-difference
and logistic directions as near-identical (0.98–0.99) for compassion; in a quantity close to theirs we
find 0.43 and 0.33–0.37. Raju [11] measure stability of a representation across feature dimensions,
a different axis.


2    Setup
Two agents, A and B, converse in 29 fixed scenarios. A’s message is generated under a steering
vector added at layer 42 of Qwen3.6-27B (revision 6a9e13bd) at doses 0, 0.33, 0.67 and 1.0 of a fixed
scale (the activation RMS at the focus layer), with three replicate seeds; B’s reply is then sampled
with common random numbers across experimental arms, so that sampling noise shared between
two arms cancels inside each paired contrast. Two readouts: B’s present-emotion projection, the
projection of B’s reply activations on a difference-of-means probe for B’s own emotion, and B’s
other-speaker projection on the matching probe for the interlocutor’s emotion; both probes are
fitted on one half (808 items) of a 1,615-item probe pool while the steering and ablation directions
are fitted, per layer, on the disjoint other half (807 items). A generation-side forced-choice readout
of A’s span is the manipulation check. Every dose-response slope is an ordinary least-squares
slope over the four doses on per-scenario means; every confidence interval is a scenario-blocked
percentile bootstrap [4] (29 clusters, 5,000 draws), every between-arm contrast is scenario-blocked
and dose-paired, and false-discovery rates are Benjamini–Hochberg [1] within one family of five
testable emotions unless stated.

Two pools. The estimator study (Section 3) uses a separate, larger generation pool built for it:
4,253 items on the 27B (kept from 5,760 generated) and 3,898 on the 8B, so that disjoint halves of
up to 2,000 items (27B) and 1,200 items (8B) exist. The ablation experiments use the 1,615-item
probe pool above, on which the deployed directions’ own split-half cosine at 600 per half is 0.88–0.93
across the ablated layers (the pre-registered gate).

Footprint. For every ablation arm we record the norm of the component removed from A’s
residual stream, averaged over the masked positions and the ablated layers; all ratios are relative
to the emotion direction’s value in the same run (Table 3).


3    The estimator: reproducibility and its mechanism
Disjoint-half reproducibility. We fit the same estimator on two disjoint halves of n items each,
ten independent splits, and report the mean per-class cosine between the two fitted directions
(Figure 1). At the 27B focus layer with n = 2000 per half, difference-of-means reaches 0.974 and
logistic regression (C = 0.5) 0.566 (a2.focus_dom_split_half, a2.focus_logreg_split_half); at layer 54,
0.979 against 0.554 (a2f54.*); on the 8B focus layer at n = 1200, 0.941 against 0.286 (a2_8b.*).
Tuning C per fit by cross-validation reaches 0.61 on the 27B (a2f.focus_tuned_c_does_not_close_gap)
and 0.38 at the 8B focus layer (0.37–0.41 across three depths; a2f8.lam_declines, note).

Holding the per-sample penalty fixed does not help. A natural objection is that a fixed
C changes the effective penalty as n grows. With Cn = 0.5 Nref /n, so that the per-sample
penalty is constant, the logistic curve shows no net rise from n = 150 to 2000 at two anchors
on the 27B focus layer (0.581 at both ends for the 600 anchor; a dip at 600 of −0.021 that is
significant in a paired test over the ten shared splits, 1 of 10 positive, and a partial recovery;
a2f.focus_logreg_fixed_lambda_flat, a2f2.lam_anchor2000_no_rise), one drop at 300→600 and then flat
at layer 54 (a2f54.lam_declines_anchor600), a rise at layer 16, and on the 8B a peak at 150 followed by
a fall to 0.276 at 1200 (a2f8.lam_declines). The picture is depth-specific, with no convergence over
the reachable range (n ≤ 2000, d = 5120 on the 27B, a 27-fold increase in n; n ≤ 1200, d = 4096
on the 8B): a proportional-regime statement, not an asymptotic one.

Why. Every multinomial logistic fit on every half at every n used, under the fixed schedule and
C = 0.5, 50 and 500 alike (the two weak arms being one tolerance-limited solution), separates


                                                                                                                                            4
its training half perfectly: accuracy exactly 1.000 in every cell, arm and seed; worst single-fit log-
loss 0.0055; at most 80 iterations (a2m.separable_every_n, a2m.8b_same_regime, a2m.v2_ten_seeds). The
likelihood is saturated from n = 75: the direction is the penalty’s tie-break among separating
hyperplanes. Weakening the penalty a hundredfold or a thousandfold leaves the direction un-
changed on the same half (cosine ≥ 0.96) and no more reproducible (0.52 against 0.58 at n = 2000;
a2m.weak_penalty_same_direction). The separator starts near the mean-difference direction and de-
parts from it as n grows, cosine 0.97→0.63 on the 27B (a2m.logistic_departs_from_dom) and 0.91→0.43
on the 8B (a2m.8b_same_regime), while difference-of-means becomes reproducible (Figure 2). In these
units difference-of-means is the raw-space direction (µ+ −µ− )/σ 2 ; the plain mean difference µ+ −µ−
that contrastive activation addition uses agrees with the logistic direction at 0.43 (27B) and 0.33–
0.37 (8B) (a2f.caa_raw_vs_logreg_raw, a2f8.cross_estimator), against the 0.98–0.99 that Herbster et al.
[6] report for compassion. Not claimed: that the fit sits at the max-margin limit (the weak arms
are tolerance-limited), or a support-vector mechanism for the drift.

Is this just n < d, and does it cost anything? Separation is guaranteed for generic data
whenever n < d, so the mechanism is regime-conditional and the paper’s recommendation holds
because probe pools in this literature are small relative to model width. Three checks bound
what this means. On held-out items at the 27B focus layer the logistic probe decodes better than
difference-of-means (six-way accuracy 0.82 to 0.94 against 0.79 to 0.88 at n = 75 to 2000), and
the two estimators’ projections of the same held-out items correlate at 0.96 to 0.90 over the same
range (a2l.heldout_and_projcorr): the coeﬀicient row is a fine classifier and a poor direction, the
dissociation Marks and Tegmark [9] described, and the instability is largely geometric. Projecting
the activations onto their top 100 principal components and refitting shows the regime dependence
directly: with n/d′ up to 20 the fits stop separating their halves from n = 600 (training accuracy
0.999, 0.989, 0.974 at n = 600, 1200, 2000), the logistic direction becomes reproducible (0.90 at
n = 2000 in standardised coordinates against 0.94 for difference-of-means, from 0.39 and 0.33 at
n = 75), and its cosine to the difference-of-means direction stays at 0.91–0.94 across n instead of
falling from 0.90 to 0.62 as it does at full width; held-out accuracy in 100 dimensions is 0.92 against
0.94 at full width (a2l.lowd_recovers). The 8B behaves the same way (a2l.8b_lowd). The mechanism
is therefore the n < d regime itself, and the recommendation holds because probe pools in this
literature are small relative to model width.


4    Transfer
B’s present-emotion projection rises with A’s dose for desperate, afraid, happy, sad and angry, with
none-arm slopes from +48 to +212 and CIs excluding zero; calm’s (+44 [−3, +99]) does not, so
calm is untestable under the pre-registered rule (b1c.verdict_H1; Figure 3). The slope is a poor
summary for two emotions: happy rises +71 [+50, +94] over the first dose step and +0 [−24, +25]
over the last, calm +38 [−3, +78] then −34 [−77, +13], while desperate, sad and angry rise at both
steps (rev.dose_shape). The transfer is robust to sampling: the B1 follow-up run, with independently
sampled replies, gives the same five testable emotions and afraid/sad as the blocked ones (b1f.*);
B1c, B1d and B1e share the pool, the directions and the seed recipe and agree bit for bit on
their shared arms, which is a determinism check, not a replication (b1d.replicates_b1c_arms). The
manipulation check on A passes for all six emotions in each run. On the 8B the same five emotions
respond (b1c8.transfer_replicates), with a steering manipulation half as strong and less specific, so
only the pattern is compared.

What the probe measures. B’s other-speaker projection, the matching probe for the inter-
locutor’s emotion, rises with A’s dose as well: slopes +59, +119, +65, +93, +96 for desper-

this work’s claim that the present shift exceeds the other shift did not survive a non-circular probe
(report 18 in the repository).


5    Three pre-registered ablations

13–63 is that layer’s own difference-of-means direction; “readable” means the layer passes the pre-
registered split-half gate (≥ 0.80), which every layer from 13 upward does and the window starts
at 0.2× depth by design.

6    What the controls teach
Table 2 puts every arm on one scale. The random direction, the random 5-frame and the permuted-
label rank-1 direction are inert; the permuted-label rank-5 frame blocks three emotions at the
false-discovery threshold, as strongly as the affect subspace blocks two of them; the top principal
direction blocks a different emotion. So a control that is fitted by the same estimator at the same
rank as the intervention can bite where a random control of the same or larger footprint does not,
and a study that reports only the random control would have called B1d’s blocks emotion-specific.
Whether the fitted rank-5 frame bites because of its rank or its footprint is an open question that
one further arm would settle (a random 5-frame scaled to the permuted frame’s footprint). Until
then the rule is procedural: beside the random direction, run the same estimator on permuted
labels at the rank of the intervention, report every arm’s footprint and dose-0 readout shift, and
check the manipulation with an instrument that does not share the ablation’s target.


7    Limitations and what is not claimed
The present-emotion projection does not separate B’s own state from B’s representation of A’s; the
other-speaker projection moves comparably. One probe pool and one direction estimate underlie all
ablation runs on the 27B, and no ablation interval reflects the direction’s own sampling variability
(the deployed directions’ split-half cosine is 0.88–0.93 at 600 per half). The A-span readout is
forced-choice and shares a target with the subspace ablation. The dose-response slope summarises
a saturating curve for happy and afraid and a reversing one for calm. All intervals are percentile
bootstraps over 29 scenario clusters; multiplicity is controlled within experiments, not across the
three experiments and two models. The estimator mechanism is regime-conditional (n < d): the
low-dimensional check reproduces it and n > d on the full width is not reached. Provenance limits
and the defects recorded under review are listed in Appendix B; seeds, hyperparameters, hardware
and software are in Appendix A. Not tested: the token channel (three rewrite designs failed their
own manipulation check: the neutral rewrite kept 67–98% of the readable affect and the affect-
preserving rewrite changed the transfer itself; b1c.text_arms_uninterpretable), the behavioural reach,
layers below 13, and an ablation rerun with the logistic direction.


8    Conclusion
The idea to take away is not that affect transfers between agents, though something does, nor that
logistic probes are unstable, which was known. It is that the two failures we found are the same
failure seen twice. A logistic direction on separable activations is decided by the penalty’s tie-break,

                                                   9
so two independent fits agree only as far as their tie-breaks do; and a control direction is informative
only if it is decided the way the intervention direction was. Random vectors are decided by nothing,
which is why they are inert and why they miss what a fitted control catches. Any study that reads
a concept with a probe and intervenes with its direction should report disjoint-half reproducibility
of the direction, use an estimator that is an average rather than an extremum, and run its ablations
against a control fitted by the same estimator on permuted labels at the intervention’s rank. For
affect transfer specifically, the linear rank-1 direction carries a fifth to a third of the effect for two
emotions and none of it for the others, one of the two replicates, the probe cannot yet say whose
state it reads, and the next experiments are the two that would settle the open questions here:
a footprint-scaled random frame, and an intervention that separates the receiver re-reading the
sender’s tokens from the receiver reading the sender’s residual stream.

Code, pre-registrations, result files and the claim registry are in the accompanying repository
(research-use licence). Models: Qwen3.6-27B revision 6a9e13bd and Llama-3-8B-Instruct-abliterated

rows of 29 scenarios each, 19–21 s per arm-row), the 8B one under thirty minutes. Seeds: pool
generation seed 0; per-generation seeds are a CRC32 of (seed, emotion, dose, replicate, arm,
part) with the arm field empty for the receiver’s reply so that arms share random numbers;
ten split seeds (0–9) for every split-half number; bootstrap seed 0 with 5,000 draws. Hyper-

      false-discovery rate ran over five emotions rather than six.

      blocked its transfer, so overlap with the readable-affect direction does not explain its blocking;
