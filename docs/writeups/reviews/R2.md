# Review — Reviewer 2

**Paper:** *Affective transfer between LLM agents: the estimator, two pre-registered ablations, and a control that bites*
**Venue:** ICLR, main track

---

## 1. Summary

The paper steers agent A into one of six emotions, lets A talk to agent B, and reads B's "present-emotion projection" off a linear probe; that projection rises with A's steering dose for five of six emotions on two models. It then argues that the logistic-regression direction the field uses to read and write such states is not an estimate at all — every fit on these activations separates its training half perfectly, so the fitted row is the penalty's tie-break among separating hyperplanes, reproducing across disjoint halves at only 0.57 (27B) / 0.29 (8B) against 0.97 / 0.94 for difference-of-means. Three pre-registered ablations then test whether a rank-1 emotion direction carries the transfer (it carries a fifth to a third for two emotions and nothing for the rest), and the paper's headline methodological claim is that a control direction fitted by the same estimator on permuted labels can block transfer where the field's norm-matched random direction is inert.

## 3. Strengths

1. **The provenance machinery is the real thing, not a gesture.** Every number carries a monospace identifier into a machine-checked registry; model revisions are pinned (`6a9e13bd`, `dd67dd05`); per-generation seeds are a CRC32 of (seed, emotion, dose, replicate, arm, part) *with the arm field deliberately blanked for the receiver's reply* so that arms share random numbers. That last detail is a correct, deliberate variance-reduction design that most activation-steering papers do not attempt, and it is what makes the paired between-arm contrasts worth reading at all.

2. **The negative results are reported as negative results, in the abstract.** B1d is declared `instrument_failed` rather than spun; the three text-channel rewrite designs are declared uninterpretable because they failed their own manipulation check (the neutral rewrite kept 67–98% of the readable affect); calm is dropped as untestable because its none-arm slope includes zero; the B1d per-emotion contrasts are stated not to clear the registered BH-FDR rule at q = 0.051. I went looking for the usual quiet reframing of failures and did not find it.

3. **The B1e PC1 dissociation is a genuinely useful result and cuts against the paper's own convenience.** The top principal direction of A's span removes 79% and 71% of afraid's and sad's readable affect and blocks neither, while blocking angry by 61%. That is a direct empirical refutation of the "if the ablation removed the readable concept, it must have removed the causal channel" inference that the interpretability literature runs on daily. It deserves more of the paper than it gets.

4. **Internal consistency between figures and text is genuinely high where I could check it.** I reconstructed all six Figure 3 none-arm OLS slopes from the plotted per-dose points (desperate +83.4 vs +84; afraid +211 vs +212; happy +49.8 vs +48; calm +43.6 vs +44; sad ≈ +98; angry +197 vs +196), and Table 1 agrees with Figure 4 to ±0.01 in all thirty cells. The B1d slope-unit blocks (−49.5, −26.7, −44.3) reconstruct from Table 1's fractions times the none-arm slopes. This is rarer than it should be and I credit it.

## 4. Weaknesses

**W1. The separation "mechanism" is a restatement of n < d, and the paper never runs the one experiment that would separate the two.** Section 3 reports d = 5120 and a maximum of n = 2000 per half; on the 8B, n ≤ 1200 with d = 4096. Every fit in the paper is therefore in a regime where points in general position are linearly separable with probability 1. "Every multinomial logistic fit ... separates its training half perfectly: accuracy exactly 1.000 in every cell" is not a discovery about *these activations*; it is arithmetic. The paper concedes this obliquely ("a proportional-regime statement, not an asymptotic one"; "Not tested: ... n > d") and then states the conclusion generally anyway, in the abstract ("every logistic fit on these activations is a perfect separator") and in Section 8 as advice to the field. The decisive control is cheap and absent: project the pool to 100 or 500 dimensions (PCA, or random projection) so that n ≫ d, refit, and report split-half cosine. If logistic reproducibility recovers, the finding is "don't fit logistic probes in the n < d regime", which is a much narrower and much older claim than the one the paper makes. If it does not recover, the paper has a real result. Right now it has neither.

**W2. The paper never shows that logistic instability changes a single downstream conclusion.** This is the load-bearing gap. Section 3 establishes that two logistic fits agree at cosine 0.57 and two mean-difference fits at 0.97. It never reports held-out *accuracy* for either estimator — only training accuracy 1.000 — so the reader cannot tell whether the logistic probe is a bad readout or merely a non-identified parameterisation of an equally good readout. Directions at cosine 0.57 in 5120 dimensions can induce projections correlated at 0.99 on the data manifold. And the ablation suite (Section 5) is never rerun with the logistic direction. If B1c returns the same verdict under both directions, the estimator study is a curiosity, not a warning. Run B1c's four arms with the logistic direction and report the verdict; and report held-out per-class accuracy for logistic and difference-of-means at every n in Figure 1.

**W3. The headline control claim — "a control that bites" — is confounded with rank and footprint, and the arm that would resolve it is reported with no numbers at all.** The apples-to-apples comparison in this paper is between a rank-1 random direction (Table 1, B1c row: inert) and a rank-1 fitted permuted-label direction (Table 1, B1e last row: 0.02, −0.00, −0.10, −0.04, −0.01, 0.00 — inert). *Both* rank-1 controls are inert. The only control that blocks is B1d's rank-5 permuted subspace, which the paper itself says has ~13× the per-position footprint of the rank-1 control (6.5 against 0.5) and then admits "which matters is untested". The claim is rescued only by the clause "while a random 5-frame is inert" — and that arm appears nowhere else in the paper. It has no row in Table 1, no bar in Figure 4, no CI, no number. The entire Section 6 rule, the title, and the abstract's "a fitted, label-free control blocks transfer where the field's norm-matched random direction is inert" rest on one unreported comparison. Either report the random rank-5 frame with the same five-emotion blocked fractions and CIs as every other arm, or run a footprint-matched rank-1 fitted control (the appendix concedes the 0.85×–5.8× band is unsampled) and let the claim stand or fall.

**W4. The receiver-side readout is never shown to measure B's *own* state rather than B's representation of A's state, which is the mechanism the paper's title claims.** The abstract says "the second agent's internal representation of *its own state* moves with the dose". The evidence is that a linear probe's projection on B's reply activations rises with A's dose. A probe trained on a pool of first-person emotion text will light up equally well on "my interlocutor is desperate and I should respond to that", which is not affective transfer, it is reading comprehension. The paper needs a discriminant control and does not have one: no probe for other-directed vs self-directed affect, no third-person or reported-speech control message, no scrambled-content dose control, no behavioural correlate. The three text-channel rewrite arms — exactly where this would have been tested — failed their manipulation check and are declared uninterpretable (`b1c.text_arms_uninterpretable`). Section 7 lists "the behavioural reach" as untested, which is honest, but the untested thing is not a nice-to-have extension; it is the difference between the paper's claim and its evidence. At minimum: fit an "A's emotion" probe on B's activations and show it does *not* explain the dose-response that the "B's present emotion" probe does.

**W5. It appears that one direction is projected out at 51 different layers, and if so the central ablation is underpowered by construction.** Section 5: "Projecting the difference-of-means emotion direction out of A's span at every hidden state from 13 to 63"; B1e: "removed at all 51 layers" (13–63 inclusive, which does check out). The Setup says directions "come from the disjoint other half" of the probe pool but never says they are refit per layer, and every mention is singular. Emotion directions are layer-specific in every representation-engineering paper the submission cites. If a layer-43 direction is projected out at layer 15, almost nothing of layer 15's emotion representation is removed, and "removing the emotion direction at every readable layer leaves 91% of the transfer" degrades into "removing a poorly-aimed direction leaves 91% of the transfer" — which supports no conclusion about rank-1 sufficiency. Relatedly, "every readable layer" is defined nowhere; why 13, why 63, and what makes 12 unreadable? State whether the direction is refit per layer, report the per-layer readable-affect removal profile, and if it is a single direction, rerun with per-layer difference-of-means directions.

**W6. B1c's manipulation check shares a target with B1c's ablation, and the paper concedes this only for B1d.** Section 7 says "the A-span readout is forced-choice and shares a target with *the subspace ablation*". But B1c also ablates A's span, and the "readable affect removed" quantity used in `b1c.readout_residual` is presumably read by the same A-span instrument. The correlation the paper leans on — "the blocked fraction tracks how much readable affect the ablation removes (r ≈ 0.85 over five points; blocked-per-removed ≈ 0.27)" — is therefore between two quantities both computed downstream of the same intervention, using an instrument the intervention perturbs. Two further problems with that same sentence: r ≈ 0.85 on n = 5 is p ≈ 0.07, i.e. not evidence; and the extrapolation to "blocked-per-removed ≈ 0.27" is a linear extrapolation from five points to a 100%-removal counterfactual that is nowhere in the data. This one correlation is what upgrades B1c from "the ablation didn't do much" to "the rank-1 direction is insufficient", and it cannot carry that weight.

**W7. Statistical practice is inconsistent across the paper and the dose summary is wrong for at least three emotions.** (i) Every interval is a percentile bootstrap over 29 clusters; percentile cluster bootstraps are known to undercover below roughly 30–50 clusters, and no coverage check or small-cluster correction is reported. (ii) BH-FDR is enforced in B1e (q 0.001, 0.028) and in B1d (where the paper correctly reports failure at q = 0.051), but Figure 4's caption asserts "The fitted rank-5 permuted subspace (third bar) blocks afraid, sad and angry" from unadjusted "CI-implied ranges", a term the paper never defines. (iii) No multiplicity control spans the three experiments and two models; afraid and sad are selected as the blockers from six emotions across five contrasts, and afraid then fails to replicate — the textbook signature of selection. (iv) The dose-response is summarised by an OLS slope over four doses, but Figure 3 shows happy peaking at dose 0.33 and flat-to-declining thereafter, calm peaking at 0.67 and crashing at 1.0, and afraid saturating; I verified the reported slopes reproduce (+48, +44, +212), which is precisely the problem — happy's "+48 [+8, +88]" is driven almost entirely by the 0→0.33 step. Report a monotone trend test (Jonckheere–Terpstra or an isotonic fit) or the dose-0-vs-dose-1 paired contrast alongside the slope.

**W8. Presentation defects that obscure the numbers.** Figure 4's y-axis is set by calm, an emotion the paper itself declares untestable, and calm's bars (0.94, 1.56, 1.02) are ratios with a near-zero denominator; happy's blue and orange whiskers run off the bottom of the axis and are silently clipped. Figure 3 uses unnormalised probe scores with a different y-range per panel and shows no per-point uncertainty, so the eye cannot compare arms across emotions. Figure 1's right panel extends the x-axis to 2000 where the 8B has no data point at all. And the monospace registry identifiers, which are excellent for an auditor with the repository, are unusable for a reviewer without it — put the key ones in a table with the values they resolve to.

## 5. Cross-checks and mismatches

I checked fifteen numbers across text, Table 1, Figures 1–4 and Appendix A. Eight reconstructions were clean (all six Figure 3 slopes; the median 9% of {0.06, 0.21, 0.05, 0.33, 0.09}; the median 0.14 of {0.14, −0.13, 0.05, 0.26, 0.17}; the B1d slope-unit blocks −49.5/−26.7/−44.3 against fractions × none-slopes; all thirty Table 1 ↔ Figure 4 cells; 13–63 = 51 layers; abstract 0.57/0.29/0.97/0.94 against §3's 0.566/0.286/0.974/0.941 and Figure 1). The following do not reconcile, and I report them exactly:

1. **Figure 1 caption vs §3, 8B.** Caption: "the three logistic variants plateau near 0.6 (27B) or **fall to 0.28** (8B), **including** the one whose per-sample penalty is held fixed." §3: "Tuning C per fit by cross-validation ... [reaches] **0.37–0.41 on the 8B**", and Figure 1's right panel puts tuned C at ≈0.383 at n = 1200. Two of three variants fall to 0.28; the caption says three.

2. **Related-work paragraph vs §3, 27B.** Related work: "reproducibility across disjoint halves, on the same estimator family, is **0.41–0.57 on the 27B**". §3: "Tuning C per fit by cross-validation **reaches 0.61 on the 27B**" (`a2f.focus_tuned_c_does_not_close_gap`), which Figure 1 confirms at ≈0.61. The quoted range excludes the paper's own highest same-family value, in the paragraph where the paper is arguing against a competitor's 0.88–0.99.

3. **The B1c random direction has two different magnitudes.** §5: "A unit-norm random direction, **removing twice the norm** ...". Appendix A (B1e): "The control removes less norm than the emotion direction (0.84× afraid, 0.77× sad) and less than **B1c's inert random direction (1.22)**." Twice, or 1.22×? These may be different quantities — but *footprint* is never defined anywhere in the paper, and it is the quantity on which W3's entire control argument turns.

4. **Two unreconciled values of cos(difference-of-means, logistic) on the 27B.** Figure 2 and §3: the same-half cosine falls to **0.63** at n = 2000 (`a2m.logistic_departs_from_dom`). "Against the near-identity claim": the cosine is **0.43** on the 27B (`a2f.caa_raw_vs_logreg_raw`). The paper attributes the difference to "raw space, ten subsamples" without saying what the non-raw space is, and quotes the lower of the two when rebutting Herbster et al.

5. **The abstract claims a five-emotion replication that Appendix A withdraws.** Abstract: transfer "for five of six emotions on Qwen3.6-27B and **the same five** on Llama-3-8B". Appendix A: on the 8B "**afraid's readout sits below chance**", and §5 states "afraid is unreplicated" and "Only sad replicates across models on both outcomes". An emotion whose readout is below chance should not be counted in the abstract's five.

6. **The estimator study cannot have come from the stated pool.** Setup: "a probe fitted on one half of **a 1,615-item probe pool**". §3 fits at **n = 2000 per half**, i.e. 4,000 items minimum, and Figure 1 plots that point on both 27B panels. The estimator study's pool is never described, sized or related to the ablation pool.

7. **The headline reproducibility number is not the reproducibility of the direction actually deployed.** The abstract sells difference-of-means at 0.97; the ablation and steering directions are fit on one half of 1,615 items, i.e. n ≈ 807, where Figure 1 reads ≈0.93 (interpolating 0.915 at 600 and 0.953 at 1200). State the split-half cosine at the deployed n.

8. **A control arm is asserted with no reported number.** §5: "while **a random 5-frame is inert**". No row in Table 1, no bar in Figure 4, no CI, no value in the text. Section 6's rule and the paper's title depend on it. Likewise `b1d.rank5_vs_rank1`'s "+29.3" and "−60.6" cannot be reconstructed from Table 1 or Figure 4, because the rank-5-versus-none arm is not reported either.

9. **Figure 4's caption asserts blocking the text says is not established.** Caption: "The fitted rank-5 permuted subspace (third bar) **blocks** afraid, sad and angry." §5: "**no per-emotion contrast clears the registered BH-FDR rule** (q = 0.051)". If these are different test families, say so in the caption.

10. **The paper asserts both a tracking law and its violation, one page apart.** §5, B1c: "The blocked fraction **tracks** how much readable affect the ablation removes (**r ≈ 0.85** over five points)". §5, B1e: PC1 "leav[es] afraid and sad **untouched even though it removes 79% and 71% of their readable affect**: readable-affect removal and blocking **dissociate**." Both cannot be general. The paper never reconciles them, and the first is what licenses the "blocked-per-removed ≈ 0.27" extrapolation that upgrades B1c to a claim about rank-1 insufficiency.

11. *(Minor, pixel-read, flagged as such.)* §3 reports the 8B same-half cos(logistic, DoM) as falling from **0.91**; Figure 2's right panel reads ≈0.92 at n = 75. Within my reading error; noting it only because the 27B pair (0.97 / 0.972) matched exactly.

One further presentational point that is not a mismatch but is a policy problem: the submission carries an author name, a dated draft line, and repository paths (`docs/review/claims.json`, `src/rev3/paper_figures.py`), and describes its own blind-review history ("Seven of our own interpretive sentences were withdrawn under blind review"). Whatever the merits, this is not an anonymised submission.

## 6. Questions for the authors

1. Is the ablation direction refit at each of hidden states 13–63, or is a single direction (fitted at layer 43) projected out at all 51 layers? If the latter, what is the per-layer readable-affect removal profile, and does the "leaves 91%" result survive per-layer directions?

2. What defines "every readable layer" as 13–63? What is the criterion, and what does the readout do at layers 0–12?

3. Please define *footprint* precisely, in the units used for 0.85×, 1.22, 5.8×, and "6.5 against 0.5 per position". Is it the mean per-position norm removed from the residual stream, and is it measured on A's span at the ablated layers?

4. What are the blocked fractions and CIs for the **random rank-5 frame**, per emotion, in the format of Table 1? Section 6's rule depends on this arm and it is currently unreported.

5. What is the **held-out** per-class accuracy of the logistic and difference-of-means probes at each n in Figure 1? And what is the correlation between the two directions' *projections* on held-out data at n = 2000, where their cosine is 0.57?

6. If you project the activations to d′ ≪ n (say 200 dimensions) and refit, does perfect separation disappear, and does logistic split-half reproducibility recover? This is the experiment that separates "logistic on activations is a tie-break" from "logistic in the n < d regime is a tie-break".

7. What verdict does B1c return when the ablation and steering directions are the **logistic** directions instead of difference-of-means? If the verdict is unchanged, what practical consequence does the estimator study have?

8. What is the pool used for the Section 3 estimator study — size, composition, and its relation to the 1,615-item ablation pool? How can n = 2000 per half be drawn from 1,615 items?

9. Can you rule out that B's "present-emotion projection" is reading B's representation of *A's* state? Specifically: does a probe fitted to detect the interlocutor's emotion show the same dose-response, and does it screen off the present-emotion probe's?

10. Why is `a2f.caa_raw_vs_logreg_raw` = 0.43 while the same-half cosine in Figure 2 is 0.63 at n = 2000? What differs, and which is the right comparison to Herbster et al.?

11. Given that afraid and sad were selected from six emotions across five contrasts, and afraid then failed to replicate on the 8B, what is the family-wise or FDR-adjusted status of the surviving claim, adjusting across experiments rather than within?

12. Absent a valid pre-registration stamp for B1c, what independent evidence establishes that the B1c analysis plan predates the run?

## 7. Overall

**Score: 4 / 10. Confidence: 4 / 5. Recommendation: reject at the ICLR main-track bar — but a near miss, and I would move to 6 or 7 on a well-executed revision.**

I came to this expecting to find the usual thing — a probe projection wiggling, a random-vector control, and a mechanism claim resting on neither — and that is not what this is. The provenance discipline is genuinely better than most of what this subfield submits: pinned revisions, seeded common random numbers designed so that arms share sampling noise, disjoint fit-and-deploy pool halves, a per-number registry, and an appendix that volunteers its own broken pre-registration stamp and a pool 25% short of what was registered. The negative results are reported as negative results in the abstract, and the B1e PC1 dissociation is a real contribution to how this field should reason about ablations. I concede all of that, and I do not concede it easily.

But the two claims in the title are the two the experiments do not support. "A control that bites" is established only by a rank-5 intervention with roughly thirteen times the footprint of the rank-1 control; the rank-matched fitted control (B1e) is exactly as inert as the random one, as the paper's own Table 1 shows, and the arm that would settle whether *fitted* or *big* is doing the work — the random rank-5 frame — is asserted in a subordinate clause with no number anywhere in the paper. Meanwhile the estimator half diagnoses perfect separation in a regime (n ≤ 2000, d = 5120) where separation is guaranteed by dimension counting, never runs the low-dimensional control that would distinguish the two explanations, never reports held-out accuracy that would show the instability costs anything, and never reruns an ablation with the "bad" direction to show a verdict flips. And underneath both halves sits a dependent variable — a linear probe's projection on the receiver's activations — that the paper has not shown measures the receiver's own affect rather than its comprehension of the sender's, which is the entire distinction the title trades on. The paper is honest about most of this in Section 7 and Appendix A, and I want to be clear that the honesty is why I can be this specific. It is also why the gap between what the abstract says and what the appendix concedes is the thing the authors should fix first: run the four missing controls (low-d refit, logistic-direction ablation, footprint-matched rank-1 fitted control, own-versus-other discriminant probe), report the random rank-5 arm, and this becomes a paper I would argue for.

## Recalibration — IAB @ NeurIPS 2026

**1. Original (ICLR main track):** 4/10, confidence 4/5, **reject**.

**2. Recalibrated (IAB workshop, non-archival):** **7/10, confidence 4/5, accept.**

**3. Justification.** Two of my three blocking objections were answered by running the experiments, not by rewording. The low-d control (§3) is now there and it partly disarms the authors' own alarm — in 100 dimensions the fits stop separating and logistic reproducibility rises to 0.90 — and they report that plainly, alongside held-out accuracy showing the logistic row *decodes better* (0.94 vs 0.88) with projections correlating 0.90–0.96. That converts an overclaimed pathology into a correctly scoped one: fine classifier, poor direction, in the n < d regime that probe pools actually live in. Table 2 now puts every arm against baseline on one scale with one FDR standard, including the random 5-frame (inert at 5× footprint) I said was missing. And §4 concedes that the other-speaker projection rises comparably (+59/+119/+65/+93/+96 against +84/+212/+48/+98/+196) and withdraws the "its own state" claim outright. Retracting your headline construct because a non-circular probe killed it is the behaviour the field needs and rarely gets. At a non-archival venue that explicitly invites negative and preliminary results, this is squarely in scope and above the bar; at ICLR it still would not be, because the remaining positive claim is thin.

**4. Condition.** Retitle. "Affective transfer between LLM agents" asserts in the title what §4 now withdraws in the text; the framing must match the concession.
