# Reviewer 3 (LLM agents / multi-agent interaction; IAB workshop reviewer)

Note on venue (b): I fetched https://aiworkshoptracker.com/workshop/neurips-2026-iab/ and it returned only the title, the deadline (6 Sep 2026, extended), the location and a pointer to OpenReview; no scope text. The official site https://iab-agents.github.io/ returned only a page title. I therefore score against the usual bar for a NeurIPS workshop on interpreting LLM-agent behaviour: non-archival, values well-instrumented negative results and controls practitioners can adopt, and asks whether the submission actually interprets agent behaviour.

## 1. Summary

Agent A (Qwen3.6-27B) is activation-steered into one of six emotions at four doses and writes one message; agent B (the same model) replies, and a difference-of-means probe projection of B's reply activations rises with A's dose for five of six emotions, also on an abliterated Llama-3-8B. The paper's main technical finding is that logistic-regression probe directions do not reproduce across disjoint halves (0.57 / 0.29) because every fit is a perfect separator in the n < d regime, whereas difference-of-means does (0.97 / 0.94); projecting to 100 PCs restores reproducibility. Three pre-registered ablations on B's forward pass over A's tokens show that removing the rank-1 emotion direction at every readable layer leaves ~91% of the dose-response, that a rank-5 affect subspace breaks its own manipulation check, and that a rank-5 subspace fitted on permuted labels blocks three emotions while random directions and a footprint-matched random 23-frame are inert. Only sad's block (median 35% across ten direction re-splits) survives every control and replicates on the 8B. The authors concede that B's "other-speaker" projection rises as much as its "present-emotion" projection, so the probe cannot say whose state it reads, and they distil a rule: control an ablation with the same estimator on permuted labels at the same rank.

## 2. Strengths

1. Disjoint-half reproducibility of the direction is the right quantity and it is measured properly: ten splits, two models, three depths, a fixed per-sample penalty arm, tuned-C arm, and the direct diagnosis that training accuracy is 1.000 in every cell (Fig. 2). The 100-PC experiment (Sec. 3, "Is this just n < d") converts the mechanism into a prediction and confirms it on both models. This part is careful and useful.
2. The controls register is a real, adoptable finding: the norm-matched random direction, the random 5-frame and the footprint-matched random 23-frame are inert; the permuted-label rank-5 frame and the top principal direction bite (Table 2, Fig. 4). "A study with only the random control would have called B1d's blocks emotion-specific" (Sec. 6) is exactly the kind of sentence a practitioner needs.
3. Every arm against baseline sits in one table with one significance standard, with footprint and dose-0 readout shift reported per arm (Table 3). Common random numbers across arms and scenario-blocked, dose-paired contrasts are the right design for this.
4. The paper reports against its own interest: an instrument failure (B1d), a null decision contrast (affect minus permuted, q = 0.051), a withdrawn claim about present vs other-speaker shift, and a gate-fragile afraid result across ten re-splits (Table 4).
5. Appendix B records deviations and defects rather than hiding them.

## 3. Weaknesses (ranked by severity)

1. **This is not a paper about agent behaviour; it is a paper about a probe readout, and the inter-agent channel is never tested.** Sec. 7: "Not tested: the token channel (three rewrite designs failed their own manipulation check), the behavioural reach". No transcript-level outcome on B is ever measured: not a judge rating of B's reply, not a lexicon score, not a task metric. The ablations (Sec. 5, "Projecting the difference-of-means emotion direction out of A's span at every hidden state 13-63 during B's prefill") act inside B's forward pass over A's tokens. That is a single-model amnesic-probing experiment: does B's downstream linear feature survive erasure of the same feature at the positions it attends to? The "two agents" are one model reading text produced by a steered copy of itself. Sec. 1's motivation ("Systems of several language-model agents ... talk to each other [3, 8]. If one of them is in an emotional state, does anything of that state reach the others?") is not answered by anything in the paper, because what reaches B is A's text and the text is untested. For a workshop on interpreting agent behaviour this is the central problem: nothing that B does is interpreted.

2. **The construct is unresolved and the paper's own control shows it.** Sec. 4, "What the probe measures": the other-speaker projection rises "+59, +119, +65, +93, +96 ... against present-probe slopes +84, +212, +48, +98, +196", and "we make no claim about which it is". Then the readout should not be called "B's present-emotion projection" in every table, figure and the title's "moves a conversational partner's activations" should not carry the implicature of affect transfer. The simplest reading of all six panels of Fig. 3 is that B encodes the emotional content of the message it just read, which is reading comprehension, not coupling. The paper never reports the cosine between the present-emotion and other-speaker probe directions at the readout layer; if it is high (likely, since both are fitted from the same six-by-six crossed pool with the same emotion words), the "comparable rise" is trivially expected and the two readouts are one readout. Sec. 2 gives counts for the probe pool but not how the labels were produced (instruction to the generator to "sound X"?), so the reader cannot judge what the probe detects.

3. **The manipulation check on A is itself an activation readout, so there is no evidence that A's behaviour changed.** Sec. 2: "A generation-side forced-choice readout of A's span is the manipulation check"; Sec. 7: "The A-span readout is forced-choice and shares a target with the subspace ablation." Nothing tells me whether A's message at dose 1.0 (steering at 1x activation RMS, layer 42) is a coherent, recognisably emotional message or a degraded one. "Degenerate and refusing outputs dropped" is stated for the estimator pool only. For an agents audience, "we steered A" without showing what A wrote is not a manipulation.

4. **Prior work on exactly this design is missing.** "Rank-1 direction is insufficient" (H1) is the standard result of amnesic probing and concept erasure (Elazar et al. 2021; Ravfogel et al. INLP/RLACE; Belrose et al. LEACE): a single linearly-decodable direction can be removed without removing the concept, because the model re-derives it. The permuted-label control is the ablation analogue of Hewitt and Liang's (2019) control tasks. Sec. 1 "Relation to prior work" covers only estimator stability. Without these, contributions 3 and 4 read as novel when they are re-discoveries in a new setting.

5. **The permuted rank-5 control is not shown to be "with no emotion information", and its mechanism is not identified.** Sec. 5 (B1d): the permuted frame has "a third of its span in common with the affect frame" and still "blocks afraid (23%), sad (27%) and angry (23%)". A class-mean subspace fitted on permuted labels is a noisy sample of the pool's high-variance directions; the obvious hypothesis is that removing high-variance directions of A's span at 10x footprint over 51 layers degrades B's processing generically. The paper does not report the overlap of the permuted frame with the pool's top PCs, nor what happens if the permuted frame is orthogonalised against the affect frame. Until it does, the practitioner lesson is "fitted controls bite" without saying why, and the abstract's "blocks three emotions as strongly as the affect subspace does" is contradicted by Table 2 (sad 0.27 vs 0.54; angry 0.23 vs 0.40).

6. **Ecological validity of the two-agent setup is thin.** One message, one reply, 110 tokens each, temperature 0.9, 29 scenarios that are never described (are they emotionally neutral? do they invite affect?), both agents the same checkpoint, and on the second model an abliterated Llama-3-8B whose refusal direction has itself been ablated from the residual stream. No multi-turn, no task, no tool use, nothing that the cited agent systems [3, 8] do. The choice of an abliterated model for a study of residual-stream direction geometry is not justified anywhere.

7. **Cross-model replication is weaker than the abstract and contributions imply.** Sec. 5, "Replication on the 8B": B1c's rule "returned instrument_failed", B1e on the 8B "adds no discriminating power", "afraid's readout sits below chance", steering is "half as strong and less specific", and "Only sad replicates across models on both outcomes". The contributions list nonetheless says "with replications of two designs on the second model". Separately, the 8B's logistic direction reproduces at 0.29 against 0.57 on the 27B although d is smaller (4096 vs 5120), which is the opposite of what "The mechanism is therefore the n < d regime itself" (Sec. 3) would predict for the magnitude; the gap is not discussed.

8. **Clarity for a non-statistician is poor.** The abstract carries roughly twenty-five numbers; prose is interrupted by registry identifiers (a2f.focus_logreg_fixed_lambda_flat) that an external reader cannot resolve; sentences such as "a dip at 600 of -0.021 that is significant in a paired test over the ten shared splits, 1 of 10 positive, and a partial recovery" (Sec. 3) communicate nothing. Fig. 3's per-arm bands overlap completely in all six panels and the caption then says "Between-arm separation is decided by the paired contrasts of Tables 1-2, not by the drawn bands", so the figure shows the wrong quantity; a paired-difference plot is what the design produces. Fig. 4's "CI-implied ranges (the contrast's confidence interval divided by the none-arm slope)" divide by a slope that is itself uncertain (happy +48 [+8, +88]), which is why happy's whiskers run off the axis. Two B1c windows (hs 13-42, hs 13-63) appear in Table 2 and Fig. 3 with different significance patterns (sad 0.33* vs 0.28 unadjusted; angry 0.12* vs 0.09) and the text does not say which is the pre-registered arm. The process narration ("Seven of our own interpretive sentences were withdrawn under blind critique and the reviews of the first version forced eighteen more corrections", Sec. 1) belongs in a repository, not a paper.

9. **"Pre-registered" is carrying more weight than the deviations allow.** Appendix B: pool of 1,615 items against the registered 2,160; FDR over five emotions rather than six; the pre-registration stamp is empty; the 30- vs 51-layer contrast was "declared in the driver, not in the pre-registration"; both 8B runs are "out-of-pre-registration"; the permuted direction's cosine "is not stamped in the result file". Each is small; together they mean the reader cannot take "as registered" at face value.

10. **The untestable emotion is where every arm bites, including a random one.** Calm is excluded by the pre-registered rule, correctly, but Table 2 shows the footprint-matched random 23-frame moving calm by (+0.69) with unadjusted CI excluding zero, and Appendix B reports it shifting calm by -30 [-55, -4] slope units. The abstract calls that frame "inert". A reader adopting the controls register should be told that the random frame is inert on the five testable emotions only.

## 4. Questions for the authors

1. What is the cosine between the present-emotion and other-speaker probe directions at the readout layer, per emotion? Over which of B's reply positions is the projection taken (mean, last token)?
2. How was the 6 x 6 probe pool generated and labelled? Were "present" and "interlocutor" emotions given by instruction to the generator, and was any human or judge validation of the labels done?
3. Is there any text-level evidence that A's message changes with dose (judge rating, lexicon, human), and is A's output at dose 1.0 coherent in all 29 scenarios?
4. What are the 29 scenarios? Do any of them invite emotional content on their own?
5. What is the overlap of the permuted-label rank-5 frame with the pool's top-5 and top-23 principal directions, and does its blocking survive orthogonalisation against the affect frame?
6. Why Llama-3-8B-Instruct-abliterated rather than the base Instruct model? Does the un-abliterated model reproduce the estimator and B1c results?
7. Why does the 8B logistic direction reproduce worse than the 27B when d is smaller? What besides n < d sets the magnitude?
8. Which B1c window (hs 13-42 or hs 13-63) is the pre-registered primary arm, and why does the narrower window block more?
9. Does anything in B's reply text change with dose or with the emotion-direction ablation? If the 9% or 35% block has no behavioural correlate, what should someone interpreting an agent do with it?
10. In the 100-PC experiment, is the comparison 0.90 vs 0.94 (standardised coordinates) matched to the full-width 0.57 vs 0.97 (raw)? The text says difference-of-means is 0.33 at n = 75 in standardised coordinates against 0.56 raw, so standardisation alone moves the numbers a lot.

## 5. Overclaims or inconsistencies

- Abstract: "a rank-5 subspace fitted by the same estimator on permuted labels blocks three emotions as strongly as the affect subspace does". Table 2: permuted 0.27 / 0.23 vs affect 0.54 / 0.40 for sad / angry. Same significance threshold, not same strength. Sec. 6 repeats "as strongly as the affect subspace blocks two of them".
- Sec. 1: "only sad loses a third in every one of ten direction draws"; Sec. 8: "the rank-1 direction carries a third of sad's response in every draw and on both models". Table 4: range [0.24, 0.56], and Appendix B: "sad's range starts at 0.07" for the draw-0 interval. "At least a quarter at the point estimate" is what the data say.
- Sec. 3: "The mechanism is therefore the n < d regime itself". The 8B, with smaller d, reproduces worse (0.29 vs 0.57); the regime is necessary, not the whole mechanism.
- Sec. 1, contributions: "with replications of two designs on the second model". Sec. 5: B1c's rule returned instrument_failed on the 8B; B1e on the 8B "adds no discriminating power".
- Sec. 1: "A transfer that changes the receiver's internal representation would be invisible in transcripts and visible only in activations". Transcripts were never examined; this is asserted, not shown, and the other-speaker result suggests the visible thing is the transcript's content.
- Abstract: "leaves a median 91% of the response, against a random-direction control or against baseline (83% with the untestable emotion included)". Sec. 5: with calm the medians are 85% (vs random) and 83% (vs baseline); the parenthetical is attached to both.
- Abstract: "a random 23-frame of the same footprint ... are inert". Table 2 and Appendix B: it moves calm by (+0.69) of calm's slope, unadjusted CI excluding zero. Inert on the testable family only.

## 6. Scores

**NeurIPS main track: 4/10, confidence 4/5.** Carefully instrumented, but two incremental contributions (a disjoint-half reproducibility note whose mechanism is already in Marks and Tegmark and Soudry et al., and a permuted-label control that is the ablation analogue of control tasks) are wrapped around a construct the authors themselves cannot pin down, with no behavioural outcome and a thin second-model replication.

**IAB workshop: 6/10, confidence 4/5, recommended format: poster.** The controls register, the every-arm-against-baseline table and the honest instrument failures are what a workshop on interpreting agents should want and practitioners can copy tomorrow; but the thing interpreted is a probe readout of a one-turn exchange between two copies of one model, not agent behaviour, so the fit is on method only and I would not spotlight it.

## 7. The single change that would most raise each score

- **Main track:** split it into one paper. Either the estimator paper, extended to a behavioural cost of the logistic direction (steer with logistic vs difference-of-means directions on several concepts and model families, with control-task and concept-erasure baselines), or the transfer paper with a probe that separates B's own state from B's model of A's (train present and interlocutor probes on a pool where the two labels are decorrelated, report their cosine) and a behavioural outcome on B.
- **Workshop:** add one behavioural readout on B's reply (judge or human rating of emotional tone, plus any downstream task metric) across doses and arms, so the audience sees what, if anything, the 9% and 35% blocks change in what the agent does; and compress the controls rule of Sec. 6 into a one-box checklist with the footprint, dose-0 shift and manipulation-check columns a practitioner has to fill in.
