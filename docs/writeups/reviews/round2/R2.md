# Reviewer 2

Reviewer profile: mechanistic interpretability; linear probes, steering vectors, activation addition, ablation controls.

Materials read: submission.txt (12 pp.), fig1-4.png and fig1-4.txt. Nothing else.

Workshop scope: the page at aiworkshoptracker.com/workshop/neurips-2026-iab/ returned only logistics (deadline 6 Sep 2026 UTC, Sydney, OpenReview). One retry on the official site it points to (iab-agents.github.io) returned only a page title. I therefore score part (b) against the typical bar for a NeurIPS workshop on interpretability of LLM agents: non-archival, values well-instrumented negative results, methods and controls a practitioner can adopt, fit to "interpreting agent behavior".

---

## 1. Summary

Two copies of Qwen3.6-27B (and, as a weaker replication, an abliterated Llama-3-8B) converse; agent A is steered into one of six emotions at four doses, and a difference-of-means probe on B's reply activations rises with dose for five of the six. The paper first argues that the logistic-regression coefficient row is the wrong object for such work: across disjoint halves it reproduces at cosine 0.57 (27B) / 0.29 (8B) while difference-of-means reaches 0.97 / 0.94, because every fit in the n < d regime separates its training half perfectly and the row is decided by the penalty rather than by the data. It then runs three pre-registered ablations on A's span during B's prefill: removing the rank-1 emotion direction at all readable layers leaves a median 91% of the dose-response (only sad, ~35%, and less reliably afraid, ~18%, are partially blocked); a rank-5 class-mean subspace fails its own manipulation check; and a rank-5 subspace built from permuted labels blocks three emotions while a random direction, a random 5-frame, a footprint-matched random 23-frame and a permuted-label rank-1 direction are inert. From this the authors propose a rule: beside the random direction, run the intervention's own estimator on permuted labels at the intervention's rank, and conclude that "the fitted control bites because it is fitted, not because of what it removes". They also concede that the readout does not distinguish B's own state from B's representation of A's.

## 2. Strengths

1. Disjoint-half reproducibility of the fitted direction (Figure 1) is the right quantity to report, and the paper is candid that overlapping-subsample robustness (Gao et al.) shares most of the data and so inflates agreement. Reporting it per depth and per n on two models is useful.
2. The fixed per-sample penalty arm (C_n = 0.5 N_ref / n, Figure 2) pre-empts the obvious objection to Figure 1, and the training-accuracy = 1.000 measurement in every cell is the right diagnostic to print.
3. The projection-correlation bound (0.90-0.96 held-out, Section 3) is an honest and important caveat that most "probe directions are unstable" papers omit: the instability lives largely in directions the data do not occupy.
4. Table 2 is the most useful object in the paper: every hooked arm against the same baseline with the same significance rule, including the two random controls the argument depends on. Few ablation papers do this.
5. Pre-registration with verdicts reported as registered, a manipulation check on the sender, common-random-number sampling across arms, and scenario-blocked inference are all above the field's usual standard. Appendix B's list of recorded defects is unusual and welcome.
6. The ten direction re-splits (Table 4) are the right response to a single-pool study; sad's block surviving all ten draws against both baseline and the permuted control is the one result in the paper I would bet on.
7. The other-speaker probe check (Section 4, "What the probe measures") is a genuine negative result about construct validity, stated without spin.

## 3. Weaknesses (ranked by severity)

**W1. The headline methodological claim, "the fitted control bites because it is fitted, not because of what it removes" (abstract; Section 6: "the control must be fitted by the intervention's own estimator"), is not licensed by the evidence, and the paper's own tables point at a different, better-defined variable: alignment with the pool's activation covariance.**
A rank-5 class-mean frame built on permuted labels is, statistically, a draw from the sampling distribution of the class-mean noise, i.e. a random frame shaped by the data covariance Sigma-hat/n_class. It will therefore concentrate in the top principal directions. Everything in Tables 2-3 is consistent with "removes high-variance directions" and inconsistent with "fitted" being the operative property: (i) the permuted frame has 10x the emotion direction's footprint and "a third of its span in common with the affect frame" (Section 5, B1d), an overlap that would be ~0.1% for two independent random rank-5 frames in d = 5120, so both frames sit in a very low-dimensional high-variance subspace; (ii) the top principal direction, which is not fitted to anything, also blocks (angry 61%); (iii) the "footprint-matched" random 23-frame matches the average norm removed but spreads it over 23 isotropic directions, which is a completely different perturbation from removing five high-variance directions, so it does not test the alternative it is presented as ruling out; (iv) the arms that block are exactly the arms "estimated from the pool's activations" (Section 5, last paragraph), which is a description of covariance alignment, not of fitting. The discriminating control, a random rank-5 frame drawn from N(0, Sigma-hat) or a random rotation inside the top-k PC subspace, was not run. Until it is, "fitted" is not a well-defined property (fitted to permuted labels means fitted to nothing) and the rule in Section 6 tells a practitioner to run the wrong test.

**W2. The rank-5 permuted-label control is a single permutation draw.** Section 5 (B1d) and Table 2 report one permuted-label subspace. A permuted-label control is a null-distribution device; one draw is one sample from that null. The claim that it "blocks afraid (23%), sad (27%) and angry (23%) at q = 0.001, 0.035 and 0.013" is a statement about that draw. The paper's own B1e re-splits show the rank-1 permuted direction reaching significance in one of twenty emotion-draws (Appendix B), so draw-to-draw variation is not hypothetical. Without ~20 permutations the reader cannot tell whether "the permuted frame blocks three emotions" is a property of the estimator or of split seed 0. Note the asymmetry: the ten re-splits were spent on the rank-1 case, where the control is inert and nothing turns on it.

**W3. Rank and metric are confounded across the rank-1 and rank-5 arms, and the paper reads the difference as rank plus footprint.** Section 3 defines the rank-1 direction as "(mu+ - mu-)/sigma^2", a diagonally whitened direction that up-weights low-variance coordinates; its footprint (0.46-0.66, Table 3) is half that of a unit random direction (1.15-1.26), which is what one expects of a direction concentrated in low-variance coordinates. The rank-5 "class-mean affect subspace (16-21% of the pool's variance)" is evidently the span of raw centered class means, i.e. a top-PC-like frame with 10.5x footprint. Section 5 says of the two permuted controls: "they differ in rank and in footprint (10x against 0.85x)". They also differ in metric, which is the simplest explanation of both the footprint gap and the inert/bites split, and which is nowhere stated. A rank-1 raw mean-difference ablation and a rank-5 whitened frame would separate the two.

**W4. "Blocks" is never distinguished from "damages".** The arms that block have footprints 5.8-10.5x the emotion direction's and shift the dose-0 readout by up to +0.55 (top PC) and +0.39/-0.25 (affect subspace), Table 3. No measure of B's reply under these arms is reported: perplexity under the unablated model, length, refusal rate, off-target probe drift, anything. Projecting the top principal direction out of every position at 51 layers is a heavy intervention, and a reduced dose-response slope under it is what one would expect if B simply processes A's message less coherently. For the rank-1 emotion direction (footprint 0.5) damage is implausible; for every arm that "bites" it is the first alternative to exclude, and it is not excluded.

**W5. The primary readout shares its target with the ablation, so "carries" (Conclusion: "the rank-1 direction carries a third of sad's response in every draw and on both models") is licensed only for what that readout measures.** The ablated direction on A's span and the readout direction on B's reply are the same estimator, same layers, disjoint halves, split-half cosine 0.88-0.93 (Section 2). Attention heads that copy or average context residuals give a direct linear path from "v removed from A's positions at layer L" to "less v at B's positions at layer L+", with no emotion processing involved. The paper raises exactly this shared-target concern for the B1d instrument check but not for its own outcome measure. The one thing that would help, "the secondary readout on B's reply" (Section 5, B1e; Appendix B), is never defined in the main text, and it disagrees for afraid.

**W6. Rank-1 insufficiency is presented as evidence against "the linear account of the channel" (abstract), but it is the expected outcome of this design.** The ablation touches A's residual stream at layers 13-63 of B's forward pass; A's tokens, and layers 0-12, are untouched, so every emotion-laden word is still there to be re-read by non-linear features and later layers. Self-repair under ablation is a documented phenomenon (the hydra-effect literature is not cited), and single-direction ablations are known to under-estimate causal importance for this reason. There is also no positive control: replacing A's message with the same scenario's dose-0 message would calibrate what a 100% block looks like on this readout. Without it, "leaves a median 91%" has no scale.

**W7. The estimator finding is a known mechanism re-measured, and the paper's stated reason to care about it is never tested.** That penalised logistic regression on separable data tracks the max-margin separator (Soudry et al.; Rosset, Zhu and Hastie 2004 on the regularisation path), that the max-margin direction is determined by a few extreme points and hence high-variance in d >> n, and that the class-mean difference is the stable alternative (Marks and Tegmark) are all established. The paper says as much in "Relation to prior work". Its claimed increment is the disjoint-half quantity, but it does not run the comparison that would establish the increment, namely Gao et al.'s 80%-overlap pairwise robustness on this pool alongside the disjoint-half number, so "0.61 against 0.87-0.98" remains an explanation rather than a measurement; and its "tuned C" is cross-validated for accuracy (Appendix A), not for stability as RAPTOR does, so the comparison is not like-for-like. More seriously, the whole reason the paper gives for the reader to care, "the direction has to be trusted twice, once to read and once to intervene" (Section 1), is left untested: Section 7 lists "an ablation rerun with the logistic direction" under "Not tested". Since held-out projections correlate at 0.90-0.96, the reading cost is small by the paper's own bound, and the intervention cost is unmeasured. The two halves of the paper are joined by an assertion.

**W8. The n < d framing and the "penalty's tie-break" sentence are tidier than the evidence.** (a) Appendix B: "The weak-penalty arms (C = 50 and 500) give the same solution to three decimals, so they are tolerance-limited". Then the direction at those C is where lbfgs stopped, not the penalised optimum, and the Section 3 statement "the direction is the penalty's tie-break among separating hyperplanes" is not separated from "the direction is the optimiser's stopping point on a path that starts at the mean difference" (gradient at zero initialisation is proportional to the class-mean difference, which is why cos(logistic, DoM) starts near 1 in Figure 2). The distinction matters to a practitioner: one fix is the penalty, the other is the estimator. (b) "The mechanism is therefore the n < d regime itself" (Section 3) conflates a sufficient condition for separability with the mechanism. The 100-PC experiment shows training accuracy 0.999 at n = 600 with n/d' = 6, i.e. still separating for all practical purposes, so the transition is gradual and governed by the margin, not by the sign of n - d. (c) The 100-PC comparison also changes the coordinate convention (standardised), which the paper flags, but it means the "0.91-0.94 across n instead of falling from 0.90 to 0.62" comparison is cross-convention.

**W9. Power, multiplicity and the second model.** 29 scenarios; several decisions sit on the threshold (sad vs random q = 0.048; affect minus permuted q = 0.051); multiplicity is controlled within arm only, over roughly nine arms and two contrast families. The 8B model is an abliterated Llama-3-8B, itself the product of orthogonalising a direction out of the residual stream, which is not discussed anywhere as a possible interaction; its steering is "half as strong and less specific", "afraid's readout sits below chance", and B1c's manipulation check returned instrument_failed there. The abstract's "on Llama-3-8B" does not say any of this.

**W10. Clarity.** Section 5 is a wall of numbers with registry identifiers inline; the definitions a reader needs to evaluate W1 and W3 (what exactly the affect subspace is, whether the rank-1 direction is whitened, what "readable affect" and "the secondary readout" are) are missing or implicit. Table 1 and Table 2 report different contrasts for the same arms and it takes three readings to see which is which.

## 4. Questions for the authors

1. Is the rank-1 ablation direction (mu+ - mu-)/sigma^2 (whitened) and the rank-5 affect subspace the span of raw centred class means? If so, what do a rank-1 raw mean-difference ablation and a rank-5 whitened frame do?
2. How many permuted-label draws underlie the B1d rank-5 control? If one, what is the distribution of blocked fractions over 20 permutations, and where does the affect frame fall in it?
3. What fraction of each frame's span (affect, permuted, random 5, random 23, PC1) lies in the top-10 PCs of A's span activations? Does a random rank-5 frame drawn inside the top-20 PC subspace block?
4. Do B's replies stay coherent under the affect subspace, the permuted frame and PC1 arms (perplexity under the unablated model, length, refusal rate, drift of an unrelated probe)?
5. What is the "secondary readout on B's reply"? Is it independent of the ablated direction (a text classifier, or a probe fitted on a disjoint pool)?
6. What block does replacing A's message with the same scenario's dose-0 message give? That is the scale on which "median 91% remains" should be read.
7. Did you compute Gao et al.'s 80%-overlap pairwise robustness on this pool, and RAPTOR's stability-tuned ridge, so the 0.61 vs 0.87-0.98 comparison is like-for-like?
8. The logistic fits are six-way multinomial. Is the split-half cosine also low for one-vs-rest binary fits, and for the gauge-free pairwise differences w_k - w_j?
9. Is the irreproducible component of the logistic direction concentrated in low-variance PCs (the 0.90-0.96 projection correlation suggests so)? If so, what does the untested logistic-direction ablation do, and does it differ from DoM where the paper claims it should?
10. Why an abliterated 8B, and what is the cosine between the abliteration direction and each emotion direction at layer 21?

## 5. Overclaims or inconsistencies

1. Abstract: "a rank-5 subspace fitted by the same estimator on permuted labels blocks three emotions as strongly as the affect subspace does". Table 2: affect subspace sad +0.54, angry +0.40; permuted subspace sad +0.27, angry +0.23; for afraid the permuted frame (+0.23) blocks more than the affect frame (+0.11). The pre-registered contrast affect minus permuted has q = 0.051. "As strongly" is not what the table shows. Section 6's "as strongly as the affect subspace blocks two of them" is the same claim in a more ambiguous form.
2. Section 5 (B1d): "a permuted-label subspace built by the same estimator, with no emotion information and a third of its span in common with the affect frame". Table 3 gives its "readable affect removed" as 0.07-0.52. A frame that shares a third of its span with the affect frame and removes up to half of the readable affect for some emotion is not one "with no emotion information". Appendix B's "removed only 6.5% of afraid's readable affect" covers afraid only.
3. Abstract: "the fitted control bites because it is fitted, not because of what it removes". Not licensed; see W1. The 23-frame rules out average norm as the explanation, not spectral location.
4. Abstract: "The logistic-regression direction most work uses to read and write such states". For writing, the cited steering work uses mean differences (CAA [10]) or PCA of differences (RepE [14]), and ITI [7] chose mass-mean over the probe row. Logistic rows are common for reading, not for writing.
5. Section 3: "The mechanism is therefore the n < d regime itself", against the same section's "training accuracy 0.999, 0.989, 0.974 at n = 600, 1200, 2000" in 100 dimensions and the abstract's "the fits stop separating": at n = 600 in 100 dimensions they have not stopped separating in any practical sense.
6. Section 3: "the direction is the penalty's tie-break among separating hyperplanes", against Appendix B: the weak-penalty arms are "tolerance-limited and bound nothing about the C -> infinity limit". The paper cannot distinguish penalty from optimiser path with the arms it ran.
7. Section 1: "only sad loses a third in every one of ten direction draws". Table 4: emotion minus none for sad ranges [0.23, 0.44]; the lowest draw loses under a quarter.
8. Abstract: "the receiver's 'other-speaker' projection rises with dose as much as its own". Section 4 slopes: other +59/+119/+65/+93/+96 against present +84/+212/+48/+98/+196. For afraid and angry the other-speaker slope is about half; for happy it is larger. "Comparably" (Section 7) is defensible; "as much as" is not.
9. Abstract "on Llama-3-8B" vs Appendix A "Llama-3-8B-Instruct-abliterated", with the 8B caveats confined to Appendix B.
10. Contributions: "survival of the rank-1 block against a permuted-label rank-1 control (H3)", stated without the qualifier that in the re-splits afraid's verdict is H3 in four draws and instrument_failed in six (Section 5, B1e).

## 6. Scores

**NeurIPS 2026 main track: 4/10, confidence 4/5.** Carefully instrumented and unusually honest, but the estimator result is a known mechanism re-measured whose intervention cost is never tested, and the one new methodological claim (the fitted control) rests on a single permutation draw with an unaddressed covariance confound and no damage check.

**IAB workshop: 6/10, confidence 3/5 (scope page unavailable). Recommended format: poster.** A well-instrumented, pre-registered negative result on a multi-agent interpretability question, with a controls table practitioners can copy, is what such a workshop exists for; the recipe's rationale is not yet pinned down, so poster rather than spotlight.

## 7. The single change that would most raise each score

**NeurIPS main track:** replace the single B1d permuted-label draw with a null distribution (about 20 permutation draws of the rank-5 frame) and add one covariance-matched random arm (a random rank-5 frame drawn from N(0, Sigma-hat), or a random rotation inside the top-20 PC subspace), each with a coherence measure on B's reply. If the covariance-matched random frame blocks like the permuted frame, drop "because it is fitted" and restate the rule as "match the control to the activation spectrum, and report reply coherence"; if it does not, the fitted-control claim becomes an actual finding and the paper has a contribution the main track would want. By Appendix A this is two or three GPU-runs of about two hours each.

**IAB workshop:** the same experiment would move this to a spotlight. If only one thing is affordable before the deadline, run the permutation null for B1d and report the affect frame's blocked fractions as percentiles of it, so that the "controls register" in Section 6 is a permutation test rather than one extra arm.
