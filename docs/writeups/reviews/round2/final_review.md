## 1. Summary

The paper steers agent A into one of six emotions at four doses and shows that a difference-of-means probe on agent B's reply activations rises with dose for five of six emotions on Qwen3.6-27B and on Llama-3-8B-Instruct-abliterated, with scenario-blocked bootstrap intervals and common random numbers across arms. It then shows that the logistic-regression probe direction used by most probe studies does not reproduce across disjoint halves (0.57 / 0.29) while the standardised mean-difference direction does (0.97 / 0.94), and traces this to perfect separation in the n < d regime, with a PCA-100 refit as the regime test. Three pre-registered ablations of A's span during B's prefill find that projecting out the rank-1 emotion direction at every readable layer removes a median 9% of the dose-response, that a rank-5 affect subspace fails its instrument check, and that a rank-5 frame fitted on permuted labels blocks three emotions where a norm-matched random direction, a random 5-frame, a footprint-matched random 23-frame and a permuted-label rank-1 direction are all inert. Ten direction re-splits and a five-permutation null narrow the affect-specific claim to sad (block 24–56% across draws, replicating on the 8B), with afraid's manipulation gate passing in only four of ten draws. The paper concludes with a procedural rule for ablation controls and concedes that the probe cannot separate B's own state from B's model of A's, that the token channel is untested, and that whether label-fitting adds anything beyond covariance shaping is untested.

## 2. Strengths

1. **Pre-registration with verdicts reported as registered**, including an instrument failure (B1d, `instrument_failed`) and a rank-1 insufficiency verdict (H1) that go against the linear story the study set out to test. Post-review runs were pre-registered as addenda before launch (Appendix A).
2. **One table, one standard.** Table 2 puts every hooked arm against the same baseline with the same BH family, Table 5 gives the slope units and CIs behind it, and Table 3 gives rank, footprint, dose-0 readout shift and readable-affect removed per arm. A reader can recompute every blocked fraction, and I did for several.
3. **The estimator study is the cleanest section.** Disjoint halves rather than overlapping subsamples; three depths and two models; a fixed per-sample penalty schedule at two anchors; weak-penalty arms; training accuracy reported for every fit; held-out accuracy and projection correlation to bound the cost of the instability; and the PCA-100 refit as a direct regime test. Figure 2 is the paper's best figure: same-half cos(logistic, DoM) falling as split-half DoM rises is one picture of the mechanism.
4. **A controls register that goes beyond the random direction**: random 5-frame, footprint-matched random 23-frame (added specifically to rule out footprint), permuted-label rank-1 and rank-5 arms, and the top principal direction as a label-free high-variance comparator, with coherence checks (Table 6) so the blocking arms are shown not to have broken B's output.
5. **Direction resampling (Table 4) and a permutation null (Table 7)**, both read conservatively: the three-emotion pattern is explicitly downgraded to a single-realisation observation, and afraid's verdict split is attributed to the gate rather than to the contrast.
6. **Honesty against interest**: the other-speaker probe result (Section 4) is reported as bounding the construct claim; calm, which carries the largest point effects in Figure 3, Table 2 and Table 7, is excluded by the pre-registered rule rather than promoted; Appendix B lists defects (empty pre-registration stamp, pool size short of the registered count, five- versus six-emotion family, tolerance-limited weak-penalty arms).
7. **Reproducibility statement is concrete**: model revisions, library versions, GPU, per-arm-row timing, CRC32 seeding rule, bootstrap seed and draw count, grid of C values.

## 3. Weaknesses (severity-ranked)

**W1. The discriminating control for the paper's own rule is missing. [needs a run; mechanism]**
Location: Section 6, "Whether label-fitting adds anything beyond covariance shaping is untested (a random frame inside the pool's top principal subspace would tell)"; abstract, "the control that bites is the one built from the pool's activations, not the one that removes the most"; Section 6, "The rule is procedural: beside the random direction, run the same estimator on permuted labels at the intervention's rank".
Why it matters: a permuted-label class-mean frame is, to first order, five noise vectors drawn with the pool's covariance, i.e. a covariance-shaped random frame. The top-PC arm (rank 1, 5.8× footprint, blocks angry by 61%) already shows that variance captured, not label information, is what bites, and the inert 23-frame shows that norm is the wrong matching quantity. So the recommended control ("the same estimator on permuted labels") may be nothing more than "any frame inside A-span's high-variance subspace". One run of the paper's stated size (~2 h: a random rank-5 frame drawn inside the top-k principal subspace of A's span, k ≈ 20–50, none arm shared) would decide whether the rule is about the estimator or about covariance. Independently of the run, Table 3 should carry a column for the share of A-span variance each arm removes; the paper has this number for the affect subspace only ("16–21% of the pool's variance").

**W2. The permutation null is not used as a null. [needs a run, or a sentence; statistics]**
Location: Section 5 B1d, "four fresh label permutations of the same rank-5 control ... block angry in all four ... sad in three ... and all three together in only one"; Table 7; Section 5, "The pre-registered contrast, affect minus permuted subspace, clears no false-discovery threshold (q = 0.051)".
Why it matters: the null is used only to describe the control's variability. The question it could answer, whether the affect subspace's block exceeds what label-free frames of the same construction produce, is left at q = 0.051 against a single permutation. With five permutations the smallest attainable one-sided permutation p is 1/6, so the null as it stands cannot decide anything; yet the affect frame's sad block (0.54) and angry block (0.40) exceed every permuted draw (maxima 0.40 and 0.23), which is suggestive and unremarked. Twenty permutations at 72 arm-rows each (~25 min per permutation by Appendix A's timing, ~8 h) would give a usable null for sad and angry. If not run, Section 6 should say that Table 7 is descriptive and cannot re-decide B1d. Also: the fresh-permutation counts use unadjusted CIs while the paper's own frame (permutation 0) is BH-adjusted; the abstract counts them together without saying so.

**W3. The abstract overstates the permuted-frame comparison. [needs a sentence; honesty]**
Location: abstract, "blocks three emotions at the same false-discovery threshold as the affect subspace (at half to two-thirds of its size)".
Why it matters: Table 2 shows the affect subspace clears q < 0.05 on two emotions (sad 0.54, angry 0.40), the permuted frame on three; on afraid the control blocks 0.23 (q = 0.001) where the affect subspace blocks 0.11 (unadjusted only). "Half to two-thirds" holds on sad and angry only, as Section 6 correctly says ("on the two it also blocks"). The abstract drops the qualifier and thereby hides that the control out-blocks the treatment on one emotion, which is the more damaging fact for the linear account and the one a reader most needs.

**W4. No behavioural outcome at all, for a workshop on interpreting agent behaviour. [needs a run (analysis only); fit]**
Location: Introduction, "A transfer that changes the receiver's internal representation need not show in transcripts, which we do not examine here"; Section 7, "Not tested: the token channel (three rewrite designs failed their own manipulation check), the behavioural reach".
Why it matters: nothing in the paper says whether B's reply text changes with A's dose, or whether any ablation changes it. B's replies are stored for every arm-row (Table 6 computes word counts and perplexity on them), so a lexicon-based or judge-based affect rating of B's reply per arm-row is an analysis over existing outputs, not a generation run. Without it the 9% median block has no stated consequence for anything B does, and the "channel" question is answered only at the activation level. This is the single largest gap between the paper and the IAB audience.

**W5. "Ten draws" are ten direction draws under one noise realisation. [needs a sentence; statistics]**
Location: Section 5 B1e, "Sad's block is 35% (24–56%), significant in all ten"; Appendix A, "Generation seeds, doses, replicates and scenarios are unchanged, so each draw's none arm is a fresh sample of the same design".
Why it matters: the draws share generation seeds by design (common random numbers), so the "10/10" is robustness to the direction estimate, not ten independent replications of the receiver's response. That is still valuable, but the abstract's "holds in all ten draws" reads as replication. One clause fixes it.

**W6. Internal inconsistencies between a caption, a table and an appendix. [needs a sentence]**
(a) Figure 3 caption: "the ablation arms separate from baseline to significance for afraid and sad only among the testable emotions". Table 2, row "emotion direction (rank 1, hs 13–42)", marks angry +0.12∗, and Table 5 gives angry −23 [−44, −4] for that arm. The caption is wrong for the 13–42 window.
(b) Section 5 B1c: "sad (33%, q = 0.048)" against the random control. Appendix B: "five-emotion family on the same recomputation: 0.001, 0.037, –, 0.103" for what is described as the same contrast. Say which value is canonical and why the recomputation differs (bootstrap seed, p-value convention, or pool).
(c) Section 4: "The B1 follow-up run, with independently sampled replies, gives the same five testable emotions and afraid/sad as the blocked ones (b1f.*)" is garbled and names a run that appears nowhere else in the paper.

**W7. The estimator called "difference-of-means" is diagonal LDA, defined only in passing. [needs a sentence; cheap refit optional; method]**
Location: Section 3, "In these units difference-of-means is the raw-space direction (µ+ − µ−)/σ²; the plain mean difference µ+ − µ− that contrastive activation addition uses agrees with the logistic direction at 0.43".
Why it matters: every ablation direction and every reproducibility curve in the paper uses the per-coordinate standardised mean difference, and the six-class definition of µ− (rest-of-pool mean? pooled other-class mean?) is never given. The plain mean difference is a different estimator whose split-half curve is not shown; it is what most steering papers use, and its reproducibility is what Braun et al. are cited as corroborating. The σ² scaling is also exactly the "metric" leg of the rank/footprint/metric confound the paper leaves open in Section 5. Define the estimator once in Section 2; adding the plain-mean-difference curve to Figure 1 is a refit, not a generation run.

**W8. Two pieces of the mechanism argument are weaker than the prose. [needs a sentence; cheap refits optional; method]**
(a) Section 3, "Weakening the penalty a hundredfold or a thousandfold leaves the direction unchanged on the same half (cosine ≥ 0.96)" is offered as evidence for "the direction is the penalty's tie-break", but Appendix B says the C = 50 and C = 500 arms "give the same solution to three decimals, so they are tolerance-limited and bound nothing about the C → ∞ limit". The main text should carry that caveat where the claim is made.
(b) The PCA-100 experiment shows that reducing d restores reproducibility, which is consistent with "no longer separable" and equally with "fewer parameters, less variance for any estimator". The paper concedes "though not the whole of it". A sweep of d′ ∈ {100, 300, 1000, 2000} at n = 2000 (refits only) would show whether reproducibility falls off where training accuracy reaches 1.000, which is the actual claim.

**W9. The writing will lose a workshop reader. [needs a rewrite of wording; clarity]**
Location: the abstract (one ~360-word paragraph carrying ~40 numbers; sentence 8 exceeds 80 words); inline monospace registry identifiers throughout the prose (e.g. "a2f.focus_tuned_c_does_not_close_gap"); Contributions, "Seven of our own interpretive sentences were withdrawn under blind critique and the reviews of the first version forced eighteen more corrections; both lists are in the repository"; footnote 1, "after two rounds of adversarial review (three reviewers, a citation audit, a numerical audit and an area chair in each round; all in the repository)".
Why it matters: the abstract is a compressed results section, not an abstract; the registry identifiers belong in a footnote or an appendix map; and the footnote does not say the review rounds were internal, so it reads as though the paper had already been through peer review, which a program committee will not like. The title's "the controls register" is a coined noun phrase that no reader will parse on first sight. Little of this is AI-sounding (the voice is distinctive and terse); the problem is density, not filler.

**W10. The replication model is an odd choice, unexplained. [needs a sentence; design]**
Location: Section 2, "The second model is Llama-3-8B-Instruct-abliterated (revision dd67dd05); its runs are out-of-pre-registration replications with a weaker, less specific manipulation".
Why it matters: an abliterated model is itself the product of a rank-1 directional ablation of the kind the paper studies, its manipulation is half as strong, afraid's readout sits below chance (Appendix B), and both 8B runs are outside the pre-registration. Say why this model rather than the base instruct model, and what abliteration might do to a directional-ablation study.

**W11. Ratio-of-point-estimates fractions and family scope. [minor; statistics]**
Location: Section 7, "blocked fractions divide by the none-arm slope's point estimate"; Tables 1–2 and Figure 4 for happy.
Why it matters: happy's fraction −0.24 is an amplification of +11 [−2, +25] slope units on a slope of +48 [+8, +88]; showing it as a fraction adds noise. Multiplicity is controlled within an arm's five emotions but not across 9 arms × 5 emotions; sad's B1c q = 0.048 would not survive a broader family, though the ten-draw evidence for sad is the stronger one. Both are acknowledged; neither is fixed.

**W12. The other-speaker comparison is not in common units. [minor; construct]**
Location: Section 4, "slopes +59, +119, +65, +93, +96 ... against present-probe slopes +84, +212, +48, +98, +196"; abstract, "rises with dose comparably to its own".
Why it matters: the two readouts are projections on different probes with their own scales, so magnitudes are not comparable without normalisation; "comparably" is not licensed. "Both rise" is.

**W13. The largest effects sit on the excluded emotion. [minor; needs a sentence]**
Location: Section 4, "calm's (+44 [−3, +99]) does not, so calm is untestable"; Appendix B, "Re-running the decision contrast's BH adjustment over the pre-registered six-emotion family ... calm 0.011".
Why it matters: keeping the rule is right; but the main text should say in one clause that the excluded emotion carries the largest point effects in Figures 3–4 and Table 7, with the pointer to Appendix B, rather than leaving the reader to discover it from parentheses.

## 5. Overclaims or inconsistencies

1. Abstract: "blocks three emotions at the same false-discovery threshold as the affect subspace (at half to two-thirds of its size)". The affect subspace clears the threshold on two (Table 2); on afraid the control blocks more than the treatment. Section 6 has the qualifier; the abstract does not.
2. Figure 3 caption: "separate from baseline to significance for afraid and sad only" contradicts Table 2's angry +0.12∗ for the 13–42 window and Table 5's −23 [−44, −4].
3. Section 5 B1c "sad ... q = 0.048" versus Appendix B "five-emotion family on the same recomputation: ... 0.037" for the same contrast, unexplained.
4. Introduction "The reason is separability rather than sample size or penalty" versus Section 3 "regime-conditional (n < d) ... necessary for the instability, though not the whole of it". The instability is a function of sample size relative to width; the intro's phrasing denies what Section 3 asserts.
5. Section 3 "Weakening the penalty ... leaves the direction unchanged" presented as evidence, versus Appendix B "tolerance-limited and bound nothing about the C → ∞ limit".
6. Conclusion "carries 24–56% of sad's response across the ten draws and on both models": the range is the 27B emotion-minus-permuted range (Table 4); the 8B values (31%, 36%) happen to fall inside it but are not part of it.
7. Abstract "rises with dose comparably to its own": the two slopes are in different probes' units; only "both rise" is supported.
8. Abstract "sad's block ... holds in all ten draws" reads as replication; the draws share generation seeds (Appendix A).
9. Footnote 1 describes "three reviewers ... and an area chair in each round" without saying the rounds were internal; a reader will take this for peer review.
10. Abstract and Section 6 present "the control that bites is the one built from the pool's activations" as the finding, while Section 6 concedes the covariance-shaped alternative is untested; the abstract should carry that concession.

## 6. Scores

**NeurIPS main track: 4/10** (confidence 4/5). A careful, honest, largely negative methodological study whose estimator finding is a sharper measurement of a known effect (Marks and Tegmark; Soudry et al.; Gao et al.) and whose controls finding is missing the one run that would make it a rule rather than an observation; one probe pool, one main model, an abliterated replication model, no behavioural outcome.

**IAB workshop: 6/10** (confidence 3/5; the workshop page gave logistics only, so this is scored against the usual bar for a NeurIPS interpretability-of-agents workshop). **Format: poster.** Well above the workshop median in rigour and in honesty of reporting, on-topic in setting (two-agent steering, probes, ablations), but with no behavioural readout, an abstract that will lose most of the room, and a headline rule that rests on an untested distinction. Spotlight if W1 is run and the abstract is rewritten; the page count (9 main) should also be checked against the CFP limit, which I could not retrieve.

## 7. Would I be comfortable with this paper being presented as is?

Mostly, as a poster, and after the ten-minute fixes in Section 4 above; not with the abstract as it stands. The paper's virtues are real and rare: pre-registered verdicts reported against interest, every arm on one scale with slope units and intervals behind it, a defect list, and a construct-validity result the authors clearly did not want. What I would not want a room to take away is the two things the abstract currently invites: that a permuted-label control is "the fix" for ablation studies (the paper's own top-PC and 23-frame arms point instead to any covariance-shaped frame biting, and the discriminating run has not been done), and that the rank-5 permuted frame merely matches the affect subspace "at half to two-thirds of its size" when on afraid it out-blocks it. With fixes 2, 8, 13, 15 and 16 applied the paper says what its tables say; with W1 run it would have a rule I would repeat to others. The presentation itself needs a plain-language first slide that the current abstract cannot supply, and a behavioural sentence, even a negative one, for an audience whose workshop is named for behaviour.
