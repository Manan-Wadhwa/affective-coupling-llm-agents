# Reviewer 1 (statistics: experimental design and inference)

Materials: submission.txt (12 pp.), Figures 1-4 with their data captions. Nothing else.

## 1. Summary

Two LLM agents converse; A is steered toward an emotion by an activation vector at layer 42 and B's reply activations are projected onto a difference-of-means emotion probe. The projection rises with dose for five of six emotions on Qwen3.6-27B (Fig. 3) and, per text only, on Llama-3-8B. An estimator study shows the logistic-regression probe direction reproduces across disjoint halves at 0.57/0.29 (27B/8B) while difference-of-means reaches 0.97/0.94, because in the n<d regime every logistic fit is a perfect separator; the two estimators' held-out projections nonetheless correlate at 0.90-0.96. Three pre-registered ablations on A's span find that projecting out the rank-1 emotion direction leaves about 91% of the response (median over five testable emotions), that a rank-5 affect subspace voids its own instrument check, and that a rank-5 subspace fitted by the same estimator on permuted labels blocks three emotions while norm-matched random directions and frames are inert. Ten re-splits of the probe pool show the rank-1 block of sad (median 35%) is stable across direction draws and afraid's (median 18%) is not, with afraid's manipulation gate passing in 4/10 draws.

## 2. Strengths

1. Scenario-blocked, dose-paired percentile bootstrap with common random numbers across arms (Sec. 2) is the right inferential unit for 29 fixed scenarios, and the 29-cluster limitation is stated (Sec. 7).
2. Table 2 puts every hooked arm against the same baseline under one significance standard. The arithmetic between Tables 1 and 2 checks to rounding for every cell I tried (e.g. B1d sad: 0.54 - 0.27 = 0.27 vs 0.26; B1c happy: -0.24 - (-0.28) = 0.04 vs 0.05).
3. Direction-draw variability was measured at all (Table 4) and the abstract reports its medians rather than the favourable Draw 0. Most probe-ablation papers report one draw and never look.
4. The estimator mechanism is checked directly rather than argued: training accuracy 1.000 in every cell, penalty weakened 1000x with no change in direction, and the 100-PC refit where n >> d' restores reproducibility (0.90). That is the correct falsification of "it is just the penalty".
5. Appendix B records the deviations from pre-registration (pool 1,615 vs registered 2,160; FDR over five not six; a driver-declared contrast; an empty prereg stamp) instead of hiding them.

## 3. Weaknesses (ranked by severity)

1. **The key control is one permutation, not a permutation test.** Sec. 5 B1d and Sec. 6: "a permuted-label subspace built by the same estimator ... blocks afraid (23%), sad (27%) and angry (23%)". This is a single realisation of a random control: one permutation seed, one rank-5 frame. The paper's central procedural recommendation (Sec. 6: "run the same estimator on permuted labels at the intervention's rank") and the abstract's claim that this control "blocks three emotions as strongly as the affect subspace does" rest on n = 1. Table 4 shows how much even the *rank-1* permuted direction varies across ten draws (afraid, permuted - none: -0.04 to +0.07); for the rank-5 frame, the one that actually bites and has 10x the footprint, no draw-to-draw spread exists. The same holds for the random direction, the random 5-frame and the random 23-frame: one realisation each. Without K >= 10 permutations and the null distribution of blocked fractions per emotion, the paper cannot say where the affect subspace's 0.54/0.40 sits relative to its permuted null, which is the only question a permutation control exists to answer.

2. **"As strongly" is contradicted by the paper's own Table 1.** Abstract: "blocks three emotions as strongly as the affect subspace does"; Intro: "blocks the response as strongly as the affect subspace itself"; Sec. 6: "as strongly as the affect subspace blocks two of them". Table 2: affect subspace blocks sad 0.54 and angry 0.40; the permuted frame 0.27 and 0.23, half. Table 1, the direct paired contrast affect - permuted: sad 0.26 and angry 0.17, both with unadjusted CIs excluding zero, family q = 0.051. The authors read q = 0.051 as equality. That is the "difference between significant and non-significant" error run in reverse, at a threshold missed by 0.001. The defensible statement is: the affect subspace blocks sad and angry about twice as much as the permuted frame, family-adjusted q = 0.051. The abstract misreports the paper's own table.

3. **Blocked fractions are ratios whose denominator uncertainty is discarded.** Fig. 4 caption: "Error bars are CI-implied ranges (the contrast's confidence interval divided by the none-arm slope)". The none-arm slope is itself a bootstrap quantity with a wide interval: happy +48 [+8, +88] (Fig. 3), a 6x range in the denominator, treated as a constant. Every fraction in Tables 1-2 and every median built from them ("median 9% ... [4%, 25%]") inherits this. The stars are decided on the slope-unit contrast, which is fine, but the fractions and the "CI-implied" whiskers understate uncertainty by an emotion-dependent and unreported factor. Happy's -0.24 (an anti-block) enters the "91%" headline with a denominator whose interval reaches +8; the number is essentially undefined. Report the contrast in slope units with its bootstrap CI, and the fraction, if at all, from a bootstrap that resamples numerator and denominator in the same scenario draw.

4. **The ten re-splits vary the direction but not the scenarios; "holds in all ten draws" is not ten replications.** Appendix A: "Generation seeds, doses, replicates and scenarios are unchanged, so each draw's none arm is a fresh sample of the same design". The 29 scenarios are the declared sampling unit (Sec. 2) and are identical in every draw; the draws also re-split one 1,615-item pool, so any two draws share about half their items per half. Appendix B applies exactly this caveat to the estimator study ("subsamples that overlap by about half the pool, so their intervals overstate precision") but not to Table 4. "Sig. neg. 10/10" counts ten positively correlated tests, and "range 24-56%" is a lower bound on the direction-draw spread. The abstract's "holds in all ten draws" should say what the draws vary and what they hold fixed.

5. **Table 4's threshold and family are unstated, and the follow-up family was chosen on Draw 0.** "Sig. neg. 9/10": unadjusted, or BH within a family of two? The addendum restricts to afraid and sad, the two emotions that carried stars in Draw 0. Meanwhile the identical emotion - none contrast for sad is q = 0.115 in B1c's five-emotion family (Sec. 5 B1c, "sad does not") and "10/10 significant" in Table 4. The same numbers get opposite verdicts depending on which experiment's family they are booked in. State the rule and report Table 4 under the five-emotion family too.

6. **The main text softens Table 4.** Sec. 5 B1e: the re-splits "put that draw at the top of afraid's spread". Table 4: Draw 0 = 0.25; range of draws 1-10 = [0.07, 0.23]. Draw 0 is outside the range, the most favourable of eleven draws, and it is the number carrying the star in Table 1 and the "verdict H3". Intro: "only sad loses a third in every one of ten direction draws"; Conclusion: "carries a third of sad's response in every draw": the minimum is 24%. Intro: "afraid a fifth in the draws where its own manipulation check passes": no table gives a statistic over the four passing draws. Table 1 should carry the across-draw median or footnote that afraid's 0.25 is the maximum of eleven.

7. **The FDR family deviates from pre-registration in the direction that rescues a borderline result.** Appendix B: "the false-discovery rate ran over five emotions rather than six." B1c's sad vs random control is q = 0.048 (Sec. 5 B1c). Under the registered family of six, q >= 0.048 x 6/5 = 0.058 unless calm's p re-ranks sad. The testability rule is pre-registered, but so was the six-emotion family; the paper reports the combination under which sad survives and not the other. Give the six-family q-values.

8. **The thesis that the direction "fails twice" is tested once.** Intro: "the direction has to be trusted twice, once to read and once to intervene, and that both uses fail". Every ablation uses the difference-of-means direction; Sec. 7: "Not tested: ... an ablation rerun with the logistic direction." For reading, the paper's own bound ("projections of the same held-out items correlate at 0.96 to 0.90") says the practical cost of the logistic direction is small. So the estimator study establishes a real geometric fact with, on the paper's evidence, a small downstream cost, and the intervention failure is shown for the good estimator only. The abstract's "the standard linear tools mislead in ways their usual checks do not catch" is stronger than what was measured.

9. **Norm is the wrong matching moment, so "fitted vs not fitted" is under-determined.** Abstract: "the fitted control bites because it is fitted, not because of what it removes". A permuted-label class-mean frame is a random frame whose directions are distributed with covariance proportional to the pool's activation covariance Sigma; it preferentially removes high-variance residual-stream directions, as does the top principal direction, which also bites (Table 2: angry 0.61*). The isotropic random 23-frame matches removed norm (Table 3: 6.7-6.9 vs 6.5-6.9) but not the spectrum of what is removed. A rank-5 random frame drawn from N(0, Sigma) is the control that separates "fitted to labels" from "aligned with high-variance directions"; without it, the simpler explanation (non-specific disruption of A's span) is not excluded and the rule in Sec. 6 may be recommending the wrong control.

10. **The manipulation gate is applied asymmetrically across models.** On the 27B a failed gate voids the verdict (six of ten afraid draws become instrument_failed). On the 8B, B1c's gate "returned instrument_failed because the upper window changes the readout by nothing measurable there and the strict criterion has no tolerance band" (Sec. 5), and the run is nonetheless cited in the abstract as "five of six emotions on ... Llama-3-8B", with no table or figure. Either the gate is the rule or it is not.

11. **Estimator-study inference over ten dependent splits supports fine claims it cannot bear.** Fig. 1: "Bands are bootstrap CIs over splits", ten re-splits of one pool. Harmless for the 0.57-vs-0.97 gap; not for "a dip at 600 of -0.021 that is significant in a paired test over the ten shared splits, 1 of 10 positive" (Sec. 3). A -0.021 dip tested on dependent splits is noise-chasing and should go.

12. **Two cosine conventions for one quantity.** Abstract and Fig. 2: "cosine 0.97 to 0.63" (raw space); Sec. 3: "falling from 0.90 to 0.62 ... these are standardised-coordinate cosines". The reader is not told which to carry. Pick one for the abstract.

13. **Smaller numerical inconsistencies.** (a) Abstract: "median 91% ... against a random-direction control or against baseline (83% with the untestable emotion included)": with calm, the median vs random is 15% (85% left) and vs baseline 17% (83%); the parenthetical fits one of the two contrasts it is attached to. (b) Abstract: the other-speaker projection "rises with dose as much as its own"; Sec. 4: +119 vs +212 (afraid), +96 vs +196 (angry), about half, and in different probe units, so "as much" is not meaningful either way. (c) Table 2: the hs 13-42 arm carries stars on afraid, sad and angry against baseline; the hs 13-63 arm, the one the abstract describes ("at every readable layer"), carries only afraid's. The text says extending to 43-63 "changes no dose-response detectably". Then say why the narrower window has three stars and the wider one.

## 4. Questions for the authors

1. How many permuted-label rank-5 frames were fitted? If one: over 20 permutations, what is the distribution of blocked fractions per emotion, and where do the affect subspace's 0.54 (sad) and 0.40 (angry) fall in it?
2. Common random numbers: once two arms' logits differ, the sampled sequences diverge and the paired-noise cancellation is partial. In what fraction of the 29 x 4 x 3 cells do the none and ablation arms share more than the first token?
3. Are Table 4's "Sig. neg." counts unadjusted, or BH within a family of two? What are they under the five-emotion family used in Table 2?
4. What are the Table 1 q-values under the registered six-emotion family?
5. What is the split-half cosine of the plain mean difference mu+ - mu- (the CAA estimator of refs. [10, 14]) as opposed to (mu+ - mu-)/sigma^2? The abstract's "difference-of-means" is the whitened version, and the introduction's "most work uses" logistic is not what the cited steering work uses.
6. How are the two-sided bootstrap p-values computed from a percentile bootstrap with 29 clusters, and with 5,000 draws is "q = 0.001" a resolution floor?
7. Are the medians' bootstrap CIs ("[4%, 25%]") from resampling scenarios and recomputing numerator and denominator of each fraction in the same draw?
8. Figure 4 clips calm's and happy's whiskers at the axis. What are the actual ranges?

## 5. Overclaims or inconsistencies found

- Abstract, Intro, Sec. 6: "blocks three emotions as strongly as the affect subspace does" / "as strongly as the affect subspace itself". Table 1: affect - permuted = 0.26 (sad), 0.17 (angry), unadjusted CIs exclude zero, q = 0.051; Table 2: 0.54/0.40 vs 0.27/0.23.
- Sec. 5 B1e: "put that draw at the top of afraid's spread". Table 4: Draw 0 = 0.25 exceeds the ten-draw maximum 0.23.
- Intro: "only sad loses a third in every one of ten direction draws"; Conclusion: "carries a third of sad's response in every draw". Table 4: range starts at 0.24.
- Intro: "afraid a fifth in the draws where its own manipulation check passes". Not in any table.
- Abstract: the other-speaker projection "rises with dose as much as its own". Sec. 4: roughly half for afraid and angry, units not comparable.
- Abstract: "(83% with the untestable emotion included)" attached to both contrasts; it is 85% vs random and 83% vs baseline.
- Abstract: "five of six emotions on Qwen3.6-27B and on Llama-3-8B". The 8B run's pre-registered manipulation gate returned instrument_failed (Sec. 5); no table or figure supports the 8B claim.
- Abstract: "held-out projections correlate at 0.90-0.96" and the 100-PC "direction reproduces at 0.90". Text only; no table or figure.
- Intro: "The logistic-regression direction most work uses". The paper's own cited steering work [10, 14] uses mean difference and PCA.

## 6. Scores

**NeurIPS 2026 main track: 4/10, confidence 4/5.** An unusually honest, well-instrumented, mostly negative study on a narrow question; the estimator lesson is partly known (Marks and Tegmark; Soudry et al.), the new control is demonstrated with a single permutation, ratio statistics ignore denominator uncertainty, the follow-up family is post hoc, and the abstract contradicts Table 1.

**IAB workshop: 7/10, confidence 4/5. Recommended format: poster** (spotlight if the permuted-label control is run as a distribution and the "as strongly" language is corrected to what Table 1 shows). Workshop page: https://aiworkshoptracker.com/workshop/neurips-2026-iab/ returned logistics only (deadline, Sydney, OpenReview link); one retry on the linked official site (https://iab-agents.github.io/) returned the title only. Scored against the typical bar for a NeurIPS workshop on interpreting LLM-agent behaviour: non-archival, values well-instrumented negative results and controls practitioners can adopt. On that bar this fits squarely, and its inferential defects are fixable in a camera-ready.

## 7. The single change that would most raise each score

- **NeurIPS:** Replace the single permuted-label rank-5 frame with K >= 20 permutations plus a Sigma-matched random frame at the same rank; report the null distribution of blocked fractions per emotion and locate the affect subspace within it. That converts the paper's central procedural claim from an anecdote into a test and settles "fitted" against "high-variance".
- **IAB:** Rewrite the abstract and Sec. 6 so every comparative claim matches Tables 1, 2 and 4 (affect blocks about twice the permuted frame at q = 0.051; afraid's 0.25 is the maximum of eleven draws; sad's block ranges 24-56%), and report fractions with denominator-resampled intervals or in slope units.
