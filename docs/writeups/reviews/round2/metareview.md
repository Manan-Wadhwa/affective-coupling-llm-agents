## 1. My reading of the paper

Two copies of Qwen3.6-27B hold a one-turn exchange in 29 scenarios; A is steered into one of six emotions at four doses; a difference-of-means probe on B's reply activations rises with the dose for five of six emotions, and the same five respond on an abliterated Llama-3-8B. Around that observation the paper builds two arguments. The first is an estimator study: logistic probe directions reproduce across disjoint halves at 0.57 (27B) and 0.29 (8B) while difference-of-means reaches 0.97 and 0.94; every logistic fit separates its half perfectly, so the direction is the penalty's tie-break, and projecting to 100 principal components, where the fits stop separating, brings reproducibility back to 0.90. The mechanism is measured rather than asserted (training accuracy 1.000 in every cell; a thousandfold weaker penalty leaves the direction unchanged), and the paper is honest that the two estimators' held-out projections still correlate at 0.90-0.96, so the practical cost of the logistic direction for reading is small. The second is a set of three pre-registered ablations of A's span inside B's forward pass: projecting out the rank-1 emotion direction at all readable layers leaves about 91% of the dose-response; a rank-5 affect subspace shifts its own manipulation readout at dose 0 and voids its verdict; a rank-5 subspace fitted on permuted labels blocks three emotions at the FDR threshold while a random direction, a random 5-frame, a footprint-matched random 23-frame and a permuted-label rank-1 direction are inert. Ten re-splits show sad's rank-1 block (median 35%) is stable across direction draws and afraid's (18%) is gate-fragile. Finally the "other-speaker" probe rises with dose comparably to the "present-emotion" probe, so the readout cannot say whose state it reads.

What I take from it. The affect science is thin and the paper mostly admits it: once the two probes are conceded inseparable, the receiver phenomenon is B encoding the emotional content of the message it just read, which steering changed, and that is expected rather than a discovery about coupling. The rank-1 ablation leaves A's tokens and layers 0-12 untouched, so "insufficiency" is the outcome the concept-erasure literature predicts; the interesting residue is that sad's block survives every control on both models. The estimator result is a careful re-measurement of a known mechanism (Marks and Tegmark; Soudry et al.; Rosset et al.) whose new quantity, disjoint-half reproducibility, is worth having, but whose stated motivation, that the direction must be trusted for intervention too, is left untested (no logistic-direction ablation was run). The paper's most transferable content is the controls lesson: a label-free control built by the intervention's own estimator catches what norm-matched random controls miss, and Table 2, every arm against one baseline under one rule, is the object a practitioner should copy. But the lesson's stated mechanism ("because it is fitted") does not follow from the arms run: a permuted-label class-mean frame lives in the pool's high-variance directions, the top principal direction also blocks, and a norm-matched isotropic frame does not test that alternative. The paper is unusually honest, registered and self-critical; it is also dense, number-choked and, in its abstract, wrong on its own tables in three places. It reads as the notebook of a careful investigator rather than as a paper with one finding.

## 2. The reviews: agreement, divergence, rulings

The revision delivered both conditions from the previous round (the footprint-matched rank-23 frame, item 23; the ten direction draws, item 24), and both sharpened the paper: the draws produced Table 4, which is now the paper's most credible affect-specific evidence, and the rank-23 frame closed the removed-norm explanation. Item 23's closing sentence, that "the control has to be fitted by the intervention's own estimator", over-reads that result, as discussed under (a).

Agreement, which I share: the honesty, the pre-registration, Table 2 and Table 4 are real strengths; the rank-5 permuted-label control is a single draw; "as strongly as the affect subspace" contradicts Tables 1-2; "a third of sad's response in every draw" overstates a 24-56% range; "as much as its own" overstates the other-speaker slopes; the 8B replication is weaker than the abstract implies; the estimator mechanism is known and the "trusted twice" thesis is tested once; the fitted-versus-high-variance confound is open; the prose is hard to read. All three score the main track 4/10 and recommend a workshop poster (7, 6, 6).

Disputed points, one paragraph each.

(a) What the permuted-label control shows. R2 (W1) argues that "fitted" is not a well-defined property (fitted to permuted labels is fitted to nothing) and that the operative variable is alignment with the pool's covariance; R1 (W9) says the same and names the discriminating arm, a random rank-5 frame drawn from N(0, Sigma-hat); R3 (W5) reads it as generic degradation of A's span at 10x footprint and asks for the frame's overlap with the top principal components and for orthogonalisation against the affect frame. None disputes that the control is useful; they dispute the mechanism and hence the rule. I side with R2 and R1. The rank-23 frame rules out removed norm as the explanation and nothing more; the top-PC arm blocking angry at 5.8x footprint is direct evidence for the covariance reading, and "a third of its span in common with the affect frame" is what covariance alignment predicts and what two independent random frames in 5,120 dimensions would not show. The recipe may still be right in practice, because a permuted-label frame is a cheap covariance-shaped null, but it is right for a reason the paper has not identified, and Section 6's "the control must be fitted by the intervention's own estimator" can mislead a reader into thinking a spectrum-matched random frame would not do. The mechanism claim must be tested or withdrawn (condition 2). R3's degradation reading is a special case of the same alternative and is addressed by the coherence measure (condition 9).

(b) Whether the paper interprets agent behaviour. R3 (W1) holds that nothing B does is interpreted, that the "two agents" are one model reading its own steered text, and that the fit to the workshop is on method only; R1 and R2 treat the fit as square. I side with R3 on the substance and with R1 and R2 on the venue. The paper is a probe-readout study of a one-turn exchange with no transcript-level outcome, and the introduction's "invisible in transcripts and visible only in activations" is asserted, not shown; the other-speaker result suggests the visible thing is the transcript's content. A methods-friendly workshop on interpreting agents will still want the controls table and the reproducibility metric, so the fit is adequate for a poster; it is not adequate for a spotlight, whose audience would expect to learn something about what an agent did. The cheapest repair is a judge or lexicon rating of B's replies across doses (condition 12); failing that, the framing must retreat.

(c) Whether rank-1 insufficiency is a finding. R2 (W6) and R3 (W4) call it the expected outcome of amnesic probing and concept erasure, uncited, with no positive control to give "leaves 91%" a scale; R1 does not contest H1. I side with R2 and R3. The citation validator independently lists the same missing antecedents (Elazar et al.; Ravfogel et al.; Belrose et al.; Arditi et al.), which confirms that contribution 3 is presented as more novel than it is. The relative comparisons across arms survive; the absolute "leaves 91%" needs either a positive control or a sentence conceding that this design cannot produce a full block (condition 11 and the recommendations).

(d) Ratio statistics. Only R1 (W3) raises that every blocked fraction divides by a bootstrap slope whose interval is ignored (happy +48 [+8, +88]), so the fractions and the "CI-implied" whiskers of Figure 4 understate uncertainty by an unreported factor; R3 notes it as a clarity issue and R2 does not raise it. I side with R1. It is a real inferential defect, contained because the stars are decided on slope units, but the medians ("9% [4%, 25%]") and the headline "91%" inherit it. Slope-unit contrasts with their CIs should be the primary report, and fractions, if kept, must come from a joint bootstrap (condition 4).

(e) What the ten re-splits establish. R1 (W4-W6) points out that the draws vary the direction but not the scenarios, that any two halves share about half the pool, that Table 4's rule and family are unstated and the follow-up family was chosen on Draw 0, and that the main text softens Table 4 (Draw 0 = 0.25 sits above the ten-draw maximum of 0.23, not "at the top of the spread"); R2 (S6) calls sad's survival the one result in the paper to bet on; R3 accepts the table. Each is right about its own claim, and I side with each: sad's block is the paper's most credible affect-specific finding, and "holds in all ten draws" must say what the draws vary and what they hold fixed, with the rule and the five-emotion-family q-values printed (condition 5). The restriction to afraid and sad is a post hoc choice and should be labelled as one.

(f) FDR family and gate asymmetry. R1 alone (W7, W10) notes that the five-emotion family rescues sad-versus-random (q = 0.048; about 0.058 under the registered six) and that the manipulation gate voids afraid on the 27B while the 8B run whose gate returned instrument_failed is cited in the abstract without qualification. I side with R1 on both. The testability rule is pre-registered, so the five-emotion family is defensible, but printing both q-values costs nothing (condition 6); the 8B gate outcome must appear wherever the 8B is cited as a replication (condition 8). R2 (W9) and R3 (W6) add that the second model is an abliterated checkpoint, which the abstract does not say; I agree.

(g) Block versus damage. R2 (W4) alone asks for a coherence measure of B's replies under the arms with 5.8-10.5x footprint over 51 layers. I side with R2. It is the first alternative to exclude for every arm that bites, it is computable from stored replies without new GPU runs, and its absence is why R3's degradation reading cannot be dismissed (condition 9).

(h) Rank and metric are confounded. R2 (W3) alone observes that the rank-1 direction is diagonally whitened, (mu+ - mu-)/sigma^2, while the rank-5 frame is the span of raw class means, so the two permuted controls differ in metric as well as in rank and footprint, and the paper's "they differ in rank and in footprint" omits the third variable. The text nowhere states the metric of each arm. I side with R2 that it must be stated (condition 10); running the crossed arms is recommended, not required.

(i) The estimator increment. R2 (W7) says the disjoint-half number remains an explanation rather than a measurement until Gao et al.'s 80%-overlap robustness is computed on this pool and the tuned C is stability-tuned as RAPTOR's is; R3 (S1) calls the estimator section careful and useful; R1 (Q5) asks for the split-half cosine of the plain mean difference, since the cited steering work uses that and not the logistic row the introduction attributes to "most work". I side with R3 on the value of the section and with R2 and R1 on the missing like-for-like numbers, which are CPU-only computations on existing activations. I list them as recommendations because the section's central claim stands without them; the "most work uses" sentence, however, is a misattribution and goes (condition 13).

## 3. Whether the citation and numbers checks change anything

Citations. All fourteen references are real. One is mischaracterised (Durupinar's agents do not converse, and a single preprint cannot show that multi-agent systems are "common"); one comparison is incomplete in a way that matters (Herbster et al. also report a divergent 0.80 case with an overlap mechanism, used the same Qwen3.6-27B, and trained at an n where this paper's own Figure 2 curve is still above 0.9, which turns a claimed disagreement into agreement with drift in n); one theorem is adjacent (Soudry's unregularised-GD result where Rosset, Zhu and Hastie's regularisation-path result fits an l2-penalised fit). Six missing citations are needed, and three of them bear on novelty: Hewitt and Liang's control tasks are the antecedent of the permuted-label rule, INLP/LEACE and amnesic probing are the antecedent of rank-1 insufficiency, and Arditi et al. is the directional-ablation pipeline whose control register the paper proposes to change. This does not change either decision; it lowers the novelty I credit to the ablation section and confirms ruling (c).

Numbers. 145 of 162 items match the committed files at printed precision; every Table 2 cell is derivable from the files to 1e-9; Table 4 matches in all eighteen cells; the rank-23 and re-split runs carry pre-registration hashes. Five numbers are wrong at printed precision, none in a headline contrast. The most consequential is Table 3's "<= 0.01" dose-0 shift for the rank-1 arm, which is actually up to 0.07 and is the cell that argues the rank-1 arm leaves the instrument intact (it still does, since 0.07 is under the 0.08 the paper accepts for the permuted frame, but the printed bound is wrong by 7x). One number, the control's 0.004 cosine, has no committed source, which the paper discloses. The check independently confirms the three abstract overclaims the reviewers found and adds two smaller ones ("rise at both steps", where only angry's last step excludes zero; "removes no readable affect", where afraid's share is 9%). It finds that the -0.021 dip sentence mixes the two anchors ("1 of 10 positive" belongs to the anchor-2000 dip), which settles R1's W11: the sentence goes. It also finds that Table 3's footprint-ratio column mixes two conventions (10.5 under one, 11.6 under the other), which matters because "10x against 0.85x" is the paper's stated contrast between the two permuted controls. Net effect: the tables are trustworthy and the abstract prose is not. The decisions are unchanged; my confidence that the camera-ready fixes are mechanical is higher; and the footnote's "machine-checked" claim should say it covers the quotable subset of the registry.

## 4. Decisions

NeurIPS 2026 main track: reject, 4/10. The paper's instrumentation and candour are above the main-track bar and its scientific increment is below it. The receiver phenomenon is construct-ambiguous by the paper's own control and is the expected consequence of steering changing A's text; the estimator study re-measures a known mechanism, adds a useful quantity, and then does not run the intervention its own motivation requires; the rank-1 ablation result is what concept erasure predicts and is uncited as such; and the one new methodological claim, that a fitted label-free control catches what random controls miss, rests on a single permutation draw with an unresolved covariance confound and no damage check. With every condition below met the paper would be a well-executed methods note on a narrow question, which is a workshop paper. I am not averaging the reviews; I arrive at 4 because a main-track paper needs a finding that survives its own best alternative explanation, and this one has not yet run the arm that would decide it.

IAB workshop: accept as poster, 6/10. The every-arm-against-baseline table, the disjoint-half reproducibility measurement with its mechanism, the honest instrument failure, and the demonstration that a study with only random controls would have called B1d's blocks emotion-specific are what practitioners at a workshop on interpreting agents should take home, and the paper's defects are fixable in prose and in a few GPU-hours. It is not a spotlight because the thing it teaches comes with a mechanism the paper misidentifies, its central control is n = 1, its abstract is wrong on its own tables, and nothing an agent does is interpreted. If conditions 2, 3 and 12 are met with a covariance-matched frame that is inert and a transcript-level readout present, I would move it to a spotlight; if only the prose conditions are met, poster stands.

## 6. Strongest and weakest sentence

Strongest (Section 6): "A study with only the random control would have called B1d's blocks emotion-specific." It is the paper's contribution in one line, it is true on Table 2 as printed (random direction q >= 0.26, random 5-frame q >= 0.51, permuted frame q <= 0.035 on three emotions), and it is the sentence a practitioner will remember.

Weakest (abstract): "A random frame matched to the permuted frame's footprint (rank 23) is inert, so the fitted control bites because it is fitted, not because of what it removes; and the receiver's "other-speaker" projection rises with dose as much as its own, so the probe does not separate the receiver's state from its model of the sender's." The first clause is a non sequitur: matching removed norm with an isotropic frame does not test the covariance alternative that the top-PC arm supports, and a frame fitted to permuted labels is fitted to nothing; the second clause misstates the numbers (the other-speaker slope is smaller for four of five testable emotions, by half for afraid and angry). It is the sentence on which the paper's recommended rule rests. Runner-up: the abstract's "blocks three emotions as strongly as the affect subspace does", which Table 1 contradicts directly.
