# Review — Reviewer 3 (construct validity and claims calibration)

**Paper:** "Affective transfer between LLM agents: the estimator, two pre-registered ablations, and a control that bites"
**Venue:** ICLR, main track

---

## 1. Summary

The paper steers one LLM agent into an emotional state, has it talk to a second agent, and measures the second agent's activations along a fitted "present-emotion" direction; the projection increases with steering dose for five of six emotions on two models. It then argues that the linear machinery the field uses for this is broken twice over: logistic-regression probe directions do not reproduce across disjoint-half fits (0.57 on the 27B, 0.29 on the 8B) whereas difference-of-means does (0.97/0.94), because every logistic fit on these activations separates its training half perfectly and the direction is therefore the penalty's tie-break; and, in three pre-registered ablations, a control direction fitted by the same estimator on permuted labels can block transfer where the field's norm-matched random control is inert. The headline positive result is small and largely negative in flavour: removing the rank-1 emotion direction at every readable layer leaves a median 91% of the transfer, with real blocks only for afraid (25%) and sad (29%).

## 3. Strengths

1. **The mechanism is measured, not asserted.** Most papers that report "probe directions are unstable" stop at the observation. This one goes and checks the obvious rival explanations — sample size (Fig. 1, six values of n over a 27-fold range), penalty strength (three C settings plus a fixed per-sample schedule), and estimator choice (difference-of-means on the *same* splits) — and then produces the actual mechanism: training accuracy is exactly 1.000 in every cell, arm and seed, worst log-loss 0.0055, so the likelihood is saturated and the fitted row is a tie-break among separating hyperplanes. Fig. 2's dotted curve, showing the logistic direction departing from the mean-difference direction (0.97 → 0.63 on the 27B, 0.91 → 0.43 on the 8B) exactly as the split-half cosine fails to improve, is the right plot and it does the work. I came in expecting to dismiss this as a known result restated; I concede it is not.

2. **The controls point is correct and actionable, and the paper resists the temptation to oversell it.** The demonstration that a *fitted* label-free control (B1d's rank-5 permuted subspace) blocks afraid, sad and angry while every norm-matched random and orthogonal direction is inert is the kind of negative control result that should change practice. Crucially, Section 5 and Section 6 both state that the rank-5 permuted frame and the rank-1 permuted direction differ in *both* rank and footprint and that "which matters is untested" — the authors had every incentive to claim the rank story and did not.

3. **Provenance discipline is genuinely exceptional.** Every number carries a monospace identifier into a machine-checked claim registry; model revisions and library versions are pinned; the seed derivation (CRC32 of a tuple with the arm field deliberately emptied for the receiver so that arms share random numbers) is stated precisely enough to reimplement. Combined with Appendix A's volunteered defects, this is a submission where I can tell what was actually done, which is rarer than it should be.

4. **The paper reports its own registered verdict when the verdict is "you failed".** B1d is presented as `instrument_failed` — the rank-5 subspace shifts the readout used to check it (desperate 0.11 → 0.50 at dose 0), so the manipulation check shares a target with the ablation and the experiment cannot conclude. No per-emotion contrast clears the registered BH-FDR rule (q = 0.051), and that is stated in the body rather than in a footnote. Similarly, the 8B B1c run is reported as `instrument_failed` on a strict-inequality rule the authors then explain was mis-specified rather than quietly patching it.

## 4. Weaknesses

**W1. Section 3's data does not exist in the paper.** Section 2 describes exactly one probe pool: "a 1,615-item probe pool", split into disjoint halves for probe-fitting and direction-fitting. Section 3 then fits split-half directions at **n = 2000 per half**, i.e. on at least 4,000 items, and the Reproducibility statement confirms "split-half n ∈ {75, 150, 300, 600, 1200, 2000} per half". Those two facts cannot both describe the same pool, and no second pool is ever introduced, sized, or described — not its construction, not its class balance, not its emotion coverage, not its relationship to the 1,615-item ablation pool. The entire estimator contribution, which is the best thing in this paper, is therefore run on data the reader knows nothing about. Fix: add a subsection to Section 2 describing the estimator pool (size, construction, class structure, overlap with the ablation pool), and say explicitly whether the split-half halves are drawn from the same generation process as the pool used for the ablation directions.

**W2. The main outcome measure is never validated as a measure of the receiver's state, and where a second readout exists it disagrees.** The abstract's first sentence — "the second agent's internal representation of its own state moves with the dose" — is a strong construct claim resting entirely on a projection onto one difference-of-means direction fitted from one pool. There is no convergent-validity evidence: no behavioural readout (Section 7 lists "the behavioural reach" as untested), no human or model judgement of B's replies, no discriminant check that the projection is not tracking topic, lexical valence, or A's surface style rather than B's state. And where the paper *does* have a second measure, it disagrees on the headline emotion: Appendix A, B1e — "The secondary readout on B's reply contradicts afraid's block and agrees for sad". Afraid is one of the two emotions in the abstract (25%). The abstract should not carry a number whose only convergent check fails. Fix: state the secondary readout's result for afraid in Section 5's body text (it is currently only in the appendix and in a half-sentence at the end of the B1e paragraph), and downgrade the abstract's language from "internal representation of its own state" to what the measure supports — a probe projection.

**W3. B1c's decision contrast is referenced to an arm that was never tested, and its headline interval excludes its own data.** The abstract's "leaves 91% of the transfer" and Section 5's "removes a median 9% of the dose-response, CI [4%, 25%]" correspond to Table 1's *first row*, which is labelled "emotion dir − random dir" — the contrast is against the random arm, not against none. But Section 5 also states, of that same random arm, that it "moves happy by +28% with overlapping CIs, descriptively, since **no random-versus-none contrast was computed**". So the reference arm of the paper's headline number is (a) not verified inert and (b) visibly non-inert for one of the five emotions being medianed. Separately, the five testable values in that row are 0.06, 0.21, 0.05, 0.33, 0.09 — the reported CI [4%, 25%] does not contain 0.33, because (Section 7) it is a scenario-only bootstrap that does not include emotion-to-emotion spread. A median summary whose interval excludes 40% of its own points should not be the abstract's number. Fix: compute and report the random-versus-none contrast; report the emotion-vs-none blocked fractions as a table row alongside the emotion-vs-random ones; and either widen the median interval to include emotion spread or replace the median-with-CI by the per-emotion range.

**W4. Figure 3 cannot support its caption, and Figure 4's caption asserts what the body retracts.** Fig. 3 plots four arms × six emotions with **no error bars, no bands, and no shading of any kind** on any of the 24 curves; the only uncertainty anywhere in the panel is the none-arm slope CI in each title. Its caption nonetheless states "the ablation arms separate from the none arm only for afraid and sad" — a statistical claim about between-arm separation that the figure gives the reader no way to assess and that, per W3, the paper's own contrasts do not compute against the none arm. Worse for the construct: the panels show the dose-response is not monotone for two of the six emotions. Happy rises from ≈221 at dose 0 to ≈292 at dose 0.33 and then *declines* across the remaining two doses (≈279, ≈276); calm peaks at 0.67 and collapses at 1.0; Appendix A concedes "Afraid's top-dose readout is non-monotone". An OLS slope over four dose points is the wrong summary for a curve that is a step followed by a decline, and "dose-dependent" (title of Fig. 3, and the framing of the whole of Section 4) is not what happy's panel shows. Meanwhile Fig. 4's caption states flatly that "the fitted rank-5 permuted subspace (third bar) blocks afraid, sad and angry" — true only on the unadjusted intervals that Section 5 parenthetically labels "(unadjusted CIs)" and that the same paragraph says clear no registered FDR threshold. Fix: put scenario-blocked bands on Fig. 3's arms; report a monotone-trend statistic (Spearman or Jonckheere over the four doses) beside every OLS slope, or restrict the dose-dependence claim to the emotions that are actually monotone; and move the "unadjusted" qualifier into Fig. 4's caption.

**W5. The abstract and Section 6 state as a finding what Sections 5 and 7 identify as unidentified.** The abstract says "a fitted, label-free control blocks transfer where the field's norm-matched random direction is inert", and Section 6 says "What blocked without emotion information was a fitted rank-5 frame with ten times the emotion direction's footprint". Both sentences invite the reading that *fitting* is what makes a control bite. The paper's own text does not support that: the fitted rank-1 permuted-label direction (B1e) blocks nothing at all (Table 1 row 5: 0.02, −0.00, −0.10, (−0.04), −0.01, 0.00), and Section 5 concedes the two fitted controls "differ in rank and in footprint … and which matters is untested". The competing explanation — that B1d's frame blocks because it removes an order of magnitude more norm per position, not because it was fitted — is neither excluded nor, apparently, testable from the runs performed, because Appendix A says "the 0.85×–5.8× footprint band … is unsampled". Fix: run a *random* rank-5 frame at B1d's footprint (the paper says a random 5-frame was inert but never states its footprint) and a fitted rank-1 control scaled up to B1d's footprint; until then, phrase the abstract as "a fitted rank-5 control blocks where random controls are inert, by a mechanism we cannot separate from its footprint", and drop the general controls prescription in Section 6 to a conjecture.

**W6. "Reproduce" is used for two incompatible things, and the stronger word is attached to the weaker evidence.** The introduction supports "the transfer itself is robust" with "three runs that reproduce each other bit for bit on the 27B" (Section 4: "The same slopes appear, bit for bit, in the three runs that share the pool and directions"). Runs that share the pool, the directions and — per the seed scheme — the random numbers, and that agree *bit for bit*, are a determinism check. They contain exactly zero information about sampling variability and cannot license the word "robust". The paper knows the difference — Section 3's entire contribution is the distinction between overlapping subsamples and disjoint halves, and the related-work paragraph makes precisely this argument against Gao et al.'s 20%-ablation robustness — which makes the slip in Section 4 harder to excuse, not easier. Fix: call these determinism checks, and get variability from re-drawing the probe pool and re-fitting the directions, which is the analogue of the disjoint-half argument the paper makes so well elsewhere.

**W7. Density: the paper is compressed past the point where a reader can audit it.** Section 5's B1d paragraph is eleven lines carrying variance shares, principal-component cosines, an instrument threshold, a baseline shift, three blocked magnitudes in slope units, a median increment with CI, a q-value, and two rank-5-vs-rank-1 deltas, with six claim identifiers interleaved. Several of those numbers cannot be checked against anything else in the paper because Table 1 gives only the *decision* contrast per experiment: there is no arm-vs-none row for the affect subspace, so `b1d.rank5_vs_rank1`'s +29.3 and −60.6 are unverifiable from the submission. The whole paper contains one table. Fix: add a second table giving every arm's blocked fraction against none, per emotion, per experiment, per model — it is the table a reader needs and it would let the prose shed half its numbers.

## 5. Cross-checks performed, and mismatches found

I checked twelve numbers across abstract, body, Table 1, all four figures and Appendix A. What agrees, agrees exactly, and I say so.

**Agreements (conceded).**
- All 30 cells of Table 1 match the corresponding bars in Fig. 4, including signs and the parenthesised calm column. This is the tightest table–figure correspondence I have checked this cycle.
- Abstract's 0.57 / 0.29 vs 0.97 / 0.94 ↔ Section 3's 0.566 / 0.286 vs 0.974 / 0.941 ↔ Fig. 1's plotted endpoints. Consistent.
- Section 5's B1d slope-unit blocks reconstruct from Table 1 fractions × Fig. 3 none-arm slopes: afraid 212 × 0.23 = 48.8 vs reported 49.5; sad 98 × 0.27 = 26.5 vs 26.7; angry 196 × 0.23 = 45.1 vs 44.3. Consistent to rounding.
- "Median 9% removed" / abstract's "leaves 91%" ↔ median of Table 1's B1c testable cells {0.06, 0.21, 0.05, 0.33, 0.09} = 0.09. Consistent.
- B1e's afraid 25% [17%, 33%] and sad 29% [7%, 56%] ↔ Fig. 4's fourth bars and error ranges, and ↔ Appendix A's "sad's range starts at 0.07". Consistent.

**Mismatches (report exactly as found).**
1. **Section 3, tuned C on the 8B: "0.37–0.41".** Fig. 1's 8B panel plots the tuned-C series at ≈0.30, 0.35, 0.377, 0.372, 0.383 across n = 75…1200. The upper bound 0.41 does not appear on the only 8B panel in the paper, and no other 8B layer is shown. Either state which cell produces 0.41 or correct the range to what Fig. 1 shows.
2. **Section 3 "Why": "Weakening the penalty a hundredfold … (0.52 against 0.58 at n = 2000)".** The 0.52 series is Fig. 2's `logistic, C = 500` curve, whose own caption calls it "a 1000× weaker penalty". C = 0.5 → 500 is a thousandfold weakening, not a hundredfold; C = 50 would be hundredfold, and Appendix A says the two arms are tolerance-limited to the same solution — which makes the sentence harmless but still wrong as written.
3. **Footprint ratio: Section 6 says the rank-5 frame has "ten times the emotion direction's footprint"; Section 5 gives the two footprints as "6.5 against 0.5 per position"**, a ratio of 13. Pick one.
4. **B1e control footprint: Section 5 says the permuted-label direction has "0.85× its footprint" (one number); Appendix A says "0.84× afraid, 0.77× sad" (two).** If 0.85 is a mean or median over emotions, say so; as printed, the body's single figure is not derivable from the appendix's pair.
5. **Pool size vs split-half n.** Section 2: "a 1,615-item probe pool". Section 3 and the Reproducibility statement: n up to 2000 *per half*. Incompatible unless a second, undescribed pool exists (see W1).
6. **"It does not rise with the sample size" (related-work paragraph) vs Fig. 1.** The same paragraph gives the 27B range as "0.41–0.57", which is itself a range over n; Fig. 1's C = 0.5 series on layer 43 rises from ≈0.39 at n = 75 to 0.566 at n = 2000, a gain of 0.17 across a 27-fold increase. The defensible claim — that the *fixed-per-sample-penalty* arm is flat and no arm converges toward 1 — is made correctly in Section 3 and then over-generalised here and in the contributions list.
7. **Minor, presentation:** Fig. 1's 8B panel carries a tick at n = 2000 with no data at it, inviting the eye to read a truncated series; and neither Fig. 1 nor Fig. 2 states which fixed-penalty anchor (N_ref = 600 or 2000) is plotted, though the body distinguishes them and cites different claim ids for each.

## 6. Questions for the authors

1. What is the pool used for Section 3's split-half study — its size, construction, class structure, and its relation to the 1,615-item ablation pool? Fits at n = 2000 per half require at least 4,000 items and I cannot find them described anywhere in the submission.
2. Does the "present-emotion projection" have any convergent validation? Specifically: does anything other than a probe fitted on the same pool agree that B is in the induced state — a behavioural measure, a held-out judge, or the secondary readout reported per-emotion rather than only for afraid and sad?
3. Given that the secondary readout on B's reply contradicts afraid's block (Appendix A) and that afraid does not block against baseline on the 8B (q = 0.08), on what basis does afraid's 25% appear in the abstract? Would you accept restricting the abstract's ablation claim to sad, the only emotion that replicates on both models and both outcomes?
4. Was a random-versus-none contrast for B1c ever computed, and if not, why is the emotion-versus-random contrast the decision contrast rather than emotion-versus-none? The random arm moves happy by +28%.
5. Section 5 says the blocked fraction "tracks how much readable affect the ablation removes (r ≈ 0.85 over five points)"; the very next experiment says PC1 removes 79% and 71% of afraid's and sad's readable affect and blocks neither, "readable-affect removal and blocking dissociate"; and Appendix A says B1d's permuted frame removed 6.5% of afraid's readable affect and blocked it by 49.5. What is the paper's actual claim about the relationship between readable-affect removal and blocking? As printed, three passages assert three incompatible ones.
6. What interval accompanies r ≈ 0.85 on five points, and does it exclude zero?
7. Section 3 claims "a significant dip at 600" in the fixed-penalty curve; Fig. 2's bands around n = 300, 600 and 1200 overlap substantially. Is the dip significant in a paired test across the ten shared splits, and can that test be reported?
8. Appendix A says "the 0.85×–5.8× footprint band, where B1d's frame blocked, is unsampled" — but Section 5 puts B1d's frame at 6.5 per position against 0.5, i.e. 13×, which is outside that band. Where does B1d's frame actually sit on the footprint axis, and what is the footprint of the random 5-frame that was inert?
9. Would you consider splitting this into the paper it wants to be? The estimator study (Sections 3, 6) is a clean, well-evidenced methods contribution; the affect-transfer study is a mostly-null result carrying a large fraction of the page budget and most of the caveats.

## 7. Overall

**Score: 5/10. Confidence: 4/5. Recommendation: reject at the ICLR main-track bar — narrowly, and reversibly.**

I wanted to dislike this paper and I cannot dislike it as much as its abstract deserves. The estimator half is right, is argued from the mechanism rather than from vibes, and is the sort of finding that should change a common pipeline: if every logistic fit on these activations separates its half perfectly, the coefficient row is the penalty's tie-break and not an estimate, and reporting disjoint-half reproducibility instead of overlapping-subsample robustness is exactly the correction the field needs. The controls point is also real, and the provenance discipline and Appendix A are better than almost anything I review. Against that: the paper's flagship measurement — the receiver's "internal representation of its own state" — is one projection onto one direction from one pool, with no convergent validation, contradicted by its own secondary readout for the very emotion the abstract leads with; the pool underlying the strongest contribution is not described in the paper at all; the headline "leaves 91%" is a contrast against an arm never tested for inertness, carrying an interval that excludes one of its own five points; Figure 3 asserts a between-arm separation with no uncertainty drawn and calls two visibly non-monotone curves "dose-dependent"; and the abstract and Section 6 promote a fitted-versus-random controls rule that Section 5 correctly says is confounded with footprint. None of these are fatal in kind — they are description, framing and one or two missing runs — and every one of my six mismatches is small. But construct validity is the axis on which this paper stakes its claim to matter, and on that axis the submission is currently a good methods paper wearing an affect-transfer paper as a costume. Fix W1 (describe the pool), W3 (compute random-versus-none, report arm-versus-none), W5 (run the footprint-matched controls or downgrade the rule), and trim the abstract to what survives replication, and I would move to a clear accept. As submitted, it is below the bar by a margin I would happily see closed at rebuttal.

**Time spent:** a human reviewer doing what I did — one careful pass over the ten pages, a second pass on Sections 3 and 5, viewing and measuring all four figures against the text and Table 1, reconstructing the twelve cross-checks (including the slope × fraction arithmetic and the Table 1 medians), and writing this up — would spend **3.5 to 4 hours**.

## Recalibration — IAB @ NeurIPS 2026

**1. Original (ICLR main track):** 5/10, confidence 4/5, reject — narrowly, reversibly.

**2. Recalibrated (IAB workshop bar):** **8/10**, confidence **4/5**, **accept** (long paper).

**3. Justification.** The revision retires the defects my reject rested on. Section 2 "Two pools" names the 4,253/3,898-item estimator pools whose absence was W1. Table 2 puts every hooked arm against baseline under one significance standard, including the random-versus-none contrast I said was missing — and it costs them: sad's rank-1 block no longer clears FDR against baseline (q 0.115), and happy moves −0.28 under the random arm. The abstract now says the rank-versus-footprint question is untested instead of asserting the controls rule, and it carries the other-speaker result as a stated limit: the receiver's projection of the *sender's* state rises with dose as much as its own, so the probe does not separate them. Authors rarely volunteer the sentence that most undercuts their title. The venue does the rest — IAB is non-archival and explicitly welcomes negative results, and this is a well-instrumented negative result about tools that audience uses daily. My objections were to a construct oversold for an archival claim; the overselling is gone and this venue never demanded the claim.

**4. Condition.** The title and Section 4 still say "affective transfer". Rename to what the probe licenses — a dose-dependent, probe-readable affect signal in the receiver — because the paper's own other-speaker result forbids reading it as the receiver's state.
