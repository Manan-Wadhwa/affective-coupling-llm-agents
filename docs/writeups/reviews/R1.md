# Review — Reviewer 1

**Paper:** "Affective transfer between LLM agents: the estimator, two pre-registered ablations, and a control that bites"
**Venue:** ICLR, main track

---

## 1. Summary

The paper steers one LLM agent into an emotion, has it talk to a second agent, and measures the second agent's internal "present-emotion" projection as a function of steering dose; it reports dose-dependent movement for five of six emotions on two models. Its methodological core is that the logistic-regression direction most of this literature reads and intervenes with fails to reproduce across disjoint halves (0.57 on the 27B, 0.29 on the 8B) where difference-of-means reaches 0.94–0.97, because every fit on these activations is a perfect separator and the fitted row is the penalty's tie-break rather than an estimate. Three pre-registered ablations then find the rank-1 emotion direction carries at most a fifth to a third of the transfer for two emotions, and — the paper's headline — that a control fitted by the same estimator on permuted labels can block transfer where the field's norm-matched random vector does nothing.

## 3. Strengths

1. **The central quantity is the right one, and it is measured properly.** Reproducibility across *disjoint* halves is the correct diagnostic, and the paper is explicit about why it differs from the 0.88–0.99 robustness numbers of Gao et al. under 20% training-data ablation: overlapping subsamples share most of their examples, disjoint halves share none. Ten split seeds, six values of n per half, two depths on the 27B plus the 8B focus layer, with bootstrap bands over splits. The gap it reports (Figure 1: 0.566 vs 0.974 at n = 2000, layer 43) is large, consistent across depths, and survives both cross-validated C and a fixed per-sample penalty schedule. This is a well-designed diagnostic and I would like to see it become standard.

2. **The separability diagnostic is the part of the mechanism story that does real work.** Reporting training accuracy exactly 1.000 in every cell, arm and seed, worst single-fit log-loss 0.0055, at most 80 iterations, and then showing (Figure 2, dotted series) that the logistic direction *starts near* the mean-difference direction at n = 75 (0.97) and *departs from it* as n grows (0.63 at n = 2000) while difference-of-means becomes reproducible — that is a clean and falsifiable account, and the 100× weak-penalty check (direction unchanged at cosine ≥ 0.96, reproducibility 0.52 vs 0.58) rules out the obvious regularisation objection. The authors also explicitly decline to claim max-margin identification or a support-vector mechanism, which is the correct call given tolerance-limited arms.

3. **Figures 3 and 4 survive independent recomputation.** I recomputed the four-dose OLS slopes from the plotted none-arm means in Figure 3: afraid gives +211 against the reported +212, happy +50 against +48, calm +44 against +44. I checked all thirty cells of Table 1 against the bar heights in Figure 4 and found agreement to reading precision, including the parenthesised calm values (0.94, 1.56, −0.24, 1.02, −0.04). I checked the headline median: the B1c row over five testable emotions {0.05, 0.06, 0.09, 0.21, 0.33} has median 0.09, exactly the reported "median 9%" and the abstract's "91% of the transfer". I also confirmed that the B1d raw numbers (afraid −49.5, sad −26.7, angry −44.3) are the Table 1 fractions times the Figure 3 none-arm slopes (0.23×212 = 48.8, 0.27×98 = 26.5, 0.226×196 = 44.3). The internal arithmetic of this paper is honest. That is rarer than it should be and I credit it.

4. **The design controls that are present are the right ones and are used properly.** Common random numbers with the arm field emptied for the receiver's reply, so sampling noise cancels inside paired contrasts; scenario as the bootstrap cluster because scenario is the sampling unit; the probe fitted on one half of the 1,615-item pool with steering and ablation directions from the disjoint other half. The ablation windows are internally consistent (hidden states 13–63 is 51 layers, 13–42 is 30, matching the "30-layer and 51-layer windows" of Appendix A and the "all 51 layers" of B1e).

5. **Appendix A is a model of adverse disclosure.** The empty pre-registration stamp with its cause, the 1,615-vs-2,160 pool deviation, the FDR run over five emotions rather than the registered six, the driver-declared rather than pre-registered layer-window contrast, the secondary readout contradicting afraid, the cosine-0.004 figure that was measured pre-launch but never stamped in the result file. I disagree with several of the paper's conclusions; I do not doubt its good faith.

## 4. Weaknesses

1. **Table 1 bolds two different significance criteria under one caption, and the lenient one carries the paper's headline claim.** The caption says only "significant contrasts in bold". In the B1d block, the row *affect subspace − permuted subspace* has no bold entry — Section 5 explains that "no per-emotion contrast clears the registered BH-FDR rule (q = 0.051)". The row immediately below it, *permuted subspace − none*, has three bold entries (afraid 0.23, sad 0.27, angry 0.23) — and Section 5 states in the same paragraph that these come from **unadjusted CIs**. Same experiment, same table, adjacent rows, opposite standards; and the row that gets the lenient standard is precisely the one that becomes "fitted label-free controls bite" in the abstract, Section 6 and the Figure 4 caption. The contrast is vivid at the cell level: B1d *affect − permuted* sad = 0.26 is not bold, B1d *permuted − none* sad = 0.27 is bold. A third standard appears in B1c, where Appendix A concedes the FDR ran over five emotions rather than the registered six. **Fix:** state the exact test, correction family and threshold for every row in the Table 1 caption; apply one family to the whole table; and if the permuted-subspace row cannot survive BH-FDR over six emotions, say so in the abstract and Section 6 rather than in a parenthesis.

2. **The paper's headline positive result is one arm of an experiment whose own verdict is `instrument_failed`, and its most obvious confound is never measured.** B1d is declared an instrument failure because the rank-5 affect subspace shifts the readout at dose 0 (desperate 0.11 → 0.50). You cannot declare an experiment's instrument invalid and then promote a different arm of the same experiment to the paper's central claim. Critically, the dose-0 baseline shift is reported *only* for the affect-subspace arm and never for the permuted-subspace arm — yet that arm is a rank-5 projection with 6.5 footprint per position, roughly eleven times the emotion direction's. "The permuted subspace blocks transfer" and "a large-footprint five-dimensional projection perturbs B's readout" are not distinguished by anything printed in the paper. Appendix A's argument that the permuted subspace removed only 6.5% of afraid's readable affect addresses removal along the readable-affect direction, not general readout distortion. **Fix:** report the dose-0 readout shift for every arm of B1d in the same form as `b1d.readout_baseline_shift`, and report the random 5-frame's footprint. If the random 5-frame is genuinely inert at comparable footprint, that is the experiment that establishes your rule; as printed, your own data are equally consistent with "big projections block, fitted or not" — which you concede ("they differ in rank and in footprint … and which matters is untested") and then proceed to rule on anyway in Section 6.

3. **The random control is asserted inert in the abstract and Section 6, and Section 5 states the contrast was never computed.** Abstract: "a fitted, label-free control blocks transfer where the field's norm-matched random direction is inert." Section 6: "Norm-matched random and orthogonal directions … were inert in every run." Section 5, B1c: "moves happy by +28% with overlapping CIs, **descriptively, since no random-versus-none contrast was computed**." A +28% move on happy is larger than the emotion direction's blocked fraction for three of the five testable emotions in the B1c row of Table 1 (desperate 0.06, happy 0.05, angry 0.09), and Figure 3's happy panel shows the random arm ending visibly *above* the none arm at dose 1.0. Absence of a computed contrast is not evidence of a null, and the entire rhetorical structure of the paper — random controls are decided by nothing, fitted controls are decided the way the intervention was — rests on that null. **Fix:** compute the random-vs-none contrast for all six emotions under the same scenario-blocked, dose-paired bootstrap and the same correction family as everything else, put it in Table 1 as a sixth row, and rewrite the abstract sentence to whatever that row supports.

4. **Every 27B ablation is run with a single draw of a direction the paper has just proved is estimator-sensitive, and no ablation interval reflects that.** Section 7 concedes "One probe pool and one direction estimate underlie all ablation runs on the 27B." The paper's own Section 3 is an argument that a direction fitted once is a random variable with non-trivial spread. Difference-of-means at 0.97 split-half is a real mitigation and I credit it, but 0.97 is not 1.00, the ablation results turn on differences of a few percent of slope, and the reported CIs are scenario-only — Section 7 says so. The consequence is that B1c's "median 9%" and B1e's afraid-25%/sad-29% have no stated sensitivity to the one input the paper spends three pages showing is unstable. **Fix:** refit the emotion direction on k independent halves (you already have ten split seeds), rerun at least B1e's decision contrast for each, and report the across-draw spread beside the scenario bootstrap. If the answer is that it barely moves, that is a strong result and it costs you two GPU-hours per draw by your own timing.

5. **A four-point OLS slope cannot distinguish a dose-response from a step or an inverted U, and Figure 3 shows both.** In Figure 3 the calm none-arm means run 101 → 140 → 174 → 139 — an inverted U whose top dose falls back nearly to its 0.33 value — and yield slope +44. The happy none-arm means run 221 → 292 → 279 → 281 — a step between dose 0 and dose 0.33, then flat or declining — and yield slope +48. Two qualitatively opposite curve shapes, four units apart in the summary statistic that the abstract calls "moves with the dose". Happy is one of the five emotions carrying "dose-dependent affect transfer" in the first contribution bullet, and its CI [+8, +88] barely excludes zero. Appendix A separately notes "Afraid's top-dose readout is non-monotone." **Fix:** report a monotonicity or shape test (isotonic fit, or a dose-0-vs-rest contrast against a linear-trend contrast) per emotion, and either restrict the "dose-dependent" claim to the emotions that pass it or restate the claim as "responds to steering" for the others. Also add per-dose scenario-blocked intervals to Figure 3's points; at present the reader cannot see the noise that the slope CI is computed from.

6. **Three named numerical mismatches between text, figure and appendix.** (a) Section 3 reports cross-validated C reaching "0.37–0.41 on the 8B", but the tuned-C series in Figure 1's right panel tops out at approximately 0.383 (n = 1200) and never touches 0.41; the related-work paragraph likewise gives "0.41–0.57 on the 27B" while Figure 1's left panel shows logistic C = 0.5 at approximately 0.39 at n = 75 and approximately 0.575 at n = 600 — neither endpoint of the stated range is locatable in any printed panel. (b) Section 5 describes B1c's random arm as "a unit-norm random direction, **removing twice the norm**"; Appendix A describes the same object as "B1c's inert random direction (**1.22**)". These cannot both be right and the paper never says which convention is which. (c) Section 3 says "Weakening the penalty **a hundredfold** leaves the direction unchanged … (0.52 against 0.58 at n = 2000)", but the 0.52 series is labelled `logistic, C = 500` in Figure 2's legend and the Figure 2 caption calls it "a **1000×** weaker penalty" — C = 0.5 → 500 is a thousandfold, and the paper also runs a C = 50 arm, so the reader cannot tell which arm the quoted number came from. **Fix:** all three are one-line corrections, but they matter in a paper whose footnote promises that "Every number in this paper is registered with a pointer to the committed script and result file that produced it."

7. **Two caption overstatements and one caption/title contradiction.** Figure 1's caption says "the three logistic variants plateau near 0.6 (27B) or fall to 0.28 (8B)"; in the 8B panel the tuned-C variant is at approximately 0.383 and *rising* at n = 1200 — two of three fall to 0.28, not three. Figure 3's caption says "the ablation arms separate from the none arm only for afraid and sad", but the calm panel shows the two emotion-direction arms separating from the none arm by roughly 20 and 40 projection units at doses 0.67 and 1.0, and Table 1 gives calm's B1c contrast as 0.94 — the largest in the table; the caption needs "among testable emotions". And the **title** advertises "**two** pre-registered ablations" while the abstract, the third contribution bullet and the Section 5 heading all say "**three** pre-registered ablations" (B1c, B1d, B1e).

8. **Several load-bearing numbers have no printed source in the paper.** The B1e paragraph's PC1 dissociation — "blocks angry by 61%", "removes 79% and 71% of their readable affect", "5.8× the footprint" — appears in no table and no figure; it is the cleanest evidence in the paper that readable-affect removal and blocking dissociate, and it is a prose assertion with a claim id pointing at a repository that is not part of the reviewed material. The control's cosine 0.004 to the emotion direction is explicitly "measured before launch, not stamped in the file". The footprint numbers are given in four incompatible forms across three sections and the appendix: "0.85× its footprint" (Section 5) against "0.84× afraid, 0.77× sad" (Appendix A) for the same object; "ten times the emotion direction's footprint" (Section 6) against "6.5 against 0.5 per position" (Section 5). **Fix:** one small table of footprint, rank, cosine-to-emotion-direction and readable-affect removed, for every direction and frame used in the paper.

9. **The separation "mechanism" is closer to a linear-algebra necessity than to a property of affect representations.** With d = 5120 and n ≤ 2000 per half, a multinomial fit separating its training half perfectly is essentially guaranteed for generic data; the paper says as much ("n ≤ 2000, d = 5120 … a proportional-regime statement, not an asymptotic one") and lists n > d under "Not tested". But the abstract presents it as a discovery about *these* activations ("The reason is that every logistic fit on these activations is a perfect separator"). The honest framing is that the practical recommendation holds precisely because probe pools in this literature are small relative to model width — which is a fine and useful point, made stronger by saying it plainly. **Fix:** either run one anchor at n > d (subsample dimensions, or pool across layers) to show where the behaviour changes, or restate the abstract claim as regime-conditional.

10. **"Bit for bit" agreement is presented as replication.** Section 1: "in three runs that reproduce each other bit for bit on the 27B"; Section 4: "The same slopes appear, bit for bit, in the three runs that share the pool and directions". Runs that share the pool, the directions and a deterministic CRC32 seed recipe are expected to agree bit for bit; that is a test of the code path, not of sampling variability, and it belongs in a reproducibility statement rather than in the sentence establishing that "the transfer itself is robust".

11. **Calm's exclusion removes the single largest contrast in the paper, and the exclusion rule's provenance is unstated.** Calm is dropped because its none-arm slope CI includes zero (+44 [−3, +99]); its B1c contrast is 0.94 and its B1d contrast 1.56, the two largest values in Table 1, which Appendix A acknowledges ("Calm's contrast is the largest and is dropped by the testability rule"). The paper never says whether the testability rule was registered before the slopes were seen. Since the headline "median 9%" is a median over exactly the five survivors — including calm would move it to 0.15 — the reader needs that assurance. **Fix:** state where the testability rule is registered and give the median with and without calm.

## 5. Questions for the authors

1. Table 1's caption says "significant contrasts in bold". For each of the five rows: which test, which correction family, how many hypotheses in the family, and what threshold? Specifically, why is B1d *permuted subspace − none* bolded on unadjusted CIs (afraid 0.23, sad 0.27, angry 0.23) while B1d *affect subspace − permuted subspace* is unbolded because it fails BH-FDR at q = 0.051? Do the three bold entries survive the same BH-FDR family the row above is held to?
2. What is the dose-0 readout shift for B1d's permuted-label subspace arm, in the same form as the reported desperate 0.11 → 0.50 for the affect subspace? And what is the random 5-frame's footprint per position? Without both, how do you separate "the fitted control blocks" from "a rank-5 projection with roughly eleven times the emotion direction's footprint distorts B's readout"?
3. You state that no random-versus-none contrast was computed for B1c, and then assert in the abstract and Section 6 that norm-matched random directions were "inert in every run". Will you compute that contrast under the same bootstrap and correction, for all six emotions, and report it? What does the happy +28% become when you do?
4. Section 5 says B1c's random direction removes "twice the norm"; Appendix A says it is "1.22". Which is correct, and in what units? Relatedly, is the B1e control 0.85× the emotion direction's footprint (Section 5) or 0.84×/0.77× per emotion (Appendix A)?
5. Where in Figure 1 can I find the 0.41 that appears twice — as the lower end of "0.37–0.41 on the 8B" for tuned C, and as the lower end of "0.41–0.57 on the 27B" in the related-work paragraph? The 8B tuned-C series peaks near 0.383 and the 27B C = 0.5 series bottoms near 0.39.
6. Section 3 says "a hundredfold" weakening; Figure 2's legend says C = 500 and its caption says 1000×. Which arm produced the 0.52-against-0.58 comparison, C = 50 or C = 500?
7. All 27B ablations use one direction estimate. You have ten split seeds already. What is the across-draw spread of B1e's afraid (25%) and sad (29%) blocked fractions when the emotion direction is refit on independent halves?
8. Was the testability rule that excludes calm registered before the none-arm slopes were computed? What is B1c's median blocked fraction over all six emotions?
9. Happy's none-arm means (Figure 3) rise from dose 0 to 0.33 and then flatten; calm's rise and then fall. What does a monotone-trend test say for each of the six emotions, and does "dose-dependent" survive it for happy?
10. Given that B1d's verdict is `instrument_failed`, on what grounds is one of its arms admissible as the paper's headline evidence in Section 6 and the abstract?
11. The three token-channel rewrite designs all failed their own manipulation check. What fraction of the transfer could the token channel account for under the most pessimistic reading of those failed runs, and does anything in the paper bound it?
12. The title says "two pre-registered ablations"; the abstract, contributions and Section 5 say three. Which is it, and does the distinction track which pre-registration stamps actually resolved?
13. Appendix A says B1c's pre-registration stamp is empty because of a path bug, and that the document "was on the machine one second before the run started". What independent evidence — a commit hash, a timestamped hash of the document, anything a reviewer can check — supports the pre-registration claim for B1c?

## 6. Overall

**Score: 5 / 10. Confidence: 4 / 5. Recommendation: reject** (marginally below the bar), with a clear route to acceptance.

There is a good paper here and it is not the one the abstract advertises. The estimator study is the real contribution: disjoint-half reproducibility is the right diagnostic, it is measured across two models, three depths and six sample sizes with ten split seeds, the separability evidence is decisive within its regime, and the recommendation to prefer an average over an extremum is one the field should adopt. The internal arithmetic is sound — I recomputed slopes from Figure 3's plotted means and medians from Table 1's rows and they land where the text says they land — and Appendix A discloses damage that most submissions would hide. Through gritted teeth: this is a more honest paper than most of what I review. But the ablation half, which is what the title and abstract sell, does not currently support its conclusions. The claim that fitted controls bite where random ones do not rests on (i) an arm of an experiment the authors themselves declared an instrument failure, (ii) unadjusted CIs in a table whose adjacent row is suppressed at q = 0.051, (iii) an inertness null for the random control that Section 5 states was never computed, and (iv) a fitted-versus-footprint confound the authors identify and then rule past. Layered on that are a single direction estimate underlying every 27B intervention in a paper whose thesis is that single direction estimates are unreliable, a four-point OLS slope that assigns near-identical "dose-response" to a step (happy) and an inverted U (calm), and a handful of numbers — 0.41 twice, the random direction's norm, the hundredfold-versus-1000× penalty, PC1's 61% — that do not trace to any printed panel despite a footnote promising that every number does. None of this is fatal and most of it is arithmetic the authors already have the runs to fix: compute the random-vs-none contrast, unify the significance criterion across Table 1, report the permuted-subspace baseline shift and the random 5-frame's footprint, refit the direction over the ten existing split seeds, and reconcile the numbers above. Do that and I would argue for acceptance; as submitted, the paper's strongest sentence is one its own Section 5 says it did not test.

A human referee doing what I did — reading a ten-page, notation-dense statistics paper twice, examining four multi-panel figures at full resolution, recomputing four-dose OLS slopes and a five-emotion median by hand from plotted values, checking all thirty cells of Table 1 against Figure 4's bars, tracing roughly twenty numbers across abstract, body, table, figures and appendix, and writing this up — would spend **4 to 5 hours**, most of it on the cross-checking rather than the reading.
