# Affective transfer between LLM agents: the estimator, two pre-registered ablations, and a control that bites

*Draft for the 2026-09-06 deadline. Every number carries its registry id in `docs/review/claims.json`; figures are produced by `src/rev3/paper_figures.py` from the result files. All results and the blind critiques of reports 22–30 are in and incorporated; a blind review of this draft against the registry (18 edits) has been applied.*

## Abstract (draft)
When two LLM agents converse and one is steered into an emotion, the other's internal representation of its own state moves with the dose: five of six emotions, in one sampling replication and two bit-identical pipeline reproductions on one pool (`b1f.*`, `b1c.*`, `b1d.*`). We ask what carries it. First we show that the direction most work uses to read and write such states — a logistic-regression coefficient row — does not reproduce across independent fits in the regime everyone works in, and why: every fit on these activations is a perfect separator, so the direction is the penalty's tie-break among separating hyperplanes, drifting away from the mean-difference direction as the sample grows while the mean-difference direction converges (`a2.*`, `a2f*.*`, `a2m.*`). Using the reproducible direction, two pre-registered ablations then test the linear account of the channel. Removing the emotion direction from the sender's span at every readable layer leaves a median 91% of the transfer (`b1c.*`); removing a rank-5 affect subspace fails its own instrument check, and a label-free subspace with the affect subspace's footprint, ten times the emotion direction's, blocks as much as the emotion direction (`b1d.*`). A pre-registered fitted, label-free rank-1 control then returns H3: the control is inert and the emotion direction blocks afraid by 25% and sad by 29% against it; a top-principal-direction control with six times the footprint blocks other emotions but not these, while removing most of their readable affect, so removal and blocking dissociate for afraid and sad, though across B1c's five emotions the blocked fraction tracks removal (`b1e.*`). On the 27B the control's footprint is below the emotion direction's; on the 8B it is matched only on average and adds no discriminating power over the random control, so the footprint caveat stands on both models. Across the two models only sad's block replicates on both outcomes (`b1e8.*`). The lesson for controls: a random direction was inert in every run, and so was a fitted rank-1 label-free direction; only a fitted rank-5 frame blocked, and whether rank or footprint does that is untested.

## 1. Setup
Two agents, A and B, in 29 fixed scenarios; A's message is generated under a steering vector at layer 42 of Qwen3.6-27B (revision 6a9e13bd; four doses 0, 0.33, 0.67, 1.0; three reps; B sampled with common random numbers across arms). Readouts: B's present-emotion projection on a probe fitted on a disjoint half of a 1,615-item probe pool (sha 70129f86…), and a generation-side forced-choice readout on A's span. Every contrast is scenario-blocked and dose-paired (5,000 draws). Second model: Llama-3-8B-Instruct-abliterated (dd67dd05) for the estimator results and a replication of the B1c design.

## 2. The estimator: reproducibility and its mechanism (Fig. 1, Fig. 2)
**Disjoint-half reproducibility.** Ten disjoint splits at n per half from 75 to 2000. At the 27B focus layer: difference-of-means 0.974, logistic (C = 0.5) 0.566 (`a2.focus_dom_split_half`, `a2.focus_logreg_split_half`); layer 54: 0.979 vs 0.554 (`a2f54.*`); 8B focus layer at n = 1200: 0.941 vs 0.286 (`a2_8b.*`). Tuned C reaches 0.61 on the 27B (`a2f.focus_tuned_c_does_not_close_gap`) and 0.37–0.41 on the 8B (`a2f8.lam_declines`, note).
**Holding the per-sample penalty fixed does not help.** With C_n = 0.5·N_REF/n the logistic curve shows no net rise from n = 150 to 2000 at two anchors on the 27B (0.581 → 0.581 at anchor 600; a significant dip at 600 and partial recovery; `a2f.focus_logreg_fixed_lambda_flat`, `a2f2.lam_anchor2000_no_rise`), one drop then flat at layer 54 (`a2f54.lam_declines_anchor600`), a rise at layer 16, and on the 8B a peak at 150 followed by a monotone fall to 0.276 (`a2f8.lam_declines`). Depth-specific, with no convergence over the reachable range (n ≤ 2000, d = 5120): a proportional-regime statement, not an asymptotic one.
**Why.** Every multinomial logistic fit on every half at every n used, under the fixed schedule and C = 0.5, 50 and 500 alike (the two weak arms being one tolerance-limited solution), separates its training half perfectly (accuracy exactly 1.000, worst single-fit log-loss 0.0055, at most 80 iterations; `a2m.separable_every_n`, `a2m.8b_same_regime`, `a2m.v2_ten_seeds`). The likelihood is saturated from n = 75: the direction is the penalty's tie-break among separating hyperplanes. Weakening the penalty 100× leaves the direction unchanged on the same half (cosine ≥ 0.96) and no more reproducible (0.52 vs 0.58; `a2m.weak_penalty_same_direction`, `a2m.v2_ten_seeds`). The separator starts near the mean-difference direction and departs from it as n grows — cosine 0.97 → 0.63 on the 27B (`a2m.logistic_departs_from_dom`), 0.91 → 0.43 on the 8B (`a2m.8b_same_regime`) — while difference-of-means becomes reproducible. Wording adopted from the blind review: *on separable activations the multinomial direction is the penalty's tie-break among separating hyperplanes, not a likelihood estimate; its split-half reproducibility plateaus at 0.55–0.60 (27B) and falls to 0.28 (8B) across a 27-fold n increase, while difference-of-means, an average rather than an extremum, rises to 0.94–0.98 on the same splits.*
**Against the near-identity claim.** In a quantity closer to the counter-paper's than our split-half battery, though still not identical to it (CAA vs logistic, raw space), the cosine is 0.43 (27B) and 0.33–0.37 (8B), not 0.98 (`a2f.caa_raw_vs_logreg_raw`, `a2f8.cross_estimator`). Not claimed: that the fit sits at the max-margin limit (the weak arms are tolerance-limited), or a support-vector mechanism for the drift.

## 3. Transfer (Fig. 3)
B's present-emotion projection rises with A's dose for desperate, afraid, happy, sad and angry (none-arm slopes +48 to +212 with CIs excluding zero; calm +44 [−3, +99] never does; `b1c.verdict_H1`), identically in three runs that share the pool and directions (`b1c.partial_block_replicates`, `b1d.replicates_b1c_arms`). MC-steer passes 6/6 in each. On the 8B the same five emotions transfer (`b1c8.transfer_replicates`), with a steering manipulation half as strong and less specific, so only the pattern is compared. Happy's is the weakest of the five (+48 [+8, +88]; Fig. 3).

## 4. Two pre-registered ablations (Fig. 4)
**B1c (PREREG_B1c, verdict H1).** Projecting the difference-of-means emotion direction out of A's span at every hidden state 13–63 during B's prefill removes a median 9% of the dose-response, CI [4%, 25%] over five testable emotions (`b1c.verdict_H1`, `b1c.median_fraction`); afraid 21% and sad 33% are the only significant blocks (`b1c.partial_block_replicates`); extending the ablation to layers 43–63 changes no dose-response detectably, with the extra blocking bounded under 0.08 for four emotions (a driver-declared contrast; `b1c.no_gain_beyond_layer42`); a unit-norm random direction, removing twice the norm, leaves five of six slopes within 5% of the none arm and moves happy by +28% with overlapping CIs — descriptive, since no random-vs-none contrast was computed (`b1c.random_control_inert`). The blocked fraction tracks how much readable affect the ablation removes (r ≈ 0.85; blocked-per-removed ≈ 0.27; `b1c.readout_residual`): the rank-1 direction is insufficient to carry the transfer.
**B1d (PREREG_B1d, verdict instrument_failed).** Projecting out the rank-5 class-mean affect subspace (which captures 16–21% of the pool's variance, cosine 0.71–0.81 to the top-5 principal components; `b1d.frame_geometry`) removes ≥ 80% of the readable affect for only 3 of 6 emotions and shifts the readout at dose 0 (desperate 0.11 → 0.50; `b1d.readout_baseline_shift`): the instrument and the ablation share a target. A permuted-label subspace built by the same estimator — no emotion information, a third overlap with the affect frame, 65× a random frame's variance — reduces the transfer for afraid (−49.5), sad (−26.7) and angry (−44.3), as much as the emotion direction did (`b1d.perm_control_blocks`; unadjusted CIs), while a random 5-frame is inert. The emotion-specific increment over that control is 0.14 [−0.03, 0.29], and no per-emotion contrast clears the registered BH-FDR rule (q = 0.051; `b1d.median_fraction_descriptive`); rank 5 differs from rank 1 in opposite directions, less blocking for afraid (+29.3) and more for angry (−60.6) (`b1d.rank5_vs_rank1`).
**B1e (PREREG_B1e, verdict H3).** The same estimator on permuted labels gives a rank-1 direction with cosine 0.004 to the emotion direction and 0.85× its footprint; removed at all 51 layers it changes no emotion's dose-response and removes no readable affect (`b1e.permdir_inert`). Against it, the emotion direction blocks afraid by 25% [17%, 33%] and sad by 29% [7%, 56%] (BH-FDR q 0.001, 0.028; `b1e.verdict_H3`). The top principal direction of A's span, with 5.8× the footprint, blocks angry by 61% (and calm, knife-edge on an untestable emotion) while leaving afraid and sad untouched even though it removes 79% and 71% of their readable affect (`b1e.pc1_dissociation`): readable-affect removal and blocking dissociate. B1d's rank-5 permuted frame blocks where B1e's rank-1 permuted direction does not; they differ in rank and footprint (6.5 vs 0.5 per position), and which matters is untested — the control's footprint (0.84× and 0.77× for afraid and sad, below B1c's inert random direction) leaves the footprint caveat standing. The secondary readout on B's reply contradicts afraid's block (null, wrong sign) and agrees for sad. On the 8B the same design returns H3 with sad blocked by 36% (q 0.002, secondary readout agreeing) and afraid by 14% (q 0.08, no block against baseline); the control is matched only on average (sad 0.86×), both directions remove about the isotropic norm, and B1c's random direction on that model reproduced the contrasts, so the run adds no discriminating power there. Only sad replicates across models on both outcomes; afraid is unreplicated (`b1e8.verdict_H3_matched`). On the 8B, B1c's design blocks the same two emotions to significance (15%, 31%; `b1c8.verdict_and_block`), with the other three too wide to tell agreement from disagreement; its manipulation-check rule returned instrument_failed because the upper ablation window changes the readout by nothing measurable there and the strict criterion has no tolerance band.

## 5. What the controls teach
Norm-matched random and orthogonal directions — the field's standard controls — were inert in every run; a *fitted* label-free control was not. Any ablation claim about an emotion (or concept) direction therefore needs a control fitted by the same estimator on permuted labels, not a random vector of matched norm. And a manipulation check must not share its target with the ablation.

## 6. Limitations and what is not claimed
One probe pool and one direction estimate for all B1 runs; the A-span readout is forced-choice and shares a target with the subspace ablation; calm is untestable at this dose grid; B1d's per-emotion contrasts do not clear BH-FDR; the median CIs are scenario-only. Provenance limits: B1c's pre-registration stamp is empty in its result files and its ordering rests on commit times, file mtimes and the run's start time; B1c's pool is 1,615 against the registered 2,160 and its FDR ran over five emotions, not six; the 8B runs of the B1c and B1e designs are out-of-prereg replications; B1e's control cosine (0.004) was measured by the pre-launch verifier and is not stamped in the file; the weak-penalty arms are tolerance-limited and bound nothing about the C → ∞ limit. Not tested: the token channel (three rewrite designs failed their own manipulation check: the neutral rewrite kept 67–98% of the readable affect and the affect-preserving rewrite changed the transfer itself; `b1c.text_arms_uninterpretable`), the behavioural reach, layers below 13, n > d.

## 7. Practice
Pre-registration before each run (B1d and B1e stamped in their files and written before their drivers; B1c's written after its driver, before its run, and unstamped), an independent pre-launch verifier with a forward-pass smoke test, and a blind critique after every result whose corrections are in `docs/review/CRITIQUE_LEDGER.md`. Seven of the authors' interpretive sentences were withdrawn under review and are absent here.
