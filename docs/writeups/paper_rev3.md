# Affective transfer between LLM agents: the estimator, two pre-registered ablations, and a control that bites

*Draft for the 2026-09-06 deadline. Every number carries its registry id in `docs/review/claims.json`; figures are produced by `src/rev3/paper_figures.py` from the result files. All results are in; blind critiques of reports 28 and 29 are pending and may adjust wording.*

## Abstract (draft)
When two LLM agents converse and one is steered into an emotion, the other's internal representation of its own state moves with the dose: five of six emotions, replicated three times (`b1f.*`, `b1c.*`, `b1d.*`). We ask what carries it. First we show that the direction most work uses to read and write such states — a logistic-regression coefficient row — does not reproduce across independent fits in the regime everyone works in, and why: every fit on these activations is a perfect separator, so the direction is the penalty's tie-break among separating hyperplanes, drifting away from the mean-difference direction as the sample grows while the mean-difference direction converges (`a2.*`, `a2f*.*`, `a2m.*`). Using the reproducible direction, two pre-registered ablations then test the linear account of the channel. Removing the emotion direction from the sender's span at every readable layer leaves a median 91% of the transfer (`b1c.*`); removing a rank-5 affect subspace fails its own instrument check, and a fitted label-free subspace of the same footprint blocks as much as the emotion direction (`b1d.*`). A pre-registered fitted, label-free rank-1 control then returns H3: the control is inert and the emotion direction blocks afraid by 25% and sad by 29% against it; a top-principal-direction control with six times the footprint blocks other emotions but not these, while removing most of their readable affect, so readable-affect removal is not what blocks transfer (`b1e.*`). On the 27B the control's footprint is below the emotion direction's; on the 8B it is matched (0.96×) and still inert, and sad is blocked by a third against it (`b1e8.*`). The lesson for controls is general: a norm-matched random direction is inert where a fitted one is not.

## 1. Setup
Two agents, A and B, in 29 fixed scenarios; A's message is generated under a steering vector at layer 42 of Qwen3.6-27B (revision 6a9e13bd; four doses 0, 0.33, 0.67, 1.0; three reps; B sampled with common random numbers across arms). Readouts: B's present-emotion projection on a probe fitted on a disjoint half of a 1,615-item probe pool (sha 70129f86…), and a generation-side forced-choice readout on A's span. Every contrast is scenario-blocked and dose-paired (5,000 draws). Second model: Llama-3-8B-Instruct-abliterated (dd67dd05) for the estimator results and a replication of the B1c design.

## 2. The estimator: reproducibility and its mechanism (Fig. 1, Fig. 2)
**Disjoint-half reproducibility.** Ten disjoint splits at n per half from 75 to 2000. At the 27B focus layer: difference-of-means 0.974, logistic (C = 0.5) 0.566 (`a2.focus_dom_split_half`, `a2.focus_logreg_split_half`); layer 54: 0.979 vs 0.554 (`a2f54.*`); 8B focus layer at n = 1200: 0.941 vs 0.286 (`a2_8b.*`). Tuned C reaches 0.61 (27B) and 0.38 (8B).
**Holding the per-sample penalty fixed does not help.** With C_n = 0.5·N_REF/n the logistic curve shows no net rise from n = 150 to 2000 at two anchors on the 27B (0.581 → 0.581 at anchor 600; a significant dip at 600 and partial recovery; `a2f.focus_logreg_fixed_lambda_flat`, `a2f2.lam_anchor2000_no_rise`), one drop then flat at layer 54 (`a2f54.lam_declines_anchor600`), a rise at layer 16, and on the 8B a peak at 150 followed by a monotone fall to 0.276 (`a2f8.lam_declines`). Depth-specific, never converging.
**Why.** Every multinomial logistic fit on every half at every n used, under three penalties spanning 1000×, separates its training half perfectly (accuracy exactly 1.000, log-loss ≤ 0.006, ≤ 80 iterations; `a2m.separable_every_n`, `a2m.8b_same_regime`). The likelihood is saturated from n = 75: the direction is the penalty's tie-break among separating hyperplanes. Weakening the penalty 100× leaves the direction unchanged on the same half (cosine ≥ 0.96) and no more reproducible (0.52 vs 0.58; `a2m.weak_penalty_same_direction`, `a2m.v2_ten_seeds`). The separator starts near the mean-difference direction and departs from it as n grows — cosine 0.97 → 0.63 (27B), 0.91 → 0.43 (8B) — while difference-of-means becomes reproducible (`a2m.logistic_departs_from_dom`). Wording adopted from the blind review: *on separable activations the multinomial direction is the penalty's tie-break among separating hyperplanes, not a likelihood estimate; its split-half reproducibility plateaus at 0.55–0.60 (27B) and falls to 0.28 (8B) across a 27-fold n increase, while difference-of-means, an average rather than an extremum, rises to 0.94–0.98 on the same splits.*
**Against the near-identity claim.** In the counter-paper's own units (CAA vs logistic, raw space) the cosine is 0.43 (27B) and 0.33–0.37 (8B), not 0.98 (`a2f.caa_raw_vs_logreg_raw`, `a2f8.cross_estimator`). Not claimed: that the fit sits at the max-margin limit (the weak arms are tolerance-limited), or a support-vector mechanism for the drift.

## 3. Transfer (Fig. 3)
B's present-emotion projection rises with A's dose for desperate, afraid, happy, sad and angry (none-arm slopes +84 to +212 with CIs excluding zero; calm +44 [−3, +99] never does), identically in three runs that share the pool and directions (`b1c.partial_block_replicates`, `b1d.replicates_b1c_arms`). MC-steer passes 6/6 in each. On the 8B the same five emotions transfer (`b1c8.transfer_replicates`), with a steering manipulation half as strong and less specific, so only the pattern is compared. Happy's response is weak and non-monotone (Fig. 3).

## 4. Two pre-registered ablations (Fig. 4)
**B1c (PREREG_B1c, verdict H1).** Projecting the difference-of-means emotion direction out of A's span at every hidden state 13–63 during B's prefill removes a median 9% of the dose-response, CI [4%, 25%] over five testable emotions (`b1c.verdict_H1`, `b1c.median_fraction`); afraid 21% and sad 33% are the only significant blocks (`b1c.partial_block_replicates`); layers 43–63 add nothing detectable for four emotions (`b1c.no_gain_beyond_layer42`); a unit-norm random direction, removing twice the norm, changes nothing (`b1c.random_control_inert`). The blocked fraction tracks how much readable affect the ablation removes (r ≈ 0.85; blocked-per-removed ≈ 0.27; `b1c.readout_residual`): the rank-1 direction is insufficient to carry the transfer.
**B1d (PREREG_B1d, verdict instrument_failed).** Projecting out the rank-5 class-mean affect subspace (which captures 16–21% of the pool's variance, cosine 0.71–0.81 to the top-5 principal components; `b1d.frame_geometry`) removes ≥ 80% of the readable affect for only 3 of 6 emotions and shifts the readout at dose 0 (desperate 0.11 → 0.50; `b1d.readout_baseline_shift`): the instrument and the ablation share a target. A permuted-label subspace built by the same estimator — no emotion information, a third overlap with the affect frame, 65× a random frame's variance — reduces the transfer for afraid (−49.5), sad (−26.7) and angry (−44.3), as much as the emotion direction did (`b1d.perm_control_blocks`; unadjusted CIs, BH-FDR q = 0.051), while a random 5-frame is inert. The emotion-specific increment over that control is 0.14 [−0.03, 0.29] (`b1d.median_fraction_descriptive`), and rank 5 blocks *less* than rank 1 for afraid (`b1d.rank5_vs_rank1`).
**B1e (PREREG_B1e, verdict H3).** The same estimator on permuted labels gives a rank-1 direction with cosine 0.004 to the emotion direction and 0.85× its footprint; removed at all 51 layers it changes no emotion's dose-response and removes no readable affect (`b1e.permdir_inert`). Against it, the emotion direction blocks afraid by 25% [17%, 33%] and sad by 29% [7%, 56%] (BH-FDR q 0.001, 0.028; `b1e.verdict_H3`). The top principal direction of A's span, with 5.8× the footprint, blocks angry by 61% and calm while leaving afraid and sad untouched even though it removes 79% and 71% of their readable affect (`b1e.pc1_dissociation`): readable-affect removal and blocking dissociate. B1d's rank-5 permuted frame blocks where B1e's rank-1 permuted direction does not; they differ in rank and footprint (6.5 vs 0.5 per position), and which matters is untested — the control's footprint (0.84× and 0.77× for afraid and sad, below B1c's inert random direction) leaves the footprint caveat standing. The secondary readout on B's reply contradicts afraid's block (null, wrong sign) and agrees for sad. On the 8B, where the control's footprint is matched (0.96×), the same design returns H3 with sad blocked by 36% (q 0.002, secondary agrees) and afraid by 14% (q 0.08, marginal) (`b1e8.verdict_H3_matched`). On the 8B, B1c's design blocks the same two emotions to significance (15%, 31%; `b1c8.verdict_and_block`), with the other three too wide to tell agreement from disagreement; its manipulation-check rule returned instrument_failed because the upper ablation window changes the readout by nothing measurable there and the strict criterion has no tolerance band.

## 5. What the controls teach
Norm-matched random and orthogonal directions — the field's standard controls — were inert in every run; a *fitted* label-free control was not. Any ablation claim about an emotion (or concept) direction therefore needs a control fitted by the same estimator on permuted labels, not a random vector of matched norm. And a manipulation check must not share its target with the ablation.

## 6. Limitations and what is not claimed
One probe pool and one direction estimate for all B1 runs; the A-span readout is forced-choice and shares a target with the subspace ablation; calm is untestable at this dose grid; B1d's per-emotion contrasts do not clear BH-FDR; the median CIs are scenario-only. Not tested: the token channel (three rewrite designs failed their own manipulation check: the neutral rewrite kept 67–98% of the readable affect and the affect-preserving rewrite changed the transfer itself; `b1c.text_arms_uninterpretable`), the behavioural reach, layers below 13, n > d.
